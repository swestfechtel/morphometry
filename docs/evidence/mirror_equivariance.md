# Mirror equivariance of the torsion, CCD, head-centre, CEA and offset landmarks

*Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-11-12 when that file was cut below its size limit; `CLAUDE.md` keeps a summary and links here. Nothing below was rewritten.*

   **Femoral torsion is mirror-equivariant by construction.** The proximal-femur and knee landmark searches are written for a left image half; a right half is measured as the reflection of a left one: `get_proximal_reference_line{,_ct}(side='right')` and `measurements/femur.py::_mri_torsion` / `_ct_torsion` reflect the right half (`reflect_image_left_right`, knee arrays `[::-1]`), run the left-side code, compose the angle there with the left conventions, and map landmarks back with `x -> n-1-x` (`geometry.mirror_index_x`, with `geometry.order_condylar_points` restoring the reported endpoint order). A mirrored left half through `side='right'` therefore gives exactly the left result (pinned by `tests/characterization/test_femoral_torsion_equivariance.py`, MRI Lee/Murphy + CT, stage by stage; pure composition in `tests/unit/test_torsion_angle_equivariance.py`). This replaced separate right-side branches that were systematically different: a flip off by one voxel (`c + (n//2-c)*2` instead of `n-1-c`), a head trim that scaled an index and so was not the mirror of the left trim, `astype(int16)` truncation of the head centre (floor is not mirror-consistent) and an asymmetric knee condylar search. `calculate_anteversion` shares the reference line, so its right side is equivariant too.
   **The CCD and the tibial torsion are too, the same way.** `measurements/hip.py::calculate_ccd` measures a right image half as the *mirror* of a left one (`mirror_image_left_right` of the hip and knee images, left-side code) and `calculate_ccd_ct` as the *reflection* (`reflect_image_left_right`); the two are not interchangeable here. The MRI CCD composes its angle from physical coordinates spanning two images whose affines have different origins, so it needs an operation that keeps every voxel's physical position — a reflection mirrors each image about its own array centre, and those are the same world plane only if the two images share an index-to-world x map (origin *and* direction), which separately acquired hip and knee series do not: equal array widths are not sufficient, and on Augsburg, where both halves are 192 wide, reflecting each about its own centre still costs a median 1.22° (5.7° on PA000031, whose hip and knee origins are 13.5 mm apart) — whereas the CT landmark code reads physical directions (which physical x is lateral) and so needs the mirror-twin leg. `measurements/tibia.py::_tibial_torsion_points` reflects the knee and ankle arrays (`[::-1]`), composes with the left conventions and maps the landmarks back, so `get_tibial_torsion_landmarks` now does depend on `side`. Pinned by `tests/characterization/test_ccd_tibial_equivariance.py` (both probes, MRI + CT); its MRI CCD probe has to reflect the hip and knee images about one *common* world plane rather than each about its own centre, which is a property of the probe, not of the measurement. Measured over 50 Augsburg cases, 60 NaKo hips and the 46 NMDID verification legs, the two sides previously disagreed by a median 0.16–0.23° (MRI CCD, max 0.70), 0.21–0.32° (NaKo isotropic hip-only CCD, max 1.00), 0.85–1.02° (CT CCD, max 3.14), 0.8–1.1° (Augsburg tibial torsion) and 1.7° (CT tibial torsion), and now agree exactly; every left-side value is bit-identical. Quote the tibial figures partitioned by notch status, never as a bare max — the tail belonged to the old no-notch fallback (`rotate_tibia`, since removed; see the fallback paragraph below), not to the side dependence. Augsburg (94 half-measurements): notch found in both frames, n=87, median 0.82°, max 3.85°; notch missing in at least one, n=7 (7.4%), median 2.63°, max 25.05° (PA000032 right). NMDID CT (92 half-measurements, 86 resolved): notch found in both, only 34% of legs, n=30, median 0.61°, max 3.28°; notch missing, n=47, median 3.07°, max 126.73° — and that 126.73° is the single case where the two folded answers landed on opposite sides of the (−90, 90] seam, not a measurement moving by 127°. Making such a leg equivariant makes the two sides *agree*; it does not make either right. The CCD disagreement came from the femoral *neck* centre (the `pv.SolidSphere` shell sampling is not symmetric about the head centre), not from the head centre or the shaft axis — the shaft centroids' former `int()` truncation shifted both endpoints by the same voxel (when both centroids were fractional) and so cancelled in the shaft vector. The endpoints are now unrounded (see the shaft-axis paragraph below), which moved the CCD itself by a median 0.08° on the 50 Augsburg reader-study hips (max 0.28°, both sides) and 0.22° on the NaKo projected CCD (max 0.57°).
   **The femoral head centre is equivariant too, which closes the family** (task #11, 2026-11-02). `get_femoral_head_center` (both paths) and `get_femoral_head_center_ct` were the last landmark functions with genuine `side == 'right'` logic inside them; both now measure a right image half as the reflection of a left one and map the centre back — `mask[::-1]` plus `geometry.mirror_index_x` on the MRI path, `image_io.reflect_image_left_right` plus `hip.py::_mirror_physical_point_x` on the CT one, which needs the mirror-*twin* leg rather than an index relabelling because its lateral cutoff is applied to **physical** x. The bodies below the guards are left-side-only and `_fit_femoral_head_consensus` no longer takes a `side` at all. `_mirror_physical_point_x` rebuilds the index→physical matrix from four `transform_index_to_physical_point` calls rather than using `transform_physical_point_to_index`, which on the SimpleITK backend is `TransformPhysicalPointToIndex` and returns an **integer** index — it would snap the sphere-fit centre to a whole voxel (0.76 mm in plane, 3.0 mm in z on this CT), against the floats-stay-floats rule below; it reads the width off `Image.shape[0]`, not `Image.array.shape[0]`, because the array is `(z, y, x)` on SimpleITK while `shape` is `GetSize()` and is `(x, y, z)` on both backends. **One mechanism, two amplitudes.** Both paths were broken by the same thing — a centroid truncated to an index, which does not commute with `x -> n-1-x`, so the boundary column was off by one on essentially every half. On the MRI consensus path that only moves the *seed*, and the refit re-converges: 91 of 100 Augsburg reader-study halves agreed with their own mirror to 1e-9 mm. On CT `_largest_component_centroid`'s truncation sets the lateral cutoff **directly**, with nothing downstream to re-converge, so it is systematic: only 2 of 46 right halves agreed. (Two further steps there also fail to commute and are not separately diagnosed, because the reflection makes them moot: `np.argmax` over island sizes takes the first maximum in raster order, and the nearest-voxel fallback for a concave island does the same over `np.argwhere` order.) **Do not pool right-side rows across this change** — and note the magnitudes are *right-half* numbers, because a left half is bit-identical before and after: MRI non-isotropic, **4 of 50** right halves move, max **1.52 mm** of centre (PA000397, almost all of it along the 10 mm slice axis) and 0.68 in-plane voxels of radius, with the slab's lower bound moving one slice on 8 of 50; CT, **44 of 46** right halves move, median **0.784 mm** and max **3.264 mm** of centre, radius median 0.317 / max 1.165 mm, and downstream on the 46 verification legs the right-side centre-edge angle moves by up to **5.127°**, acetabular version 2.786°, femoral offset 1.767 mm, mechanical axis deviation 0.777 mm and HKA 0.229°. **The two both-sides measurements move on their LEFT element as well**, which is not a defect: `calculate_center_edge_angle` and `calculate_acetabular_anteversion` take the whole both-legs image and build the acetabular reference from *both* head centres (`measurements/hip.py:722`, `G_vec = left_fhc - right_fhc_adj`, and `505-506`, the single `correct_slice` both rims are extracted from), so the right centre reaches the left value — over the 46 verification legs the left element moves by a median 0.032° / max 0.547° of CEA and a median 0.239° / max 1.072° of version, with the left head centre itself bit-identical. Every other left-side consumer is exact. **Sensitivity to this landmark varies ~20-fold between measurements, in a way worth knowing before judging any of them**: the mechanical axis deviation and the HKA are the *least* affected right-leg measurements (median 0.155 mm / 0.048°, max 0.777 mm / 0.229°) despite the same sub-millimetre head-centre shift, because that axis runs hip-to-ankle and translating one endpoint barely rotates a long line, whereas the acetabular pair and the offset / subchondral family use the head centre as a *local origin* and move by up to 5.127°. **Equivariance is consistency, not accuracy**: making the right side follow the left makes the two agree, it does not prove either is right. The reason to follow the left is that the left-side code is the path that was fixed and validated in 2026-09 (`_femoral_head_slab_bound` plus the largest-component interior-point column start) while the right-side branch was separate code never validated against anything. **No golden moved**: every CT golden is `_left`, the left head centre is bit-identical, and `ccd_ct_right` / `ccd_right` are insulated by `calculate_ccd{,_ct}`'s internal `search_side='left'`. The NaKo isotropic path is unchanged to within 2.9e-11 voxels of centre and 1.4e-11° downstream — **not literally bit-identical on the right side**, because the reflection sends a mirrored point cloud through `sphere_fit`'s `np.linalg.lstsq` and its SVD is not bit-exact under the mirror; that 1e-11 is the floor this path has always sat at (the pre-change code's own mirror gap was up to 4.4e-11, nonzero on 120 of 120 halves) and the change takes the left side to exactly 0. A residual that is left alone and needs a true tie to bite: the top-slice island selection resolves equal-sized islands with `np.argmax`, whose raster order reverses under reflection. `calculate_knee_rotation_angle` was once listed as an exception here too, for a `side` branch that flipped the two posterior-condyle orientations; it is **not** one — that branch never did anything (see the knee-rotation composition paragraph below). **Do not pool old and new right-side rows**: every NMDID CSV produced before this change carries the old right-side CT CCD (it rose by a median +0.80°, range -0.88 to +3.14 over the 46 verification legs) and the old right-side tibial torsion, the same way every CSV before 82f2221 carries the biased CT femoral offset. Don't reintroduce `side == 'right'` branches into the torsion, CCD or tibial-torsion landmark code — add left-side logic and let the reflection handle the right.
   **The NaKo centre-edge angle and the isotropic head centre are side-symmetric too.** `calculate_center_edge_angle` used a lateral-edge search band of 1.5 head radii on the image-left half and 1.1 on the image-right one (an ad-hoc edit, bd385f5); it is now one `C.CEA_UPPER_MARGIN_FACTOR` for both sides — first 1.5, then 1.1 (see the end of this paragraph). Separately, the isotropic path of `get_femoral_head_center` built its lateral cutoff from `int()` of the top-slice centroid, and floor does not commute with `x -> n-1-x`, so the image-left centre sat ~0.4 voxel more lateral than the mirror of the image-right one and read the CEA ~1.1° lower on the same hip. The cutoff now uses the unrounded centroid, and the column walk the nearest column with half-voxel ties broken towards medial (`floor(x+0.5)` left, `ceil(x-0.5)` right; plain `np.rint` rounds ties to even, which does not mirror). The anterior cutoff and the consensus/Augsburg path keep `int()` and are bit-identical. Over the 60 NaKo reference hips the reflected CEA now reproduces the swapped original on all 60 (to 3e-11°). Before, it matched on none: median 1.39°, max 12.8° with the old per-side factors (median 1.10°, max 2.40° from the head centre alone, factors equalised). The patient-left minus patient-right CEA went from +2.41° (p=3e-5) to -0.64° (p=0.22); about 1.9° of the old difference came from the factors and 1.1° from the head centre (head-centre fix alone: +1.29°, p=0.016). Pinned by `tests/unit/test_femoral_head_isotropic_equivariance.py` and `tests/characterization/test_cea_equivariance.py`. **The factor was then lowered to 1.1, and that value was chosen against the 60 NaKo reader cases — calibration on the evaluation set, so those cases no longer give an unbiased estimate of CE agreement.** The CEA under-reads both readers there, and agreement improves monotonically as the factor drops (1.5 -> 1.1: ICC2 vs MH right/left 0.37/0.39 -> 0.48/0.53, vs TH 0.50/0.47 -> 0.62/0.62; signed bias MH -6.0/-6.5 -> -4.8/-4.6°, TH -2.5/-4.6 -> -1.3/-2.7°). Both sides still share one value, so the measurement stays mirror-symmetric (60/60 exact at 1.1); patient L−R is +0.12° (p=0.8). Over the 60 hips the change moved 46 of 120 CE values (mean over all 120 +1.55°, over the 46 that moved +4.04°, max 11.8°); on the 46 NMDID CT verification cases the CEA is identical at 1.1 and 1.5. **Do not pool NaKo CE rows produced at 1.5 with rows at 1.1.** **Do not pool CEA rows produced before and after this change.** NaKo: CEA (against the original 1.5/1.1 code, at the final factor 1.1: median per-hip change +0.95° patient-right, -0.14° patient-left, max 9.76°; the intermediate 1.5 gave +0.67° / -0.67°, max 11.9°), alpha angle (median |change| 0.36°, max 4.9°), projected femoral offset (0.19 mm, max 1.1 mm), isotropic CCD (0.25°, max 1.1°) and femoral anteversion (0.05°, max 1.3°) all moved; `process_nako_large.py`'s acetabular anteversion (`isotropic=False`) did not. The single factor also applies to the CT and non-isotropic MRI CEA: against the original code their image-right band is unchanged at 1.1 radii and their image-left band went from 1.5 to 1.1. On the 46 NMDID verification cases (`process_nmdid.py` settings) the CEA is identical at 1.1 and 1.5, but that is not a guarantee for the rest of the cohort, and no non-isotropic MRI CEA was measured. `calculate_alpha_angle` was already equivariant before this change, on every path: it reverses a right half (`mask[::-1]`) and runs the left-side code (120/120 NaKo halves exact before and after).
   **The MRI femoral shaft axis and the NaKo femoral offset are side-symmetric too.** `hip.py::get_femoral_shaft_axis` truncated both of its slice centroids with `int()`, so the shaft axis of a reflected hip sat one voxel from the mirror of the original's, and `calculate_femoral_offset_projected` read the same NaKo hip a median 0.99 mm (max 1.00) differently depending on its image side, with the head centre already mirror-exact. The endpoints index no array and are now returned unrounded (float voxel indices). All 118 measurable NaKo halves now agree with their reflection exactly (0/118 before), and the patient-left minus patient-right offset went from -1.13 mm (p=0.04) to -0.23 mm (p=0.68). Pinned by `tests/unit/test_femoral_shaft_axis_equivariance.py` and `tests/characterization/test_femoral_offset_equivariance.py`. **Do not pool femoral offset or CCD rows produced before and after this change**: the NaKo projected offset moved by a median -0.43 mm on the image-left half and +0.42 mm on the image-right half (max 0.95 mm), the NaKo projected CCD by a median 0.22° (max 0.57°), the Augsburg MRI CCD by a median 0.08° (max 0.28°). CT is unaffected (`get_femoral_shaft_axis_ct` already returned floats). On the non-isotropic path the femoral offset still calls `get_femoral_head_center(side='right')` directly and is therefore not equivariant; separately, `calculate_femoral_offset` with a `knee_image` combines a shaft start in the knee's index space with a head centre in the hip's (no production caller passes a knee).
