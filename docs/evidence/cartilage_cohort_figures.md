# Knee cartilage: cohort figures and reproducibility

The `dfg_knees` cohort numbers behind the Wirth & Eckstein subdivisions and the thickness
measurement: the femoral frame's flexion-invariance figures, the validation against the
femur-tibia sagittal angle, the per-subregion thickness means, and what the detour rule costs in
mapped area. Moved verbatim out of `CLAUDE.md`, which keeps the constructions, the parameters,
the do-not-pool warnings and the caveat that the femoral shaft band was chosen by flexion
reproducibility on these same 96 scans — so these figures are not an unbiased estimate of it.

## Femoral frame: flexion invariance and validation

**Flexion invariance** (24 subject+load series over 0/30/60/90°, which is what the frame is for): CV of cF tAB 1.9-3.0% median (the whole femoral tAB, i.e. the segmentation's own noise, is 1.7%), of each third's area 1.8-3.0%, SD of each third's *share* 0.05-0.23 pp; the extension zones are looser (6.5-7.8%) because how far the trochlea is labelled is not flexion-invariant. Loaded vs unloaded (45 pairs): cF tAB median |Δ| 1.0/1.3%, max |Δshare| 0.13 pp median.

**Validation**: the femur-tibia sagittal angle (femoral frame vs a tibial shaft axis fitted the same way) tracks the nominal flexion — median -32.9 / -13.8 / +19.2 / +43.7° at 0/30/60/90°, Spearman ρ 0.956 (n=93), strictly increasing in all 21 complete series, fit `0.86*nominal - 34.9°`. The offset is the *tibial* axis (proximal metaphysis, line-fit residual 3.1 mm vs the femur's 1.6), not the femoral frame. At nominal 0° the femoral axis's **sagittal** tilt against the scanner's superior axis — the only component that reaches the subregions, because a coronal tilt rotates the axis inside the prox-ml plane and leaves `cross(prox, ml)` unchanged — is +1.22° ± 3.43 (n=24); its full 3-D angle to world +z is 9.80° ± 4.12, the rest being a coronal tilt of -6.04° ± 8.15 (distal-femur valgus plus how the leg lay in the scanner).

## Per-subregion thickness on the 96 dfg_knees

**Real data** (96 `dfg_knees`; **95 measure** — K11_90_belastet's tibia raises 'the two tibial plates overlap after hole filling' and its femur returns a NaN `cclf`; K7_90_belastet and K8_90_belastet are QC-flagged and K11_90_belastet is excluded by that failure, so n = 93; ~20 s per knee, 7.1 s tibia + 12.9 s femur): ThC.cAB.aMe mean (SD) in mm — tibia `clt` 2.30 (0.48) / `crt` 2.26 (0.53), `ilt` 1.90 / `irt` 1.89, `elt` 1.55 / `ert` 1.55, `alt` 1.69 / `art` 1.79, `plt` 1.91 / `prt` 1.65; femur `cclf` 3.15 (0.42) / `ccrf` 3.05 (0.50), `iclf` 2.25 / `icrf` 2.35, `eclf` 2.17 / `ecrf` 1.99, trochlea 2.26 / 2.31, posterior condyle 2.46 / 2.38. Whole-bone tAB-area-weighted aMe 1.81-1.87 mm (tibia) and 2.33-2.40 mm (femur) at 0/30/60/90° of flexion. The denuded fraction is tiny on this cohort (mean ≤ 1.4% per subregion, max 17% on one plate), so `tab_aMe` is within 0.03 mm of `aMe` in every subregion mean. `aMin`/`aMiv` reproduce the paper's remark that only the central regions are meaningful: mean aMin 1.2-2.2 mm in `clt`/`crt`/`cclf`/`ccrf` and 0.00-0.15 mm in every other subregion — here because `cartilage_coverage` is a distance test and cAB reaches a fraction of a millimetre sideways past the cartilage edge, not because the cartilage reconstruction tapers. The central femoral thirds (3.05-3.15 mm) read ~0.5 mm above the published healthy range (~2.3-2.7 mm) while every other subregion sits inside ~1.3-2.5 mm. That is the label maps, not the measurement: against an independent quantity — the volume of the cartilage voxels assigned to a subregion over its tAB area — `aMe` agrees to 0.1-0.5 mm on every subregion of three spot-checked knees, with the volume ratio the higher of the two, as a convex surface implies.

## What the profile-detour rule costs in mapped area

**The rule costs mapped area**: over the 15 reference femurs (rule off → on, gap cap included, measured on the earlier 40 mm domain) the covered reference tAB without a coordinate goes from median 1.82% (0.12-6.22%) to 3.05% (0.13-7.03%), +50 mm² median (0.2-202 mm², K11 173.7 → 375.8 mm²), i.e. up to +3.5 percentage points of a femur's covered area is lost at the outer condyle edges. What it buys: on K1 the covered tAB beyond arc 50 mm went 64.8 → 3.6 mm² (its posterior condyles end at ~48 mm; on other knees the long-condyle area beyond 50 mm is real and barely changes, e.g. K11 401 → 333, K23 175 → 174 mm²). Points with no coordinate are dropped and their area share recorded: `dropped_fraction` (per configuration, femur median 0.022 / max 0.053; 0.036 / 0.113 on the 40 mm domain) and `reference_unmapped_fraction` (per knee, femur median 2.19% / range 0.13-4.88%, max K11; was 3.05% / 7.03% on K9 with the 40 mm domain; tibia 0) on the current run, split by cause in `registration.csv`'s `reference_unmapped_<cause>` columns (`UNMAPPED_CAUSES`): per knee median `detour` 0.94% (max 3.35%, K11), `no_crossing` 0.72% (max 1.62%, K2), `no_profile_point` 0.03% (max 1.73%, K1; used levels whose profile passes > 2 mm from the vertex), `off_bone` 0. The whole-bone domain moved 6 of 294 femoral hotspots (K2 0° loaded both/right, K11 0° unloaded both/left, K11 60° loaded left — onto the newly mapped proximal trochlea / proximal posterior condyle at arc −45 / +57 mm — and K25 0° loaded right by one cell), no tibial one and no subregion Δ. Δ cells are defined only where both scans have measured (covered) triangles — denuded triangles are excluded, never 0. The map grid spans the covered triangles only (uncovered reference tAB outside them gets no region code): on the whole-bone domain a few near-zero-area uncovered tAB slivers map far up the shaft (arc −115 mm on K23) and stretched every femoral panel in df36e88.
