## The cohort spacing surveys, and what the shear is worth

**Measured spacing survey** (header zooms permuted into the post-LPI axis order — the reorientation can move the slice axis *into* the plane): Augsburg MRI **5715/5715** `.nii.gz` under `preprocessed/` square in plane (values 1.09375, 1.145833, 1.25, 1.302083, 1.640625 mm; slice 4–10 mm — but note that list is the **union over regions, and the per-region sets differ**, which is what makes a spacing-dependent hazard region-specific: the 952 **knee** segmentations carry `{1.09375: 949, 1.145833: 1, 1.302083: 1, 1.640625: 1}` and the 951 **hip** ones `{1.09375: 949, 1.145833: 1, 1.25: 1}`, so 1.25 mm occurs on no knee mask and the coarsest knee spacing, 1.640625, occurs on no hip mask — which is exactly why the millimetre-ordering defect recorded under the Murphy gate is *demonstrated* on the condylar line and only *possible* on the Murphy axis) and 450/450 in `reader_study_augsburg/` at 1.09375, NaKo sample 1.0 mm isotropic 60/60 — every one of them with its two in-plane zooms **bit-identical**, so nothing legitimate sits near the tolerance. Whole-leg CT **is now surveyed, and the risk is zero** (`scripts/survey_ct_spacing.py`, 2026-11-02, headers only so it runs over the cohort in minutes):

**1520 of 1520** NMDID files (1474 cohort segmentations + the 46 verification ground truths) are in-plane square with their two post-LPI zooms **bit-identical**, and **1520 of 1520** reorient to LPI (all are raw LPS), so there is no CT analogue of the `dfg_knees` non-LPI cases either. No case would have its CT femoral torsion turned into a NaN row by the assert — that worry is closed. What the survey *does* overturn is the spacing itself: the **0.8 × 0.8 × 3.0 mm quoted here was the acquisition protocol and matches not one single file**. The real in-plane spacing is per-case (reconstructed FOV over a fixed matrix):

**259 distinct values from 0.623 to 1.367 mm**, with the slice axis 3.0 mm throughout, so the slice aspect runs 2.19–4.82. That per-case variation has a consequence for the Murphy gate — see the `murphy_axis_short` bullet, whose threshold is in in-plane *voxels*.

**This is hypothetical, not published: no sheared value has ever been produced in this repo.** `calculate_knee_rotation_angle`'s only non-test callers are `scripts/process_augsburg_{reader_study,large_dataset}.py` (Augsburg, square) and an exploratory notebook; the `dfg_knees` scripts are cartilage-only, and `dfg_knees` has no ankle label, so the tibial torsion cannot reach it either.

**No value moved**: the composition arithmetic is untouched (ASTs of `signed_axial_angle`, both torsion composers, `_knee_rotation_angle`, `_mirror_landmarks`, `_proximal_angle` and the fold helpers are byte-equivalent to the previous commit with docstrings stripped), the PA000001 femoral (Lee and Murphy) and tibial torsion are bit-identical with and without the spacings and to their pre-change values, and no golden moved (the NaKo `anteversion_left` golden included — NaKo is isotropic).

## In-plane isotropy (task #10) — full paragraph (from CLAUDE.md)

*Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-11-12 when that file was cut below its size limit; `CLAUDE.md` keeps a summary and links here. Nothing below was rewritten.*

   **The axial-plane angles are composed in index space, which assumes equal in-plane voxel spacing (task #10).** `geometry.signed_axial_angle` — and with it `_femoral_torsion_angle`, `_tibial_torsion_angle` and `_knee_rotation_angle` — works on voxel *indices*, as does `measurements/femur.py::_proximal_angle`, the neck angle against the image x-axis that `calculate_anteversion` reports. At spacing `(sx, sy)` a physical direction `(cos t, sin t)` has index direction `(cos t / sx, sin t / sy)`, i.e. index angle `atan2(k sin t, cos t)` with `k = sx / sy`, so on in-plane anisotropic data the angle *between* two lines comes out sheared by an amount that depends on how the limb happens to lie in the slice, not only on `k` — and a *single*-line angle like the anteversion's takes that shear directly, with none of the partial cancellation a two-line angle gets. Worst case over all limb orientations, a true 15 / 30 / 45° angle is off by at most 0.01 / 0.03 / 0.04° at `k = 1.001`, 0.15 / 0.29 / 0.40° at 1.01, 1.5 / 2.8 / 4.0° at 1.1, 7.3 / 13.8 / 18.7° at 1.5 and 20.1 / 35.5 / 44.7° at 2.4; the error grows with the true angle, so the sup at `k = 1.001` is 0.0573° (approached as the true angle tends to 90°, where the folded angle can also cross the (−90, 90] seam), not the 0.04° of the 45° column. At `k` = 2.4 a true 15° reads anywhere in [6.3°, 35.1°]. **`dfg_knees` violates the precondition**: its 0.75 mm sagittal slice axis becomes an *in-plane* axis under the LPI reorientation, giving 0.75 × 0.3125 mm (`k` = 2.4) on 95 of its 96 scans (axis 0 on 94, axis 1 on K10_90_belastet; the exception is the broken 1 mm isotropic K11_90_belastet). Measured there with the landmark search held fixed, removing the shear moves the knee rotation angle by a median 3.75°, p90 14.87°, max 149.86° (by flexion: 0° 2.99/7.72, 30° 3.89/13.63, 60° 3.59/15.71, 90° 8.47/149.86; 17 of 95 scans move by more than 10°, and the four values near 140–150° are the (−90, 90] fold seam, not a 150° move). **The route taken is the assert, not a physical re-derivation.** `geometry.validate_in_plane_isotropy(spacing, source)` raises `ValueError` when `|sx − sy| > C.IN_PLANE_SPACING_RTOL` (1e-3) `× max(sx, sy)`, and also when the two in-plane entries are missing, non-finite or non-positive — every rejection is a `ValueError` and nothing else, because the per-measurement handlers of `compute_torsion.py` and the batch scripts catch only `(RuntimeError, AssertionError, ValueError)`, so an escaping `ZeroDivisionError` / `IndexError` would abort a cohort run instead of writing a NaN row. The measurements call it wherever they can know the spacing: `calculate_anteversion` and the CT femoral torsion check their `Image`/`Segmentation` arguments outright, and the entry points that historically took bare arrays (`calculate_knee_rotation_angle`, `calculate_tibial_torsion`, `get_tibial_torsion_landmarks`, and the MRI femoral torsion's `knee_mask`) now accept an `Image` **or** an array — `image_io.mask_and_spacing` unwraps it, so a geometry-holding caller is checked with no extra argument, while array-only callers can still pass `spacing=` / `knee_spacing=` / `ankle_spacing=` explicitly (an explicit value wins over the image's). `docker/compute_torsion.py` passes the spacings (its knee/ankle halves are array slices); the `scripts/` runners already hold `Segmentation`s on the very lines that pass `.array` (`process_nmdid.py` tibial torsion, `process_augsburg_*` femoral torsion and knee rotation) and would be covered by dropping the `.array`. There is deliberately **no warn-when-absent**: it would fire on every existing call over known-conforming cohorts and get tuned out. **The one path where this change can alter a cohort CSV** is the CT femoral torsion, which now checks both `Segmentation`s unconditionally: if any NMDID case has rectangular in-plane voxels, `process_nmdid.py` would write a NaN `femoral_torsion` where it previously wrote a sheared number — unverifiable until the mount returns. Likewise `api/ingest/dicom.py` preserves the DICOM `PixelSpacing` without resampling, so a vendor sending non-square pixels would turn the hip check into six error entries in `compute_torsion.py`'s output (handled per measurement, not a crash, but a visible behaviour change). **Why not physical coordinates.** The conventions themselves survive the move cleanly (an LPI affine is `diag(-sx, -sy, -sz)` in RAS and negating both in-plane components leaves `signed_axial_angle` unchanged, so the same formula and the same `side` x-negation hold in world coordinates), and on an axis-aligned image the world composition is exactly the index one (bit-identical on all six PA000001 probes). The reason not to is the one cohort that actually violates the precondition: on `dfg_knees` the off-diagonal of the unit index→world matrix is 0.42 median, 0.76 max (knee flexion), so the world axial plane and the slice plane are genuinely **different planes** — a world composition there is not "the same measurement, unsheared"; axial rotation of a flexed knee has no canonical plane, and (a) would swap one arbitrary frame for another on exactly the data the check is for. Refusing the data is the honest answer. Composing in world RAS would also move the production cohorts' values (5 of the 50 reader-study patients have oblique affines, largest off-diagonal 0.0384; the femoral torsion moves by a median 0.0003° and up to 0.021124°, PA000405) — that breaks bit-identity, though it is not a material move, and it belongs to task #9 (position/rotation dependence) with its own evidence and do-not-pool burden. A *scale-only* physical composition would remove the shear without the obliquity, but it still composes in the index rotation frame and still assumes the hip-and-knee (or knee-and-ankle) series share that frame, which separately acquired series need not: it would trade a checkable precondition for an uncheckable one. Pinned by `tests/unit/test_in_plane_spacing.py`. **Separate, unfixed, same class:** `measurements/knee.py::calculate_joint_line_convergence_angle` composes its angle in the **coronal (x, z)** index plane, where the spacing ratio is 3.66 on the Augsburg knee MRI (1.09375 vs 4.0 mm) — a larger shear than any in-plane case, on the only cohort it runs on, and no golden pins it (PA000001 cannot resolve its condyles, so `test_knee_ankle_length.py::test_joint_line_convergence_angle_left` xfails). `measurements/ankle.py::calculate_pma_angle` (the plafond-malleolus angle) is the same wider family (an unsigned index-space angle between two reference lines) at lower priority — already flagged provisional. Fixing either would move values — but **both are CLOSED as unreachable from production, checked rather than assumed (2026-11-06)**, so both are latent and neither should be prioritised on the strength of the shear alone. `calculate_joint_line_convergence_angle` has **no script caller at all**: only `notebooks/CT_conversion.ipynb` and its own xfailing golden, so "the only cohort it runs on" overstates it. `calculate_pma_angle` is **imported by `process_augsburg_reader_study.py` and `process_augsburg_large_dataset.py` and never called** — a dead import, no column, no published value. And PMA is worse than sheared: **one of its two reference lines does not exist.** `ankle.py::get_plafond_reference_line` returns a fixed `[0,0,0] -> [0,0,1]` whatever its input, with its own docstring saying "For now just a dummy implementation ... a proper implementation requires isotropic images" and a `TODO` for 3-D plane fitting. So the "plafond-malleolus angle" is the angle between the malleoli line and **an axis of the image**, which is why its golden sits at **89.678°** — pinned near 90 by construction rather than measuring ankle obliquity. Implementing the plafond line is a new measurement needing a definition from the literature, not a repair. **The surveys — Augsburg, NaKo and the 1520-file NMDID CT headers, plus the `dfg_knees` violation and the proof that no sheared value was ever published in this repo — are in [`docs/evidence/in_plane_spacing.md`](docs/evidence/in_plane_spacing.md).**
