# Signed axial-angle composition (femoral/tibial torsion, knee rotation)

*Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-11-12 when that file was cut below its size limit; `CLAUDE.md` keeps a summary and links here. Nothing below was rewritten.*

   **Femoral torsion composition is rotation-invariant and signed.** `measurements/femur.py::_femoral_torsion_angle` returns the signed axial-plane angle from the (undirected) posterior condylar line `c` to the neck axis `n = hip_start - hip_end` (neck point -> head centre), folded into (-90, 90]. In LPI a left image half is a patient-right femur (medial = +x, anterior = -y), so `AT = atan2(-(c_x n_y - c_y n_x), c . n)`; `side='right'` negates the x components. **Positive = antetorsion relative to the landmarks, negative = retrotorsion.** It is computed in index space, which assumes equal in-plane x/y voxel spacing — a precondition now checked wherever the spacing is known (see the task #10 paragraph below). The former fold-to-acute composition equalled `sign(neck image angle) * AT`, i.e. returned `-AT` whenever the head lay level with or posterior to the neck point in the image. The Augsburg readers never recorded a negative AT, so they cannot adjudicate retrotorsion. Evidence (reader-study and NMDID before/after, synthetic derivation) is in the commit message; pinned by `tests/unit/test_femoral_torsion_composition.py` and the real flip cases in `tests/characterization/test_torsion_head_posterior.py`. **Tibial torsion** (`measurements/tibia.py::_tibial_torsion_angle`) uses the same helper, `geometry.signed_axial_angle` (medial -> anterior is the same rotation sense as lateral -> posterior): the signed angle from the (undirected) posterior tibial condylar line to the tibia -> fibula centroid line, **positive = external torsion, negative = internal, relative to the landmarks**; the former fold-and-sign composition negated it whenever the fibula centroid was level with or anterior to the tibia centroid in the image (pinned by `tests/unit/test_tibial_torsion_composition.py`). Tibial torsion is mirror-equivariant as of `_tibial_torsion_points` (see above); its posterior condylar line used to be unreliable whenever `find_notch` failed, which the next paragraph fixes.
   **The knee rotation angle composition is rotation-invariant and signed too, and its direction is the published one.** `measurements/knee.py::_knee_rotation_angle` returns `geometry.signed_axial_angle` from the (undirected) posterior **femoral** condylar line to the (undirected) posterior **tibial** condylar line, folded into (-90, 90]; `side='right'` negates the x components, so a knee and its mirror twin read the same. Both lines are undirected, so neither endpoint order enters, and only their two directions do, so the value does not depend on how the limb is rotated in the scanner. **The direction is settled (backlog task #16, 2026-10-29) — by the literature, not by our data.** What this function computes is the measurement the literature calls the **tibiofemoral rotation (TFR) angle**: the angle between the posterior femoral condylar line and the posterior tibial line on an axial image. Of it, Anzillotti et al. (J Exp Orthop 2026;13(2):e70708, following the standard technique of Diederichs et al., Am J Sports Med 2013;41:51–57) write "this measurement is positive if the tibia is externally rotated compared to the femur" — which is exactly the shipped `+1` in `measurements/knee.py::KNEE_ROTATION_POSITIVE_SIGN`. It is *also* the same rotation sense as positive tibial torsion and positive femoral antetorsion, so all three agree, but that internal consistency with `measurements/tibia.py::_tibial_torsion_angle` was the old and much weaker reason for it; the local data never could adjudicate the direction (see the evidence paragraph below, which is kept because it records what the readers do show). **Only the sign carries over from that literature, never the magnitude.** The published technique picks both of its slices differently — femoral line through the posterior condylar *cartilage* at the widest A-P diameter, tibial line at the first slice below the menisci at the PCL insertion, against our bone lines on the largest-convex-area section — and the same paper measures a 6.18° mean gap between CT and MRI on one set of knees (standard MRI 2.11°, CT 8.28°, plateau-anchored MRI 9.80°; n=58 knees of 46 patellofemoral patients, no SDs or ranges reported, and they note controls run slightly *internally* rotated on MRI). So our Augsburg cohort mean of +3.80° landing near their 2.11° is reassurance at most and not validation: different cohort, different protocol, and no published spread to compare against. That constant remains the only place the direction is decided; flipping it would be one line plus a re-capture of the `knee_rotation_angle_left` golden, and the magnitude, the rotation invariance and the mirror equivariance are all independent of it (pinned by `tests/unit/test_knee_rotation_composition.py`, which asserts the invariances under either value and pins the direction itself in one deliberately convention-dependent test, `test_sign_is_external_tibial_rotation`). The former composition folded each line to an acute angle against the image x-axis and added or subtracted the two by the signs of their AP tilts, which equalled `±sign(femoral line y-extent) × ` the signed angle above (`-` on an image-left half, `+` on a right one) — i.e. it negated the answer whenever the medial end of the *femoral* condylar line lay anterior to its lateral end *in the image*, an image-frame coordinate sign with no anatomy in it — and, past 90°, returned the supplement instead of the folded angle (reachable, but `|value| > 90` occurs on 0/1902 Augsburg halves, so it never bit in production). Measured, with the 100 real Augsburg condylar line pairs rotated exactly in the axial plane (0-175°, 5° steps, landmark search held fixed): the old composition varied with the rotation on 98/100 halves, median peak-to-peak 180.0°, sign flipped on 98/100; the new one varies on 0/100 (max peak-to-peak 2.2e-14°). The two invariant halves have exactly parallel condylar lines (a true 0°, which no rotation can turn into anything else). **`side` never did anything** in this measurement: the old right-side branch negated *both* AP tilts and only their relative sign was used, so it cancelled — `|kra(side='left') - kra(side='right')|` is 0.000e+00 on all 100 real halves and on 345 synthetic landmark configurations, i.e. every knee-rotation value ever published was composed with the left-side rule. **The landmark search was not mirror-equivariant either; that is now fixed (task #17, 2026-11-03).** `calculate_knee_rotation_angle` was the last measurement reading a posterior condylar line straight out of `knee.py::get_knee_reference_line` without reflecting a right half first — `measurements/femur.py::_mri_torsion` and `measurements/tibia.py::_tibial_torsion_points` both already did — and it now measures a right half as the reflection of a left one (reverse the mask along x, run both condylar searches there, compose with the left-side conventions). Only the angle is returned, so no landmark has to be mapped back. The composition contributed **exactly 0** to the old disagreement: `side='right'` negates the x components, which is algebraically the same as mirroring both lines, so it held for *any* pair of lines and therefore said nothing about the search. The asymmetry was entirely the search, which rotates the section about the image centre and rounds. Before: a half and its mirror disagreed by a median 1.16°, p90 3.06°, max 6.21°, exact on only 5 of the 100 Augsburg reader-study halves. After: **exactly 0 on all 100**. **Do not pool knee-rotation rows across this change**, and note these are *right-half* figures because a left half is bit-identical: over the full Augsburg cohort (1902 measurable halves) **all 951 image-left halves are unchanged to 0.000e+00** and **914 of 951 image-right halves move**, by a median 1.11°, p90 2.94°, with 85 sign flips; the cohort mean goes +3.88° → +3.96° and the negative share 22.5% → 21.1%, so this is scatter from the search and not a bias. The **max of 54.19° is one leg and is not a correction**: PA000877's femoral search lands on reference layer 0 (the FOV edge), takes the no-notch fallback and returns a condylar line 7.3 voxels long directly and 6.3 reflected, so both answers are degenerate (69.16° → 14.97°, against a cohort mean of ~4°) and neither is trustworthy — the second-largest move is 7.01°, and only 11 of 951 move by more than 5°. A short-condylar-line QC gate, analogous to `murphy_axis_short`, catches that leg, and the base rate it needed exists as of 2026-11-06.
