# Hip landmarks: subchondral cone, MRI head consensus fit, Murphy neck base, repaired paths, get_contour

*Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-11-12 when that file was cut below its size limit; `CLAUDE.md` keeps a summary and links here. Nothing below was rewritten.*

- The ray-traced subchondral femoral-head-to-acetabulum distance (`measurements/hip.py::calculate_subchondral_distance_ray_tracing{,_ct}`) aims its ray cone with `cone_axis`, which defaults to **`'superior'`** (world `+z`, via `_superior_direction`). That axis samples the weight-bearing acetabular dome in every subject, independently of the labelling scheme. The former default `'acetabulum'` (FHC → peri-acetabular centroid) is retained as opt-in legacy behaviour only: the centroid is dragged ~46° medially by the ischium/pubis in the label, so roughly half its rays cross the non-articular **acetabular fossa** — where the head contacts no bone — inflating the mean by ~1.5 mm and roughly tripling its spread (the two modes agree, 3.65–3.77 vs 3.78–3.94 mm, over the innermost 0–20° where both sample true lunate surface). Because the fixed axis no longer aims at the label, 3.5–18.5% of rays clear the lateral acetabular rim and are dropped; heavy loss is logged as a WARNING on the `morphometry` logger, thresholded by `min_ray_fraction` (0.7). `docs/illustrations/plot_subchondral_3d.py --axis` reads its default off this signature so the illustration cannot drift from the library.
- The MRI femoral-head centre (`hip.py::get_femoral_head_center`, non-isotropic path) is a **seeded consensus sphere fit in true mm** (`_fit_femoral_head_consensus`): seed a sphere on the slab contour points medial of the top-slice centroid and within `C.FEMORAL_HEAD_SEED_DEPTH_MM` (30, about one head radius) of the top slab slice, then refit on every slab point within `C.FEMORAL_HEAD_CONSENSUS_TOLERANCE_MM` (2.5) of the current sphere until the inlier set is stable (cap `C.FEMORAL_HEAD_CONSENSUS_MAX_ITERATIONS`, WARNING logged if hit). The depth limit matters: the slab can run 40-70 mm below the head top (its bound still uses the pipelines' `x_ratio`), and a seed taken from those slices starts in the neck basin or calcar and stays there at every tolerance. The fit replaced a lateral cutoff at `0.9 * max_x` — 0.9 times an absolute voxel *index* — which made the centre depend on where the hip sat in the image and could not tell lateral head surface at the equator from neck surface below it. Pass the voxel spacing via the optional `spacing=` argument (all callers holding an `Image` do: proximal reference line, CCD, acetabular anteversion, CEA, subchondral, femoral offset). Array-only callers (alpha angle, acetabular depth, min distance) fall back to voxels of `(1, 1, x_ratio)` with a 1 mm in-plane voxel; that is still translation-invariant, but with the pipelines' current `x_ratio` its centre differs from the spacing fit by ~5 mm in z (median, max 14). No production caller is affected (the NaKo scripts are isotropic, and `calculate_min_distance_between_femoral_head_and_acetabulum` has no production caller at all), so that error is **latent, never realised** — and on the alpha angle it is now unreachable rather than merely unrealised, since that entry point refuses anisotropic spacing outright, so on any accepted call the real spacing *is* isotropic and the `(1, 1, x_ratio)` fallback at the default `x_ratio=1` is `(1, 1, 1)` — and it is not resolved by correcting `x_ratio`, which task #6 settled the other way (see the end of this bullet); the fix for an array-only caller is to pass `spacing`. The isotropic (NaKo) path is unchanged. Accuracy, checked against full-contour circle fits on the top head slices (pure head, independent of either fit): the old centre sat ~2.6 mm MEDIAL on image-left halves and on the reflected torsion path, and ~2.2 mm LATERAL on directly measured image-right halves (CCD, CEA, subchondral, femoral offset, acetabular anteversion), correlated with the image x position; the consensus centre's signed medial offset is ~0.0 mm (median -0.1 to -0.2) and its in-plane distance median 0.3 / p90 0.7 / max 2.0 mm. Pinned by `tests/unit/test_femoral_head_consensus.py` (head+neck phantom, exact translation, a calcar phantom for the seed depth), `tests/characterization/test_head_centre_translation.py` (PA000001: centre/radius, Murphy with the knee fixed, anteversion, CCD) and, for the mirror equivariance of task #11, `tests/characterization/test_femoral_head_equivariance.py` (non-isotropic MRI on the two reader-study patients the old seed truncation got wrong, CT, and isotropic NaKo) plus `tests/unit/test_femoral_head_isotropic_equivariance.py` (phantom; now an exact-equality assertion, since a right half double-reflects to the identical array and only the index map-back can fail). Still position-dependent, independently of the head fit (the one strict xfail left there, task #9): Lee torsion, through `contour_femoral_neck`'s slice rotations about the image centre. The knee condylar line (task #5) and the alpha angle's oblique reslice are both fixed, so `contour_femoral_neck` is now the only image-rotating landmark transport left in the package. With the corrected centre, CCD and Lee agree better with the Augsburg readers but Murphy read further above them; part of that was Murphy's distal landmark (see the next bullet). Separately, the pipelines' `x_ratio = spacing_z / 2 * spacing_x` is not the slice aspect (`spacing_z / spacing_x`) — it is dimensionally `mm / 2 × mm` — but **it is deliberately not corrected, and the earlier note here that "the head's own sphericity supports the latter" is withdrawn** (task #6, settled 2026-10-29; `docs/ccd_x_ratio.md`, regenerated by `scripts/analyse_ccd_x_ratio.py`). On this path `x_ratio` only bounds the head slab walk, so it is inert on every dataset measured (Augsburg, NaKo and a straight-femur phantom give identical results at 1, 5.47 and 9.14) and `calculate_anteversion` derives `spacing_z / spacing_x` from the image itself; the femoral torsion is bit-identical between the two values on all 190 resolving Augsburg reader-study (half × method) measurements, **max difference 0.000e+00°**. **The one measurement `x_ratio` materially decides is the MRI CCD**, through `get_femoral_neck_center`'s shell sampling, and there the dimensionally correct value is measurably *worse*: moving 5.469 → 9.143 on the 50 reader-study patients changes the CCD by a median 4.1–5.5° (max 19.0) and takes the within-patient left/right Pearson from **+0.770 to +0.393** (mean |L−R| 3.60 → 5.61°, no reader involved), the bias against the reader mean from +3.73/+4.81 to +3.95/**+7.77**° and the MAD from 5.38/7.26 to 7.00/9.62°. **The mechanism, not a correlation:** `_femoral_neck_points_and_center` samples a *sphere* shell of radius `r … FEMORAL_NECK_SPHERE_OUTER_FACTOR × r` in the scaled space, so a larger `x_ratio` shrinks its reach in *slices* — on these 10 mm hips the median neck point cloud goes from 11 slices (at 1.0) to 4 (at 5.469) to 3 (at 9.143) to 2 (at 12), and at the true aspect **30 of 100 image halves** span ≤2 slices, pinning the neck centroid's z to one or two 10 mm sample positions. So `x_ratio` is an **empirical conditioning parameter of this landmark, not a unit conversion**; treat the name as misleading and do not "fix" it without replacing the landmark. Two consequences worth keeping: **the CCD's absolute level is an artefact of this factor and no value of it repairs it** — over the sweep the cohort mean runs 153.5° (at 1.0) down to 138.4° (at 5.469, the shipped value, which is the minimum of the whole family) and then flattens at 139–140°, so the ~138° against a 125–135° literature range that `docs/augsburg_reader_bias.md` flags belongs to the neck landmark itself; and **the sweep does not license picking a factor**, because its two criteria disagree (left/right agreement peaks near 3–4, the reader bias at 5.469) and both are computed on the same 50 patients any choice would then be quoted against — the `CEA_UPPER_MARGIN_FACTOR` mistake. **What is actually open is therefore narrower and should be restated:** the CCD's level is set by a conditioning parameter, the shipped 5.469 happens to be the family's minimum *and* its best value on every reader-free criterion, the resulting 138.4° is inside the in-vivo 114-140° reference interval, and no millimetre-based reformulation improves any of that. A genuine improvement needs a **different neck landmark**, not a different unit. **Reclassified 2026-11-06 from an open backlog item to a documented property with no action indicated**, because all three things an item needs are absent: there is **no defect** (138.4° sits inside the in-vivo 114-140° reference interval and below the >140° coxa valga line), there is **no better parameter value** (the shipped 5.469 is the minimum of the whole `x_ratio` family *and* its best value on every reader-free criterion), and there is **no published alternative to implement** (the millimetre route is algebraically the family's worst member). What remains is that the level rests on a conditioning parameter rather than on anatomy — a real limitation, worth knowing before quoting an absolute CCD, and not a task. Taking it further means *designing* a neck axis and then having nothing to validate it against, which is the one move this file's own history argues hardest against. **The workings — the shell-sampling mechanism, the refutation of the "sample it in millimetres" premise, and the literature check that put 138.4 deg inside the in-vivo 114-140 deg reference interval — are in [`docs/evidence/ccd_neck_shell_x_ratio.md`](docs/evidence/ccd_neck_shell_x_ratio.md).**
- Murphy's distal landmark (`femur.py::get_femoral_neck_base`) follows the published definition ("just above the lesser trochanter"): the femur centroid on the **first axial slice above the lesser trochanter's superior extent** — 10 mm up on the Augsburg hip MRI, 3 mm on whole-leg CT, 1 mm on isotropic NaKo, i.e. as close above the trochanter as the acquisition resolves. `_find_trochanter_minor` returns that extent alongside the point `get_trochanter_minor` reports, now `(x, y, layer)` (`y` used to be a copy of `x`). The centroid is a continuous index, paired with the unrounded head centre (`get_proximal_reference_line` keeps the int16 cast only for Lee and Tomczak, whose searches index the mask with it); the head-to-base axis is short (~25 mm in-plane on the hip MRI), so the former slice *through* the trochanter and the int16 truncation mattered — Murphy's positive bias against the Augsburg readers dropped by ~1-1.5°. A fixed 10 mm offset was tried and rejected on the NaKo raters' signed manual Murphy values (1 mm data, 120 hips): it reads +5.6° high with ICC2 0.85, where 0-5 mm levels give bias -1.2 to +2.2 and ICC2 up to 0.94. The Augsburg hips cannot distinguish offsets ≤ 10 mm (all pick the same slice), so that evidence comes from NaKo alone. A level less than `C.MURPHY_NECK_BASE_MIN_HEAD_DISTANCE_MM` (10) below the head centre — measured in mm from the image's own slice spacing — lies inside the head, i.e. the trochanter was mis-detected, and raises `RuntimeError` (4 Augsburg legs, 2 NMDID). The remaining Murphy bias is not explained by the distal landmark.
- **Three advertised-but-broken paths, repaired 2026-11-06, all zero-movement.** (1) `method='tomczak'` **raised for its entire advertised life**: `femur.py::get_trochanter_major` read `hip_image.get_shape()`, which no `Image` has ever defined (the accessor is the `shape` property — `nibabel`'s `.shape` or SimpleITK's `GetSize()`, both `(x, y, z)`), while `get_proximal_reference_line` and `calculate_femoral_torsion` named tomczak in their docstrings and admitted it in their assertions. No caller in the repo exercises it, which is why nothing caught it — and `AttributeError` is **outside** the `(RuntimeError, AssertionError, ValueError)` tuple the batch scripts and `compute_torsion.py` catch, so a cohort run that asked for tomczak would have **aborted** rather than written a NaN row. **Its values are not validated against anything**: they are the first the method has ever produced, nothing compares them with a reader, a publication or another method, and the trochanter-major landmark they rest on has no characterization test of its own. `proximal_reference_line_tomczak_left` pins them purely as a regression anchor (PA000001 left: end [92, 189, 8], `r_fh` 18.982798, `r_tm` 10.912599) — **do not quote them as a measurement**. (2) `calculate_alpha_angle` hardcoded `np.where(mask == 1, 1, 0)` for the femoral contour while the head centre above it honours `segmentation_label`, so a caller passing another label got a contour of label 1 against a centre fitted to its own; every caller passes 1, so the repair is zero-movement (8d138af left it alone only to keep that commit's attribution clean). (3) `utils.points_on_circle` ended `return np.array(points) > 0`, a boolean array of "coordinate is positive" rather than the coordinates it had collected, contradicting its name and docstring; its single caller reads only `len(...) > 0`, so the length — and therefore the behaviour — was unaffected. **The evidence that all three move nothing**: capturing under `MORPH_UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` created exactly **one new** golden file and modified **no existing one**, which is the sharpest check available since the goldens pin the alpha angle, both femoral torsion methods, the tibial torsion and the whole NaKo hip family.
- **`utils.get_contour` reports the array border as contour. CLOSED 2026-11-06 as a deliberate non-action, not an open item: it reaches nothing, and fixing it would move the femoral head centre for no measured gain.** It is `mask - binary_erosion(mask)` with scipy's default `border_value=0`, so a mask voxel **on** the array border always survives as contour: a hip FOV cuts the femoral shaft, and those cut faces are reported as anatomical surface. The artefact is real and large — measured over the 100 Augsburg reader-study halves, **6.94%** of contour voxels sit on the border (median 296 of 4277, max 534 / 14.22%), on **100 of 100** halves. But it reaches no measurement: against a border-insensitive variant (`binary_erosion(..., border_value=1)`, i.e. outside counts as foreground so a cut face is not surface) the femoral head centre and radius are **bit-identical on 100 of 100**, because `get_femoral_head_center` restricts its point cloud to the head slab and the cut faces lie outside it; and the alpha angle's section cannot be affected at all, because its window carries a `_NECK_SECTION_MARGIN` of 1 and asserts no mask voxel lies on any border. **This is latent by virtue of those two restrictions, not by design**, which is the reason to leave it alone: `get_contour` feeds `get_contour_points`, whose consumers include that sphere-fit point cloud (`hip.py:404`) and the alpha angle (`measurements/hip.py:594`), so changing it would move the head centre and with it every hip measurement. It was also twice mis-blamed for the Lee crop residual, which turned out to be `int()` truncation; see the Lee bullets above.
