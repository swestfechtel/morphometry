# The posterior condylar line: fallback, read-out and cohort re-measurement

The evidence behind the three changes to `knee.py::get_knee_reference_line` and their consumers:
the no-notch fallback that used to flip the frame, the index-frame-invariant read-out (task #5),
and the knee-rotation composition and mirror equivariance. Includes the NMDID cohort
re-measurement and the Augsburg line-length survey that the QC gate's threshold rests on.
Moved verbatim out of `CLAUDE.md`, which keeps the constructions, the thresholds, the
do-not-pool warnings and the figures that must not be quoted bare.

Two cautions that travel with these numbers: the raw NMDID tibial-torsion sd of 24.79 deg is a
fold-seam artefact and must not be quoted as a spread, and the largest movers are the old
fallback's ~180 deg frame flip rather than a measurement moving by 130 deg.

**A note on cross-references.** These paragraphs were written inside `CLAUDE.md`, so an
"above", "below" or "recorded here" may point at a bullet that stayed there rather than at
something on this page. Nothing was reworded, which is the point; the file it came from is
`CLAUDE.md` and the two are meant to be read together.

## Prevalence and effect of the no-notch fallback, by notch status

**Prevalence and effect, partitioned by notch status.** Augsburg reader study (50 patients, 100 tibial half-measurements): the fallback fires on 11 (11%) — the fix does not change how often it fires. The 89 notch-found halves are bit-identical (tibial torsion, femoral torsion Lee and Murphy, knee rotation angle: max |Δ| 0.00°). Of the 11: five previously raised `RuntimeError('No notch found.')` and now resolve — PA000077 image-left 40.08°, PA000181 image-right 46.12°, PA000998 image-left 41.80°, PA001069 image-right 44.07° and **PA001093 image-right 19.65°, which is the one value here not to quote unfootnoted**: it sits 18.1° below its own nearest notch-resolving layer (37.73°, at only 0.71 of the selected layer's convex area) and 7–17° below both readers (Felix 26.78°, Jonathan 37.0°). The other four agree with their within-leg reference (40.08 vs 40.49; 44.07 vs 43.79; 46.12 vs 42.16 at 0.84 area; PA000998 has no reference within ±2 slices). The six that returned a value moved by a median 11.23°, p90 15.80°, max 16.28° — PA000717 image-left 32.69 -> 48.02°, image-right 28.15 -> 44.43°, PA000347 image-left 29.49 -> 39.95°, PA000077 image-right 23.12 -> 35.13°, PA000668 image-right 33.85 -> 43.85°, and PA000166 image-right unchanged at 42.20° because its 180.6° frame flip happened to map the posterior tangent *direction* onto itself (the endpoints were still on the wrong cortex). The knee rotation angle (which shares the tibial condylar line) moved on the same legs (n=7, median 10.46°, max 26.57°) and four of its halves stopped failing. The Augsburg *femoral* line never took the fallback (0/100), so no Augsburg femoral torsion moved. `dfg_knees` (96 knee MRI, the only local cohort that exercises the femoral line; no hip/ankle, so only the line itself can be scored): femoral fallback on 10 of the 95 measurable scans (10.5%), tibial on 3 of 96; the notch-found scans are bit-identical (tilt change 0.00° on all 85 femoral / 93 tibial). Not all ten femoral lines flipped: the per-scan tilt change is 8.5, 16.9, 36.5, 147.1, 147.3, 150.3, 150.5, 155.0, 158.4, 159.5°, so the median 148.8° is honest but three scans moved by less than 90°: on those the old line's *direction* happened to land closer to the posterior tangent, while its endpoints were on the anterior cortex all the same (2.0–3.2 voxels from it, 5.4–122.9 from the posterior one, versus 0.0–2.2 posterior after the fix). Tibial: 8.7, 27.9, 80.5°.

## Validating the fallback against an independent cue

**Validated against an independent cue, not just movement.** Distance from each reported condylar endpoint to the posterior cortex of its own section (per column, the most posterior mask voxel): Augsburg fallback halves went from a median 40.5, max 51.0 voxels *anterior* of it (100% beyond 5 voxels) to a median -1.0, max 0.0 (none beyond 5; a negative value is the back-rotated point's `uint16` cast landing a voxel outside the mask), the 89 notch-found halves being 0.0 before and after; `dfg_knees` femoral fallback scans from median 8.2 / p90 77.5 / max 122.9 voxels (60% beyond 5) to 1.0 / 2.2 / 6.0 (5%), tibial from 48.1 / 88.2 / 100.0 to 0.0 / 1.2 / 1.4. The one notch-*found* outlier left (`K7_90_belastet`, 58.1 voxels off the posterior cortex and 4.1 off the anterior one) is one of the three `dfg_knees` scans that do not reorient to LPI, so the 'posterior = +y' premise fails there. That premise is **shared with the notch path** (`get_dorsal_mask_point` takes the largest coronal coordinate), so this is pre-existing, not introduced here — K7_90_belastet is bit-identical before and after (58.1 voxels either way). It does **not** degrade gracefully: on a synthetic plateau turned past about +45° (or to ±90°) the reported line comes out 80–82° wrong, folded into (−90, 90].

**No torsion pipeline guards against it**: the `non_lpi_orientation` flag lives in `morphometry/cartilage/knee.py` and is surfaced only by `compute_knee_cartilage_thickness.py` and `analyse_thinnest_point_flexion.py`, none of which call `get_knee_reference_line`, and neither `compute_torsion.py` nor `process_augsburg_*` / `process_nmdid.py` / the API worker read `Image.orientation_ok`. Wiring that into the torsion pipelines was listed as open work; **it is now closed as unreachable rather than done (2026-11-06)**. Surveyed by header over every cohort a torsion pipeline runs on:

**6225 of 6225** MRI files reorient to LPI — Augsburg uniformly raw LPS (5715 under `preprocessed/` plus 450 in `reader_study_augsburg/`), the NaKo sample uniformly raw LIP — and with the CT survey's 1520 of 1520 that is **7745 files across four cohorts with not one exception**. The only non-LPI scans known anywhere are the three `dfg_knees` cartilage scans, which no torsion pipeline can reach. So the guard would be free insurance against a configuration that occurs on none of the available data; it is worth adding the day a cohort arrives that is not uniformly one raw orientation, and the survey is the thing to re-run then. Second cue, within the leg: on the 10 Augsburg fallback halves where another layer of the same tibia resolves a notch, that layer's answer needs no fallback, and the old fallback was a median 11.85° off it (n=5 that returned a value, mean 8.94) where the new one is 1.39° (same five legs, mean 3.00; over all ten, median 1.49, mean 5.50) — against a layer-to-layer spread of median 2.26° / p90 8.38° / max 41.50° on the 89 notch-found halves, which is the scale any single-layer landmark carries anyway. Third cue, the readers (compared on |pipeline|, since the Augsburg readers very likely record unsigned torsion; every Augsburg tibial value is positive, so it is a no-op here): on the same six legs the MAD against the two readers' mean goes 9.92 -> 5.85° and the signed bias -9.92 -> +0.76°; per reader 15.08 -> 8.45° (Jonathan) and 7.30 -> 7.54° (Felix, median |Δ| 7.90 -> 2.99). Felix is flat *only* because he recorded 27.75° for **both** legs of PA000717, where Jonathan recorded 45/55° and that leg's own notch-resolved layers say 46.8/46.0°: dropping PA000717, his MAD on the remaining four goes 9.61 -> 2.07°, which makes the attribution decisive rather than merely plausible (and the reader mean on those four goes 10.66 -> 5.11°). Over all 95 paired legs with a reader value the MAD against the reader mean goes 4.28 -> 4.03°.

## The NMDID cohort re-measurement, the fold-seam tail and the movement probe

**The CT fixture is now verified, and the rest of the NMDID cohort is re-measured too (the end of this bullet has it).** The fallback is recorded as firing on 62% of NMDID legs (the short plateau section in the `split_ct_image` knee crop makes the notch search fail more often; extending the crop alone does not fix it), and `case-105094` is one of them — the fallback fires on **both** of its tibial halves. With the sshfs mount back (2026-11-02) both CT tibial goldens moved and were re-captured: `tibial_torsion_ct_left` **78.116 → 66.683** (−11.43°) and `tibial_torsion_ct_right` **77.525 → 41.966** (−35.56°). Validated the same three ways as the Augsburg halves rather than on movement alone: (a) both reported endpoints sit **on** the posterior cortex of their own section (gaps +0.0/+0.0 and +0.0/+1.0 voxels, all four on bone), where the old fallback put them on the anterior one; (b) the new values are stable across every *comparable* neighbouring layer — image-left layers 16/17/19 at 0.91/0.98/1.00 of the selected layer's convex area give 66.47/67.09/69.61° and the single notch-*resolving* layer (15, at 0.74 area) agrees at 66.20°, while image-right layer 11 (0.85 area) reproduces 41.966° exactly; (c) **no layer reproduces the old values** — nothing in the left neighbourhood exceeds 69.6° against a golden of 78.12, nothing in the right exceeds 41.97° against 77.53. The old pair being near-identical on two different legs (78.12 / 77.53) is the artefact's own signature, a ~180° frame flip reading the anterior cortex; the new pair are properly distinct legs.

**Caveat on the left half**: 66.68° is high against a published tibial torsion of 38.9 ± 7.0° (Anzillotti et al. 2026, patellofemoral cohort), but it is stable across layers and corroborated by the notch-resolving one, so it is this definition on this cohort rather than a landmark failure — do not quote it as validated against literature.

Tibial torsion resolves on **2691 of 2948 halves (91.3%)**, against 95.7% on the verification sample.

**The median reproduces that sample almost exactly — 35.24 deg against 34.73** — but the raw sd is **24.79** against 17.25, and the difference is not anatomy:

**Read that last figure as corroboration, not validation**: the published cohort is patellofemoral and selects both of its slices differently (the knee-rotation bullet above records the same protocol mismatch), and nothing here was calibrated against it — which is precisely what makes a 23-point move toward it worth recording. ## The knee-rotation search: what moved and the survey that was redone

**The defect that mattered in production was internal, not between the sides**: because the two torsions reflected and this did not, a right-side row's knee rotation angle rested on a *different* posterior condylar line from the femoral and tibial torsion in the **same row** — over the 50 right halves the tibial line's orientation differed by a median 1.23° (max 4.28, equal on only 8) and the femoral line's by a median 0.62° (max 2.70, equal on 10), while on left halves both consumers agreed exactly on all 50. (The earlier note here that fixing this "would also tighten tibial torsion's condylar line" was wrong: tibial torsion already reflected, so it does not move at all.)

**The first survey was wrong on every right half and was re-derived; the figures below are the corrected ones.** The error, found by review: the survey ran `get_knee_reference_line` directly on each image half, but all three consumers measure a right half as the left-right **reflection** of a left one (`measurements/femur.py:145` `knee_mask[::-1]`, `measurements/knee.py:168` recursing on `segmentation_mask[::-1]`, and `_tibial_torsion_points`), so the line a right-half measurement — and any QC gate describing it — actually uses is the reflected one. That is not cosmetic: **left halves are bit-identical (0 of 1903 change) while 1627 of 1903 right halves do**, by a median **2.267** voxels, p90 9.48, max **18.03**. Task #5 bounds the mirror *bearing* difference (median 0.33°, max 2.06°) and says nothing about *length*, and the search is least mirror-stable exactly where the line is shortest — which is where the threshold lives.

## The line-length survey, the reader evidence and the cohort sign flips

**The gate is not a proxy for either neighbouring signal**: `PA000435` flags without taking the no-notch fallback (which fires on 300 of 1902 tibial halves, only 3 of which flag, so ~297 take it without flagging), and `PA000877`'s *image-left* femoral half is also at layer 0 and also takes the fallback yet measures 41.44 voxels and does not flag — a negative control on the same leg and the same scan.

**What this does not buy is accuracy**: within-patient left/right agreement does *not* improve significantly (mean |L−R| 3.39° → 3.17° over the 50 reader-study patients, Wilcoxon p=0.37, 28 patients better and 20 worse, median essentially unchanged), so the case for the change is equivariance and internal consistency, not reduced scatter. On a right half the `plot` overlay is now drawn on the **reflected** mask, so the knee appears mirrored — the same consequence `calculate_ccd_ct` documents. Pinned by `tests/characterization/test_knee_rotation_equivariance.py` on the three reader-study halves that carry content (PA001055 image-left 6.21°, PA000717 image-right 4.14°, PA000699 image-right 3.91°); the phantom anchors in `tests/unit/test_in_plane_spacing.py` now pin the two sides as **two independent constants**, because the right value is no longer the negation of the left one.

**Evidence, and what it does not show.** The within-patient left/right structure favours the invariant composition and is *sign-symmetric*, so it says nothing about the direction: over the 951 Augsburg patients with both knees (identical magnitudes under both compositions, so only the sign assignment differs) Spearman L vs R rises from +0.517 to +0.685, Pearson from +0.488 to +0.677, sign concordance from 70.6% to 82.5% and mean |L-R| falls from 4.21° to 3.10° (bootstrap ΔSpearman +0.168, 95% CI [+0.106, +0.231]). The readers cannot adjudicate the direction and lean the other way: on the 25 of 98 reader-study halves where the two conventions disagree, Jonathan's sign matches the OLD image-frame one 19:6 (binomial p=0.015; r 0.839 / MAE 2.37° against the old composition versus 0.467 / 3.83° against the new) while Felix matches the new one 16:9 (p=0.23) — but Felix recorded 0/102 negatives, so his values carry no sign information at all (he agrees best with `|value|`: r 0.728, MAE 2.39°, bias +0.73°), exactly as `augsburg-reader-scale-factor` records for torsion. Jonathan's agreement with an image-frame sign is most simply explained by his having measured each line against the image axes himself, which is the natural thing to do with a 2D angle tool and reproduces the same frame dependence (he measured in 2025-10, so he was not following `docs/reader_measurement_guide.md`, added 2026-06) — but that is a hypothesis, not a measurement, and it cannot be checked from the tables. What it does *not* do is support the old composition: an angle that changes when the patient is rotated in the scanner is not an anatomical quantity whoever reproduces it. And since the published convention fixes the direction independently of all of this, the reader evidence is now an account of what Jonathan measured rather than a competing candidate for the sign — asking him remains worth doing (it would also sharpen task #2), but no longer to decide this.

**727 halves (38.2%) change sign** — 364/951 image-left, 363/951 image-right — by a median 7.29° and up to 44.82° (the change is exactly 2x the value; no magnitude changes anywhere, 0 fold-seam cases). The cohort mean goes +1.98° -> +3.80°, the negative share 36.3% -> 21.5%, and mean |value| is unchanged at 5.101°. On the 100 reader-study halves 26 change, median 8.92°, max 21.42°. This is **on top of** task #4's movement (see the fallback paragraphs above), which is a separate effect on the same column: measured with the old composition on both sides, task #4 moved 197 of the 1798 previously-measurable halves (median 12.09°, p90 26.40°, max 61.11°) and made 104 halves resolve that used to raise (reader study: 7 moved, median 10.46°, max 26.57°, 4 newly resolving), and 122 of those 197 also flip sign here. Neither change should be credited with the other's movement.

## Condylar line: fallback, task #5 read-out, line-length survey (from CLAUDE.md)

*Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-11-12 when that file was cut below its size limit; `CLAUDE.md` keeps a summary and links here. Nothing below was rewritten.*

   **The no-notch condylar fallback no longer flips the frame.** `knee.py::get_knee_reference_line` uses `find_notch` for exactly one thing: the sagittal coordinate that separates the two condyles, so that the posterior-most mask point of one can be told from the posterior-most point of the other (`rotate_mask_dorsal_points`). When the biggest-convex-area section does not resolve the intercondylar gap at all, the old fallback (`rotate_tibia`) rotated the section so that its longest contour chord pointed along `+x` — taking that *undirected* chord in its `-x` representative, so the rotation was ~180° minus the chord's tilt (measured per leg on the six Augsburg tibial halves that resolved: `rotate_tibia`'s own angle 140.3 / 142.6 / 158.0 / 159.7 / 169.0 / 171.2°, and after the second rotation a total frame rotation of 140.3 / 159.7 / 176.3 / 176.4 / 180.4 / 180.6°) — and so reported the **anterior** cortex as 'dorsal'. It then searched for a notch a second time in that rotated frame and raised `RuntimeError('No notch found.')` when that failed too (that exact message still comes out of `femur.py::contour_femoral_neck`, an unrelated path, so the string no longer identifies the removed code). The fallback now takes the section's centre of mass as the divider (`knee.py::get_condylar_divider`) and leaves the frame alone: the mask is assumed LPI, where the coronal axis increases posteriorly, so 'posterior' is given and no 180° ambiguity arises — the orientation is never inferred from the rotation that produced the points. One code path, no new threshold, and the notch path is untouched, so every leg whose notch search succeeds is bit-identical. `rotate_tibia` is gone. **Why the centroid is good enough, measured rather than argued:** forcing the fallback (`find_notch` -> `None`) on the 89 Augsburg tibial halves whose notch search *does* succeed moves the divider by a median -3.0 voxels (p5 -9.6, p95 +4.6, max |Δ| 11.6, i.e. up to 17% of the section width) and changes the tibial torsion by a median **0.00°** — 93% of halves within 1°, 98% within 3°, max 3.27° (PA000012 image-left 22.59 -> 25.86) — with 89/89 still resolving. The divider only decides which side of the section the second posterior point is taken from, so its exact position barely matters on a real plateau, and the fallback is therefore not a different measurement from the notch path. **Why not make the notch search succeed instead**: on these sections there is nothing to find — over the posterior half of the section the widest intercondylar gap is 0–2 voxels against a `thresh` of >2, where the 89 Augsburg halves that do resolve have a median 15. Another layer *does* resolve a notch on 10 of the 11 Augsburg halves — 9 of them within ±2 slices of the selected layer (PA000998 image-left's nearest is 3 slices away) and PA000077 image-right has none on any layer — but those layers are not equivalent sections: the nearest one's convex area is 0.70, 0.71, 0.78, 0.84, 0.97 (×4) and 0.99 (×2) of the selected layer's, four of them below 0.85. Re-choosing the layer would move every leg's landmark and still need a fallback for PA000077; the fallback has to be correct regardless.
   **The condylar line read-out is index-frame invariant (task #5, 2026-11-04).** `knee.py::get_knee_reference_line` used to find its two posterior-condylar endpoints by *rotating the 2-D section* — `skimage.transform.rotate(mask, -angle, resize=True, preserve_range=True) > 0` in `utils.rotate_mask_vec_parallel` — then reading landmarks off that **resampled** array, sweeping a rasterised ray in 1° steps, and mapping the result back through shape-derived offsets (`rot_offset`, `rot_center`, both truncated with `int()`) and a final `uint16` cast. Every one of those steps depends on where the anatomy sits in the array, so **the reported line was partly a property of the array bounds rather than of the anatomy**. Measured over 200 real sections (50 Augsburg reader-study patients × 2 image halves × femur/tibia): padding the array by (4, 3, 2) voxels — which changes every index while leaving every voxel's physical position identical — moved the endpoints correctly on only **46 of 200** (median 1.0, max 51.0 voxels of error), and cropping to the mask's bounding box on only **32 of 200** (max 57.0). A pad at the *high* end, which changes no anatomy index at all and must therefore change nothing, was exact on only **41 of 200** (max 55.0). This was reachable in production, not hypothetical: `split_image_left_right` splits at `shape[0] // 2`, and the API's `_detect_slabs` / `_split_volume` crop at *detected* gaps and changepoints, so the same series re-ingested with a boundary one voxel over got a different number — up to **4.9°** of torsion, with 175 of 300 empty-margin crops moving at least one of the four derived angles by more than 0.5°.
   **What was already invariant, and therefore untouched.** Over the same 200 sections the layer choice (`get_layer_with_biggest_convex_area`), the notch resolve/fail decision, the divider (`find_notch` or `get_condylar_divider`), the two posterior points that set the rotation angle, and the angle itself are **all exactly invariant or exactly equivariant, 200/200**. The entire defect was downstream of the angle, which is why the fix is contained: none of those steps changed.
   **The replacement rotates coordinates, never the image.** `knee.py::get_condylar_tangent_points` rotates the section's mask-point *coordinates* about the **divider**, picks the posterior-most point (ties within `CONDYLAR_POSTERIOR_TIE_VOXELS` broken by maximum rotated x, reproducing `get_dorsal_mask_point(..., knee=True)`), and takes the far-side point of minimal sweep angle, ties broken by **maximum distance from the start** — which both reproduces the old `shrink_points_to_mask` (it returned the *farthest* mask point along the ray, not the nearest) and is **required for invariance**: without it, exact collinear ties leave `argmin` to float noise and the result is invariant on only 198/200. Both endpoints are returned as their original coordinates, so there is **no inverse transform and no `uint16` cast**. The divider is the right origin for a reason stronger than "anatomy-derived": it is an `int16` index, so `points - divider` is exact integer arithmetic and a translated array reproduces the rotated coordinates **bitwise** — the invariance is provable rather than measured, and it is now 200/200 for padding, for bounding-box cropping and for a high-end pad. (Rotating about the array origin instead is non-invariant on 3/200 from pure float noise.)
   **The landmark was routinely not on the bone, which is the strongest argument for the change.** The signed per-column distance from each reported endpoint to the posterior cortex of its own column: the old code put **202 of 399** measurable endpoints a voxel *outside* the mask (the `uint16` cast), 195 exactly on it, and one endpoint — `PA000717` image-right's tibial start — in a column holding **no bone voxel whatsoever**. The paragraph above on task #4 quotes this artefact as "median -1.0, max 0.0" for the 11 no-notch fallback halves; it in fact affected **more than half of all endpoints on every half**, fallback or not. It is now **0 on all 400** Augsburg endpoints, none negative. **Do not read that as a property of the construction, because it is not one.** "Posterior-most" is defined in the *rotated* condylar frame, so where the frame angle is large the extremum need not be the posterior-most voxel of its own *unrotated* column: on `dfg_knees` it is not, on **2 of 95** clean, LPI, notch-resolving femurs (reproduced independently by both the implementer and the reviewer) — K5_90_unbelastet at a frame angle of −75.96° (start 2 voxels anterior of its column's cortex) and K9_90_unbelastet at −75.07° (1 voxel) — against K1_0_unbelastet at −34.08°, which is exactly 0. Augsburg never shows it because its frame angles are small. **The property that does hold everywhere is weaker and still a real strengthening: both endpoints are now always actual mask voxels**, where before they could lie outside the mask entirely (K9_90_unbelastet's pre-change start was 7 voxels off its column's cortex and was not a mask voxel at all). The exact-zero figures are pinned as *tests* on the cohorts where they hold (the synthetic sections in `tests/unit/test_knee_reference_line.py`, the three fallback legs in `tests/characterization/test_condylar_fallback.py` tightened from `<= 2` to `== 0`), never asserted as a general invariant. `test_condylar_line_invariance.py::test_endpoints_are_mask_points` instead asserts a **relative** margin that survives a turned frame — each endpoint at least 5 voxels further from the anterior cortex than from the posterior one, the cohort minimum being 6.3 voxels on a narrow femoral section — which is the cue that actually discriminates the task #4 defect, since that put the point *on* the anterior cortex, 40+ voxels the wrong way round.
   **Two semantic changes, neither forced by invariance.** The old sweep required **≥ `thresh` (2) rasterised voxels** on the ray before stopping — a rule that exists only because a rasterised line through a single voxel is unreliable, and which has no purpose in exact geometry. And the old sweep **quantised the tangent angle to `step_size` (1°)**. Both are gone, and together they account for the movement below. `thresh` and `step_size` are kept in the signature, documented as obsolete, and **raise `ValueError`** on a non-default value (the `method=` precedent on the cartilage entry points); no caller in the repo passes either. A third possible change was deliberately **not** made: the construction keeps a far-side point that is *more posterior* than the start unreachable, so the result is **not** a strict posterior tangent — such a point exists on 111 of 200 sections, and reaching it first would differ on 113/200 by a median 0.299°, max 1.759°, and would leave 0/200 sections with cortex posterior of the reported line against 113/200 for this construction and 98/200 for the old one. That reading is arguably cleaner but is a different landmark, so it was left alone; this change preserves the pre-change construction.

   **Endpoint *order* now breaks a near-tie deterministically, and on those sections the line itself moves.** On **6 of 200** sections the two reported endpoints come out swapped: PA000012, PA000347, PA000699 and PA001069 left tibia, PA000295 and PA000631 right/left femur. Four independent definitions agree on that set — directed tilt change > 90°, nearest-pairing crossed, `start` changing side of the divider, and the reported x-order reversing. (An independent review counted 7 under a nearest-pairing rule, adding PA000147 and PA000510 right tibia and dropping PA000631; the discrepancy was not resolved and is about near-degenerate pairings — PA000147's undirected change is 0.060° — rather than about the construction. The figure here is the one that four definitions reproduce.) The cause is structural: `rotate_mask_dorsal_points` rotates the section so the two posterior condylar points are level, so "the posterior-most point of the rotated section" is a **near-tie between the two condyles** — which the resampled image used to resolve by whatever its raster happened to give and which is now resolved by maximum rotated x. Two corrections to the obvious reading. First, it is a **near**-tie, not a tie: `get_dorsal_mask_point(knee=False)` returns `int(mean(x))` over the tied voxels, which need not be a mask voxel, so the two cortices are levelled only to within half a voxel — measurably, a far-side point is up to 0.499 voxels *more* posterior than the chosen start on 111 of 200 sections. That is why the half-voxel tie window is load-bearing rather than cosmetic. Second, the undirected line on those seven is **not** unchanged: it moves by a median 1.580°, max 2.764°, *above* the all-200 median of 0.567°, because when `start` jumps to the other condyle it also changes which far-side point becomes the tangent, and the cross-pairing residual is up to 8.2 voxels. They are not the large-movement tail either — only 1 of the top 10 movers is a flip, and the largest mover overall (PA001055 left tibia, 3.995°) is not one. What *is* true is that the swap is invisible to every consumer and to `landmarks.json`: `geometry.order_condylar_points` is canonical (`start[0] >= end[0]`) and is applied at all 5 call sites of `get_knee_reference_line` (7 ordering calls, including the two re-orderings after the mirror remap) before any landmark is returned.
   **It improves the mirror asymmetry without closing it.** A half and its mirror now give condylar lines differing by a median **0.33°**, max **2.06°**, where before it was 0.71° / 4.28°. **That figure is the line's BEARING and must not be read as bounding its LENGTH, which is far looser** (measured 2026-11-06, over the 952 Augsburg-large right halves, direct against reflected): the length changes on **827 of 952** femoral halves by a median +0.99 voxels (p5 -4.12, p95 +11.02, max |Δ| **17.02**) and on **800 of 951** tibial halves by a median -1.00 (p5 -11.04, p95 +6.30, max |Δ| **18.03**) — i.e. by more than the 14.30 voxels a short-line QC gate would threshold at. The two endpoints can slide along essentially the same line, which changes the length a great deal and the bearing hardly at all, so the tight bearing figure says nothing about length. Anything thresholding the condylar line's *length* must therefore measure it in the same frame the consumer does. The residue is the layer choice, the notch search and the dorsal-pair selection, not the read-out — so task #17's reflection (measuring a right half as the reflection of a left one) is still what makes the measurements equivariant, and the three places that attributed that asymmetry to "rotates the section about the image centre and rounds" have been corrected.
