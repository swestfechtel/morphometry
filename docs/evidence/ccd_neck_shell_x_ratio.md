# The MRI neck centre and `x_ratio`: the evidence

Why `x_ratio` is an empirical conditioning parameter of the MRI neck landmark rather than a unit
conversion, why the dimensionally correct value is measurably worse, and why the millimetre
reformulation is algebraically the worst member of the family. Moved verbatim out of `CLAUDE.md`,
which keeps the conclusion: the shipped 5.469 stands, the CCD's absolute level rests on this
parameter, and a genuine improvement needs a different neck landmark rather than a different unit.

The cohort sweep itself is `docs/ccd_x_ratio.md`, regenerated by `scripts/analyse_ccd_x_ratio.py`.

## The mechanism, the refuted millimetre premise and the literature check

**This was recorded as the open item — "sampling the neck shell in millimetres with a slice-aware thickness would remove the parameter" — and that premise is REFUTED by evidence already in this file (2026-11-06).** Sampling the shell in millimetres is **identical to the existing `x_ratio` path with `x_ratio = sz / sx`**, because the mm scale vector factorises as `sx · [1, 1, sz/sx]` and a *global* factor cannot change which points fall in a shell whose radii are fractions of `r` — `r` scales with it. On the Augsburg hip geometry that is `10.0 / 1.09375` = **9.143**, which is precisely the value the sweep already measured as substantially **worse**: moving 5.469 -> 9.143 takes the within-patient left/right Pearson from **+0.770 to +0.393**, mean |L−R| from 3.60 to 5.61°, and the reader bias from +3.73/+4.81 to +3.95/**+7.77**°. So "do it in millimetres" is not an unexplored route; it is the reader-free worst option in the family, and it was already rejected under a different name.

**And the "slice-aware thickness" half does not rescue it**: a naive mm conversion makes the shell *thinner* in slices, not thicker (on these hips `(OUTER−1)·r·sx / sz` = 0.44 slices against 0.73 at the shipped factor), so it would have to be widened by a new thickness parameter with **no principled value** — trading `x_ratio` for a different free parameter rather than removing one, and choosing it against the Augsburg data would be the `CEA_UPPER_MARGIN_FACTOR` mistake a third time.

**The premise behind that item is substantially weakened by the literature (checked 2026-11-06), the same way the alpha angle's was.** This file compares the ~138° cohort mean against a "125-135° literature range" — but that range is **cadaveric**, measured with a 360° goniometer on anatomical specimens (global mean 126.4°, SD ~5.6°, i.e. roughly mean ± 1.6 SD rather than a 95% interval), and the paper that establishes modern reference values says outright that prior estimates came mostly from anatomic specimens, small cohorts or hospital populations. The **in-vivo, general-population 95% reference interval is 114°-140°** (Acta Orthopaedica 2019; 115-140 female, 113-140 male), the widely used pathological cut-offs are **coxa valga >140° and coxa vara <120°**, and independent imaging cohorts sit well above 126: a CT study of Chinese Han adults gives mean **133.02°** (SD 4.49, range 118.74-143.15) and an upright-radiograph study **131.21° ± 4.72**. So our 138.4° is **high-normal and inside the in-vivo interval**, below the coxa valga line — not an out-of-range value. Two things that cut the other way and keep the item open rather than closing it: 138.4° is the **minimum of the whole `x_ratio` family** (the sweep runs 153.5° at 1.0 down to 138.4° at the shipped 5.469 and flattens at 139-140), so the level is still set by a conditioning parameter rather than by anatomy; and no MRI-specific protocol or MRI-derived normal values were found, so there is still no plane- and protocol-matched comparator — the same gap as the alpha angle's.

**What changes is the motivation, not the design**: the redesign is worth doing to remove the parameter, not to rescue a value that is out of range, because it is not. And it remains the one item here that should **not** be built speculatively — replacing the landmark produces a number with nothing to validate it against, on exactly the measurement whose reference range is least settled. Sources: `tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17453674.2019.1690873` (the 114-140 interval), `radiopaedia.org/articles/femoral-neck-shaft-angle` (the cadaveric 125-135 and the cut-offs), `PMC4616413` (the CT cohort).
