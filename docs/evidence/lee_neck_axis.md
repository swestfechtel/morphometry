# Lee's proximal neck axis: the index-frame invariance work

How `contour_femoral_neck` came to depend on the array bounds, what the replacement does, and how
the three crop residuals were closed one at a time. Moved verbatim out of `CLAUDE.md`, which keeps
the replacement construction, the quirks that were deliberately preserved, the `_NECK_ENDPOINT_ROUNDED`
lever, the residual grid-phase sensitivity and the do-not-pool burden.

The closing result: 5 of 100 halves reproduced their own landmark under a pad before, 100 of 100
after, and with the last residual fixed the Lee neck centre follows a margin-0 femur-bbox crop on
100 of 100. The package now contains no image-rotating landmark transport at all.

**A note on cross-references.** These paragraphs were written inside `CLAUDE.md`, so an
"above", "below" or "recorded here" may point at a bullet that stayed there rather than at
something on this page. Nothing was reworded, which is the point; the file it came from is
`CLAUDE.md` and the two are meant to be read together.

## What the old two-rotation notch search did, and which half leaked

**Lee's proximal axis is index-frame invariant, and that closes the task #9 family (2026-11-05).** `femur.py::contour_femoral_neck` located the intercondylar notch by **rotating the selected 2-D layer twice** (`utils.rotate_mask_dorsal_points`, i.e. `skimage.transform.rotate(mask, -angle, resize=True, preserve_range=True) > 0`), searching the **resampled** raster, and mapping the result back through shape-derived offsets (`rot_offset`, `rot_center`, both `int()`-truncated). All of that served one scalar: `a = |notch − head_centre| / r`, which sets the two radii `r_1 = (a − 0.1)·r`, `r_2 = (a + 0.1)·r` that crop the neck contour — the mapped-back notch's **direction is discarded**, which is what licensed removing the map-back entirely.

**Both halves of the mechanism leaked**, measured over the 100 Augsburg reader-study halves: the *first* rotation angle was already **exactly invariant (100/100)**, but the second — computed on the already-resampled raster — was exact on only **9/100** (median 1.40°, max **21.19°**), the mapped-back notch on **0/100** (median 1.11, max 9.77 voxels), and `a` on **0/100** (median 0.046, max 0.527). So correcting only the map-back would not have worked. Three of those four figures were reproduced to the digit by two independently written probes.

## Exposure on the production path

**Exposure, on the production path.** Worst over four pads at each end, 100 halves: the Lee neck centre was reproduced on **5 of 100** (median 2.5, p90 7.1, **max 30 voxels**) and the proximal axis bearing on 5 of 100 (median 1.56°, p90 4.70°, **max 20.27°**); 66 of 100 moved by more than 1° and 10 by more than 5°. A **high-end** pad — which changes no anatomy index at all, so a correct landmark must be *bit-identical* rather than merely shifted — is the sharper and strictly worse probe (max 30 voxels / 20.27° against the low-end pad's 21 / 14.93), the same probe task #5 quotes for the condylar line. For scale the Lee torsion's cohort SD is 14.58°, so the worst case is comparable to the measurement's entire spread. The head centre contributes **nothing** (padding moves it by ≤ 4.2e-12 voxels; 100% of the movement was the Lee path).

## Three array-bound guards clamped at zero cost

**Three array-bound guards were clamped at the same time, at zero cost, and that is what the crop figures rest on.** The ring crop in `contour_femoral_neck` and the `r_3` restriction in `get_femoral_neck_center_lee` each **skipped** their nulling when the circle reached past the array edge (`if center[1] + c_a < mask.shape[1]`, `if center[1] - c_a > 0`) rather than clamping it, and both loops ran `range(shape[0] - 1)`, leaving the last sagittal row never nulled. All of it fires on **0 of 100** halves in the production framing, so clamping and extending the range is **zero-movement** — verified identical on all 100, torsion and endpoint, max 0.0, with no golden moving. Under a crop to the mask bounding box it is the difference between **3 of 96** and **86 of 96** exact. (The dead `x` / `y` lists in the `r_3` loop went with it: appended to, never read.)

## The layer choice was the second crop residual

**The layer choice was the crop residual, and it is now closed too (2026-11-06).** Translation was already at 100/100 and, given the same layer, the notch frame, the notch cell and `a` are exact on 100/100 under a crop as well — but the layer loop itself was array-bounds dependent, and the fix is one line. Measured over the 100 Augsburg reader-study halves with the femur cropped to its own bounding box at **margin 0** (bone sitting *on* the border, the only framing in which this fires): the selected layer survived on **88 of 100**, `find_contours`'s count flipped on **12** (1 → 2 on nine halves, 1 → 3 on three) and the `2 · r` circle test flipped on **0**, with the head centre and radius **bit-identical** on all 100.

**Only one of the two tests was ever responsible**, contrary to the attribution this file carried: `skimage.measure.find_contours` does not close a region reaching the array edge — it returns one *open* polyline per disjoint interval of border contact, so bone on two or three sides reads as two or three contours, the single-outline test fails and the walk runs on (7 → 8, 8 → 10, 18 → 21 twice, 18 → 19, 8 → 9, 6 → 7 twice, 6 → 8, 7 → 9 twice, 16 → 17). `points_on_circle` **could not** have been responsible and should not be "fixed" for it: it keeps a sample only if it is in bounds **and** on bone, so an out-of-bounds sample already *is* a not-bone sample, and a crop that preserves every bone voxel cannot change its verdict. The fix evaluates that one test on a `_NECK_LAYER_BORDER`-voxel zero border, which makes the layer a function of the bone alone.

## Why the layer fix is zero-movement, and the four halves it recovered

**It is zero-movement, and it recovers four halves that used to fail outright.** In the production framing the same layer is selected on **100 of 100** uncropped halves, and the Lee neck centre and the proximal axis bearing are **bit-identical to the pre-change code on all 100** (max |Δ| exactly 0.0, no resolve/fail change); `git diff tests/golden/` is empty, which matters because four Lee goldens read this path. End to end under the margin-0 crop the shipped code was measurable on 96 of 100 (four raised `RuntimeError`) and followed the crop on **86 of 96**, max error **54 voxels**; the bordered code is measurable on **100 of 100** — so the four that stopped resolving a notch were the same defect — and follows the crop on **98 of 100**, max error **1 voxel**.

**It was not 100/100, and the residual turned out to be a different mechanism from the one this file twice blamed.** The two that still moved were `PA000012` image-right and `PA000667` image-left, which both `9f41e68` and this bullet attributed to 3-D `utils.get_contour`'s `binary_erosion(border_value=0)` reporting the array border as contour.

**That attribution was wrong** (corrected 2026-11-06, see the next bullet): traced stage by stage, the head centre, the layer, the contour (4258 and 5151 voxels), `r_1`/`r_2`/`a`, `center_fn`, `r_3`, the surviving contour point set *and* the fitted slope are all identical between the uncropped and cropped arrays — only the final rounding of the reported endpoint differed. Bordering `get_contour` would in any case have been the wrong move: it feeds `get_contour_points`, which the alpha angle (`measurements/hip.py:594`) and the femoral-head sphere-fit point cloud (`hip.py:404`) also use, so it would have moved the head centre and with it every hip measurement. Quote the claim as **“invariant under translation, and under any crop that does not leave bone on the array border”**. Note also that the two figures are different quantities and must not be conflated: the LAYER index goes 88 → 100 of 100, while the end-to-end neck centre goes 86/96 → 98/100, because the second mechanism survives.

## The third crop residual: int() truncation toward zero

**The last crop residual was `int()` truncating toward zero, and it is now closed (2026-11-06).** `get_femoral_neck_center_lee` reports a point `_NECK_AXIS_REACH` (80) voxels back along the fitted neck axis from the head centre — a *synthetic far point*, not a landmark on bone, so it routinely falls outside the array. It was rounded with `int()`, which truncates **toward zero**: it rounds a positive argument down and a negative one up, so it does **not** commute with adding an integer across zero. Cropping drops `center[1]` below the axis reach, the coronal argument lands in `(-1, 0]`, and `int()` rounds it **up** to 0 where `np.floor` gives −1 and reproduces the uncropped answer. This is the hazard the "physical coordinates stay floats" convention below already records ("casting mm coordinates to `int` truncates toward zero, which makes a measurement depend on the image's position in the scanner frame") — biting an **index** coordinate, which is why it was not looked for there. The fix is `np.floor`, and it is **zero-movement**: over the 100 Augsburg reader-study halves both arguments are non-negative on all 100 in the production framing, so `int` and `floor` agree exactly there, and the Lee neck centre and proximal axis bearing are **bit-identical to the pre-change code on all 100** with an empty `tests/golden/` diff. Under the margin-0 femur-bbox crop the coronal argument falls in `(-1, 0]` on exactly those two halves, and the neck centre now follows the crop on **100 of 100**, max error **0.000 voxels** — so the Lee crop invariance is complete: 86/96 before the layer fix, 98/100 after it, 100/100 now. The sagittal argument is `-80 + center[0]` with `center` already `int16`, i.e. an exact integer, so its sign never mattered; it is floored too only so the pair reads consistently. Pinned by `tests/characterization/test_lee_endpoint_rounding.py`, whose two parametrisations fail against the pre-change code with exactly the coronal off-by-one.

## One dead expression, since repaired

**One dead expression, recorded here so it is not mistaken for the defect, and since repaired**: `utils.points_on_circle` used to end `return np.array(points) > 0`, returning a **boolean array** rather than the points it collected. Only `len(...) > 0` was ever read and `femur.py` is its only caller, so it was harmless, and it was listed here as a separate tidying item — **it was then fixed in `d980792` (2026-11-06) and the function now returns `np.array(points)`**, so this paragraph describes history and not the shipped code. The same commit is the one the "three advertised-but-broken paths" bullet above records; do not read the two as disagreeing about the current return value.

## A failure path improved on the way

**A failure path improved on the way**, untested on data because it never fires on these 100 halves: when no second dorsal point existed the old code raised `IndexError` (`indices[-1]` on an empty contour), which is **not** in the `(RuntimeError, AssertionError, ValueError)` tuple the batch scripts and `compute_torsion.py` catch — so it would have aborted a cohort run instead of writing a NaN row. It now raises `RuntimeError`.

## Lee neck axis — full bullets (from CLAUDE.md)

*Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-11-12 when that file was cut below its size limit; `CLAUDE.md` keeps a summary and links here. Nothing below was rewritten.*

- **The femoral-torsion method labelled "Lee" is Schock et al.'s, and its gap with Murphy is anatomy rather than error** (`femur.py::get_femoral_neck_center_lee`; task #2, closed 2026-11-04 as **premise refuted**, not fixed). What is implemented is the automatic method of **Schock et al., Sci Rep 11:23244 (2021)** — the paper this pipeline derives from — which its authors describe as *"closest to the method of Lee"* and not as Lee's method: they define the neck **axis** as the reference line where Lee et al. (Med Biol Eng Comput 44:895–906, 2006) define the neck **centre**. So the contour-fitted neck axis is the published design, not a misreading, and the label is a shorthand. **A proximal neck landmark reads systematically lower than a distal one**, because the distal femoral neck is more posterior — Schock et al.'s own radiologists measured **16.1° by Lee against 32.2° by Murphy** (their algorithm vs their Lee reader: p = 0.859, not significantly different), and Lee et al. give the law `θ = 25.3·(L/D) − 5.4` for `L/D` 0.1–0.6, `L` being the distal distance from the proximal-most neck confluence and `D` the head diameter (mean 42.8 mm). On the 50 Augsburg reader-study hips this method's slice sits a median **12.8 mm** below the head centre and Murphy's **44.9 mm** — a 32.1 mm separation, for which that law predicts **≈19°** against the **18.97°** measured (our Lee 11.59°, Murphy 30.55°; their Murphy 32.2°). Two things that look alarming and are not: the **~21% of legs called retrotorted** is 21.0% observed against **21.3% expected** from this method's own mean and SD (11.59 ± 14.58°), i.e. pure arithmetic of a lower-reading method; and the **−6.1°/−5.9° "bias"** against the Augsburg readers on sign-unambiguous legs is a definitional difference, not an error. **What does not reconcile is the readers' Lee−Murphy gap of 4.3–5.8°**, which is ~11° too *narrow* for landmarks this far apart by both the pipeline and two independent publications; `docs/augsburg_reader_bias.md` carries a retraction of the conclusions that rested on it. **Do not "fix" this method against the reader data.** A candidate that replaced the contour fit with the femur centroid on the same slice reproduced the readers' gap almost exactly (−4.48° against their −5.10°) and was nearly adopted — it would have moved a correctly-implemented published method away from its own definition to match reference values that contradict the literature, the same trap `CEA_UPPER_MARGIN_FACTOR` records. Separately, the shipped `int16` cast of the head centre on this branch is what makes the method sensitive to the dependency stack (see the torsion-image bullet above).
- **The replacement, in three parts.** The angle is accumulated on **coordinates** (no raster), so it inherits the first pass's exact invariance; the notch raster is sampled **once** on a grid anchored at `rint(head centre)` with `map_coordinates(order=0, mode='grid-constant', cval=0)`; and because `a` is a **distance**, and distances survive rotation, it is computed in that frame directly — the head centre is the grid origin by construction, so there is **no map-back, no `rot_offset`, no `rot_center` and no `int()` truncation**. `mode='grid-constant'` is deliberate and not interchangeable with `'constant'`, which returns `cval` outside the *closed* `[0, n−1]` and so discards the outer half voxel of every axis — itself an array-end dependence, measured at 15° on a shallow-FOV case in the alpha-angle work. The invariance is argued, not merely measured: under a translation `t` the anchor and the mask points shift together, so the rotated coordinates are **bitwise** identical and the sampled coordinates shift by exactly `t`; and the 1-voxel window margin is *provably* sufficient, because a border cell is ≥ 1 from every rotated mask point while nearest-neighbour rounding reaches at most 0.707.
- **Result: 5 of 100 → 100 of 100 exact, max 0.** No half gains or loses a value, the notch-search failure rate is 0/100 before and after, and the **Murphy** torsion is bit-identical on all 90 measurable halves — so nothing outside the Lee path moved. `tests/characterization/test_head_centre_translation.py`'s two strict xfails for exactly this defect now pass and were un-xfailed; `test_compute_torsion_pipeline.py` and `test_femoral_torsion_equivariance.py` both exercise Lee and both still pass, so mirror-equivariance survives.
- **The endpoint is still rounded at all**, which the "floats stay floats" rule would argue against since nothing indexes it — making it a float would move every Lee value, so it is left at the shipped behaviour — but it is **no longer an open task**: the rounding is named `femur.py::_NECK_ENDPOINT_ROUNDED` (shipped `True`, zero movement), so flipping it is one edit, and **the size of that move is bounded analytically (2026-11-06)**: the endpoint is `floor(-80 + cx), floor(-80·m + cy)`, so its x component is *exactly* `-_NECK_AXIS_REACH` from the head centre and only the y component is perturbed, by an amount in `[0, 1)` voxels. With `dx = -80` fixed, that tilts the reported bearing — which is the only thing used — by at most `atan(1/80)` = **0.716°**, with an expected **0.358°**. So de-rounding would move every Lee value, but by **under three quarters of a degree**, against this method's cohort SD of 14.24° and against the 1.430° median that task #9's own change moved it by. That is a bound from the construction, not a cohort measurement, and it is the reliable form here: **a probe that monkeypatched `np.floor` to measure this empirically is not trustworthy and its numbers are deliberately not recorded** — patching it globally perturbs every floor in the call stack, and the tell was that only 24 of 100 halves still resolved. A faithful empirical probe needs the endpoint computation reimplemented, since the rounding is inline; the analytic bound makes that unnecessary for deciding.
- **Two quirks of the old slicing were preserved rather than quietly fixed**, and both are preserved-not-endorsed: the side bands are asymmetric by one voxel (`>= divider + 3` against `<= divider - 4`), and the far-side branch carries a **three-voxel shear** (the old code sliced at `thresh + 3` and added back `thresh`, so the dorsal line is sheared by three voxels on that branch). Removing the shear would move the Lee torsion on **13 of 100** halves by a median 1.15° / max 10.34° — measured and reported, deliberately not done, for the same reason task #2 was closed as premise-refuted and the no-notch condylar fallback kept its construction.
- **A floating-point subtlety that is easy to get wrong in exactly the opposite direction.** The old divider was `int()` of a centre of mass, and it is tempting to argue that is already translation-exact because `floor(m + t) = floor(m) + t` for integer `t`. That holds in exact arithmetic and **not** in floating point: `mean(points + t)` and `mean(points) + t` can land either side of an integer, which costs up to **0.32°** of frame angle on 3 of 100 halves under a crop. The replacement floors in the frame **relative to the integer origin** — the same quantity, computed on a value that is bitwise invariant under translation — and uses `np.floor` rather than `int()` for the same reason. Do not “simplify” it back.
- **One resample is unavoidable, and that was measured rather than assumed.** `find_notch` thresholds the **number of empty cells along a raster row** (`thresh=5`), which — unlike task #5's divider, an extremum of a point set — has no point-set equivalent without redefining "gap" as a continuous distance. The coordinate-only analogue (rotate the mask points, round to integers) loses a mean **7.3%** of the mask area to aliasing (min 77.2% retained) and breaks the notch search on **15 of 100** halves, where an `order=0` resample retains 100.03% and resolves 100/100.
- **The notch search is the same code but no longer the same test, and this is the dominant cause of the movement tail.** The old `rotate(..., order=1) > 0` **dilated** the mask by a one-voxel fringe — measured **+7.9%** of area on the real selected layers, against +0.03% for nearest-neighbour sampling — and because `find_notch` counts *empty* cells, `thresh=5` on a dilated raster is effectively `thresh=7` on an undilated one. The constant is unchanged; what it tests is not. **Do not "restore" the old behaviour by raising `thresh`**: that would reintroduce a resampling artefact as a tuned constant.
- **One leg is made measurably worse, and it is the whole tail.** `PA000346` image-left goes **35.755° → 3.168°** — the single mover above 10° and the entire maximum. Its `a` falls to **0.768**, i.e. `r_1 = 0.67 r`, so the ring is drawn closer to the head axis than the head's own radius rather than peripheral to it, and is far too small to follow the neck contour the fit is meant to use — on that half the notch sits 6.2–16.1 mm from the head centre against 32.2 and 33.1 mm on PA000001's two sound halves. It is the only reader-study half below `a = 1.0` (next lowest 1.199). **This sentence used to say "the whole ring sits inside the fitted femoral head and the kept contour is head cortex, not neck", and that stronger claim is false — corrected 2026-11-06 after it was measured.** `a` is an in-plane distance in the *selected axial layer*, while the head is a sphere: the head's cross-section at that layer has radius `sqrt(r² − dz²)`, and the Lee layer is the most proximal qualifying one, so it sits well off the equator. Measured with the unrounded head centre, that cross-section is 0.516 / 0.496 / 0.000 / 0.332 of `r` on the four Augsburg-large halves the gate flags (PA000346 left, PA000622 right, PA000810 right, PA000932 left), so the notch is **outside** it on three of the four, and on PA000810 the layer lies below the fitted sphere entirely. A true containment test would flag 1 of the 4. So `a < 1` is the deliberately *more inclusive* reading and the defect is "the ring is too small to be on the neck", not "the ring is inside the head" — which is also why the shipped flag is named `lee_ring_within_head_radius` rather than `lee_ring_in_head`, the verdict it is modelled on (`murphy_base_in_head`) being a genuine 3-D containment test in millimetres. The gate and the value 1.0 are unaffected; only the mechanism was weaker than it first looked. Those four fractions were produced by the implementer and reproduced exactly by the lead (0.516 / 0.496 / 0.000 / 0.332, outside on three of four). **Probe hazard, since the first reproduction attempt got it wrong**: `r` is in **in-plane voxels** while the layer index is in **slices**, so `dz` must be scaled by `spacing_z / spacing_x` (9.14 on this cohort) before being compared with `r`. Omitting that gives `xsec/r ≈ 0.99` on all four halves and the opposite conclusion — the same slice-versus-in-plane-voxel confusion task #10 is about, here biting a *probe* rather than a measurement. That layer was already poor before the change: its old `a` of 1.190 was the cohort minimum and its contour fit had **7 points against a cohort median of 53**. Its own neighbouring eligible layer reads 1.753 under **both** implementations, so the new answer is the one that disagrees with the leg. The old 1.190 was already the cohort minimum, so that layer was poor before — but this change makes it worse, and the 33.3° must not be quoted as an unqualified movement. An **`a < 1` QC condition** catches it, analogous to `murphy_base_in_head`, and **is now implemented (2026-11-06) after the prevalence survey it was waiting on**. The gate it is modelled on earned its thresholds from a survey over 2128 image halves across three cohorts, and all that existed here was 1 of 100 — so the item was deliberately held until the same evidence base existed. It now does, on three cohorts, and the gate **flags without repairing**: a flagged leg keeps its Lee torsion, exactly as `murphy_axis_short` does.
  - `femur.py::lee_neck_axis_qc(notch_distance_factor, head_radius=None)` is the pure verdict (a dict, with the `;`-joined `flags` string); `femur.py::get_lee_neck_qc(hip_image, side, ...)` is the never-raising wrapper the scripts call, mirroring `get_murphy_neck_qc` including its `except Exception` -> `_landmark_failure_flag` path, which is **not stylistic** — in both Augsburg scripts the QC calls sit outside any `try` and an escape would abort the cohort run (see the handler bullet at the end of this file). `_landmark_failure_flag` gained a `method` parameter and the shared reason table five **appended** Lee codes — `neck_layer_not_found`, `notch_not_found`, `neck_layer_empty`, `neck_section_empty`, `dorsal_point_not_found` — plus the pre-existing `anisotropic_in_plane`, reported as `lee_landmark_failed:<reason>`. Appended rather than inserted, so every Murphy code is provably unchanged; an unrecognised message still surfaces as the exception type, so a new failure mode stays visible rather than silent. `get_femoral_neck_center_lee` gained an optional return flag (defaulting off, the `get_femoral_head_center(return_layers=...)` precedent) so the QC reads the **same `a` the measurement used** rather than re-deriving it through a parallel copy of the layer search, which could drift. It refuses in-plane anisotropic spacing (`geometry.validate_in_plane_isotropy`): `a` is dimensionless, but `|notch - head centre|` is an index-space distance while `r` is in in-plane voxels, so the ratio only means something when the two in-plane edges agree. **The gate's cohort survey and its verification are in [`docs/evidence/qc_gates.md`](docs/evidence/qc_gates.md).**
  - The single flag is **`lee_ring_within_head_radius:<a>`** — named for the head *radius*, not the head, because the containment reading is false (see the correction above). Threshold `C.LEE_MIN_NOTCH_DISTANCE_FACTOR = 1.0`, whose comment carries the full derivation and disclosure. **Justified three ways, and the constant says which is weakest.** (i) Geometry gives a *family*, not a point: the ring crosses the head radius at `a` = 0.9 (outer edge), 1.0 (centre) and 1.1 (inner edge), so the honest phrase is "geometric family, data-broken tie" and not "the data had no say". (ii) The Augsburg large cohort breaks the tie: over the **1896 of 1908** halves that resolve, min 0.3089, median 1.6222, max 2.2406, with a gap from **0.8434 to 1.0192** so every threshold in [0.844, 1.019] flags exactly the same **4 halves (0.211%)** — PA000346 left 0.7684, PA000622 right 0.8434, PA000810 right 0.3265, PA000932 left 0.3089. For scale, 1.05 and 1.1 flag 6, 1.15 flags 11, 1.20 flags 16, 1.30 flags 51. (iii) It **replicates on two further cohorts and fires on neither**: the 50-patient reader study 100/100 resolving, min 0.7684 (the same half), next 1.1995, median 1.5529, **1 of 100**; and the 60-case NaKo sample through the isotropic path, 120/120 resolving, min **1.1388**, median 1.5231, **0 of 120** below 1.0 or even 1.1. So it is specific rather than trigger-happy, and NaKo's minimum clears the threshold by 14%.
  - **That transferability is a property of the quantity, not of the gate design**, and this repo has the counterexample: `a` is a dimensionless *ratio*, so its distribution is comparable across 1 mm isotropic and 1.09375 × 1.09375 × 10 mm data, whereas the condylar analogue would be a length **in voxels** and does not transfer at all (see the condylar-line paragraph above). Do not generalise "there is a gap" from one to the other.
  - Wired into `scripts/process_augsburg_{reader_study,large_dataset}.py` — a new `_join_flags` helper puts the Murphy and Lee families in one `qc_flags` cell, Murphy first, dropping empties so a sound half stays exactly `''` and tables produced before the gate stay comparable wherever no Lee flag fires — and into `morphometry/docker/compute_torsion.py` as `femoral_torsion_{left,right}_lee_qc_flags`. **The API still discards these**, as it does the Murphy ones (`ResultsModel` has `extra="ignore"`), so the new keys are visible only when the container is run by hand with a mounted output dir.
- **The case for this change is invariance, not accuracy — there is no second cue.** The within-leg corroboration that decided the condylar-line work is a **dead heat, marginally worse**: the agreement between each half's `a` and that of its own neighbouring eligible layers is a median 0.104 before and 0.119 after, with the new answer closer on 49 halves and further on 50 (an independent run of the same comparison gives 0.076/0.108 old against 0.089/0.127 new, closer on 41 and further on 58 of 99 — a dead heat either way, and both runs are quoted rather than one being picked). Within-patient left/right agreement moves the same way and is likewise not significant: mean |L−R| 6.605 → 7.138°, Pearson 0.838 → 0.805, Wilcoxon p = 0.24. Read it the way this file reads the knee-rotation equivariance: the justification is frame-independence and the removal of two `int()` truncations, not a correction. The one non-golden assertion on this path (`test_torsion_head_posterior.py`, `|angle − reader mean| < 12°`) moves in **opposite** directions on its two cases — `PA001023` left's headroom improves 4.69° → 10.41°, `PA000147` right's degrades 8.64° → **2.92°** — which is itself a reason not to read the reader comparison as evidence either way, consistent with task #2 having closed that premise as refuted. 2.92° is **not** a comfortable margin and the test docstring says so.
- **Residual, stated so "anatomy-anchored" is not over-read.** The grid's phase relative to the anatomy follows `rint(head centre)`, so the notch is a function of the anatomy **and** of the head centre's rounded cell: shifting that anchor by one voxel moves `a` by a median **0.013** (p90 0.035–0.053, max **0.247**), roughly **half** the magnitude of the dependence just removed, with the notch still resolving 100/100 in every direction. This is not a regression — the old code had the same sensitivity with the *array* centre in place of the head centre, which is precisely what made it position-dependent — and it is not a defect, because the head centre is itself now translation-invariant to 4e-12 voxels, so the phase no longer moves when the array does. The forward-looking consequence is the useful half: **any future change to the MRI head fit will move every Lee value again, at about half the scale of this change.** Anchoring on the unrounded centre (as the alpha angle does) would make the phase continuous rather than stepped, removing the 0.247 discontinuity; it was considered and declined, because it would move every value a third time and trade a provable exact-integer-offset argument for ~1e-14 empirical invariance while leaving the sensitivity itself intact.
- **Do not pool Lee rows produced before and after this change.** Over the 100 reader-study halves `a` moves on **100 of 100** (median 0.0269, p90 0.0862, max 0.597) while the proximal axis and the Lee torsion move on **60 of 100** (median **1.430°** over the movers, p90 4.271, max **32.587°**; 6 above 5°, 1 above 10°, and the **second largest is 6.439°** — the maximum is a lone outlier, `PA000346`, discussed above); the median over all 100 including the 40 unchanged is 0.683°. Cohort mean **+11.395 → +11.287°**, SD 14.535 → 14.240, **one sign flip** (`PA000007` left −0.956 → +1.115, a near-zero value), and **no half gained or lost a value**. The smallest nonzero movement is 0.604°, consistent with the quantisation below, so the 40 "unchanged" halves are unchanged *to within the quantisation* and not unaffected — `a` moved on every one of the 100. **Reverting an earlier, incidental change to the first pass halved this**: before that revert the median over the movers was 1.42°, which is the measure of how much of a do-not-pool burden an unforced deviation can add.. Note the bearing is **quantised**: the reported endpoint's x-component is exactly −80 on all 100 halves and its y is an integer, so the granularity is 0.36–0.72° and a genuine sub-voxel improvement in `a` can register as **exactly zero movement** — "unchanged on N halves" therefore does not mean "unaffected on N halves". Four goldens were re-captured deliberately and only four read this path (the suite was run *before* re-capturing, so the failures enumerated them): `femoral_torsion_left_lee` 7.409439 → **6.025752**, `femoral_torsion_right_lee` 11.643151 → **13.043961**, `femoral_torsion_PA000147_right_lee` 21.487695 → **15.766445**, `femoral_torsion_PA001023_left_lee` 19.885816 → **25.607066**; both Murphy goldens are bit-identical, and an md5 diff over all 49 golden files confirms nothing else moved. The capture was made by running **only the four failing tests** under `MORPH_UPDATE_GOLDEN=1`, so nothing else was even evaluated — the discipline that matters, because that variable rewrites every golden it evaluates and swept up unrelated drift twice earlier in this work. **The workings — the two-rotation mechanism and which half of it leaked, the padding exposure, and the three crop residuals (the array-bound guards, the layer choice and the `int()` truncation) closed one at a time — are in [`docs/evidence/lee_neck_axis.md`](docs/evidence/lee_neck_axis.md).**
- **Dead code removed with it**, which is the closing move of the family: `utils.rotate_mask_dorsal_points`, `utils.rotate_mask_by_angle` and `utils.transform_point` had no remaining callers and are gone, and with them the `skimage.transform.rotate` import — **so the package now contains no image-rotating landmark transport at all**. `utils.rotate_point` stays (used by `vec_parallel_rotation_angle`), as do `dorsal_points_rotation_angle` and `get_dorsal_mask_point` (used by `knee.py`'s coordinate path). `rotate_indices` moved from `knee.py` to `geometry.py` so `femur.py` can use it without importing `knee.py`, which `tests/test_import_graph.py` forbids.
