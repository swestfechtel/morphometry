# Knee cartilage: subregions, thickness, surfaces, deformation maps

*Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-11-12 when that file was cut below its size limit; `CLAUDE.md` keeps a summary and links here. Nothing below was rewritten.*

- Both `extract_subregions` enforce subregion contiguity (`_remove_subregion_fragments`, keep 26-connectivity components ≥ 20% of the largest): a subregion is one anatomical patch, but the nearest-tAB-triangle transfer can hand a voxel a region its own patch does not touch (across the intercondylar notch, say). Both bones now subdivide on the bone surface, so this is only a safety net; it is a no-op on clean scans (single-component subregions), and it affects only the voxel point clouds (`plot_segments`), never the thickness, which is measured on the surfaces.
- **Tibial subregions follow Wirth & Eckstein 2008** (`morphometry/cartilage/tibia_regions.py::subdivide_tibia`, plan step 2; replaced the voxel-sphere/bounding-box scheme outright, no legacy switch). They are defined on the tibial subchondral bone area (tAB, `surface.py`) in world mm, so `Tibia` now needs the **tibial bone label** (`bone_label=2`; `tibia_bone_label=2` on `calculate_tibial_cartilage_thickness` / `calculate_knee_cartilage_thickness` / `calculate_thinnest_cartilage_area`; `--tibia-bone-label` on `compute_knee_cartilage_thickness.py` and `analyse_thinnest_point_flexion.py`); a missing bone label raises. Plates = the two largest edge-connected cAB components (`find_plates`; second ≥ `min_plate_ratio` 0.2 × largest, else a fallback area-weighted 2-means split of the largest — never fired on the 96 `dfg_knees`), named by **patient side from RAS world x** (smaller x = patient left; this rests on the header, as before). Other covered islands are dropped unless enclosed by a plate (median 0.15 mm², max 0.66 mm² per knee over the 94 usable knees; 15 mm² on the broken K7_90_belastet). tAB is hole-filled **per plate** (`total_subchondral_area` with only that plate's cAB covered), so the intercondylar area can never be filled even if the cartilage plates touch. Per plate: centre of gravity S and principal axes b1..b3 from the **exact area-weighted triangle second moments** (`area_moments`; the paper uses vertices), b3 = normal oriented superior, right-handed. Central (`c`) = elliptical cylinder along b3, `rho = sqrt((x/σ1)² + (y/σ2)²)` with σ1, σ2 the in-plane standard deviations (our reading of the paper's unspecified λ1/λ2: a uniform elliptical plate gives a scaled copy of its outline), level found by bisection on the exactly cut mesh to 20% (`central_fraction`) of the plate tAB ± 1e-5. Peripheral = two planes through S containing b3 at ±45° to `tc` (S→other S, projected into the plate plane): internal (`i`) faces the other plate, external (`e`) opposite, anterior/posterior (`a`/`p`) by world +y (no fibula landmark, the paper's reference). Cartilage voxels take the region of the nearest tAB triangle centroid (`TibiaSubdivision.region_codes_at`, world mm), stored per point in `Tibia.point_labels`. Transfer QC: `Tibia.transfer_qc` (`tibia_regions.transfer_qc`: median/p99/max voxel-to-tAB distance, `far_fraction` beyond `TRANSFER_FAR_MM` 6 mm) and a WARNING on the `morphometry` logger when `far_fraction` > `TRANSFER_MAX_FAR_FRACTION` (1%) — clean `dfg_knees` max 4.8 mm, K7_90_belastet 49% beyond 6 mm (max 45 mm), the only knee flagged; `classify_point` is a single lookup. `Tibia.plot_regions()` draws the labelled tAB. On the 96 `dfg_knees` (1 failure, K11_90_belastet, broken segmentation: 1 mm voxels, tibia label fills the volume; K7_90_belastet runs but its tibial cartilage is ~¼ of the unloaded scan's) the region shares of the plate tAB are, patient-left / patient-right plate (n=94): c 20.0/20.0, e 16.9/15.9, i 15.6/14.1, a 23.8/25.0, p 23.8/25.0 % (paper MT/LT: c 20/20, e 16.0/17.7, i 15.6/17.5, a 24.2/22.6, p 24.1/22.2); belastet vs unbelastet same subject/angle: median |Δshare| 0.14 pp (max 1.3); across flexion angles within one subject/load: median SD 0.21 pp. All of these re-derive from `scripts/report_knee_cartilage_cohort.py` to the decimal shown (on its default 93-scan clean set as well as on this 94-scan one). Subdivision alone ~1-2 s per bone, a whole knee including both bones' thickness ~15 s single-process on a 32-core workstation (the ~3 s / ~20 s recorded earlier were a different machine; timings are not a regression signal). **Naming / laterality**: keys stay patient-side (`clt … plt` = patient-LEFT plate, `crt … prt` = patient-RIGHT; display `cLT`/`cRT`) whatever the laterality, so `find_thinnest_area`, `split_tibia_thickness_by_compartment`, plotting and the scripts are unchanged. `Tibia(laterality='left'|'right')` / `anatomical_tibia_names` / `rename_tibia_regions` give the paper's medial/lateral names (right knee: patient-left plate = MT; left knee: patient-right plate = MT) — there the `LT` means *lateral*, so `split_tibia_thickness_by_compartment` refuses any dict holding `*MT` keys. `compute_knee_cartilage_thickness.py --laterality-table CSV(subject,laterality)` adds an `anatomical_subregion` CSV column. **Do not pool tibial subregion rows produced before and after this change**: K1_0_unbelastet knn `clt` 1.885 -> 1.800 mm, `crt` 1.414 -> 1.311, `elt` 1.208 -> 1.370, `plt` 1.984 -> 1.729, `irt` voxels 916 -> 590. (The femoral rows moved then too, because their A-P window was cLT/cRT's extent; they have since been redefined outright, see the femoral bullet.) The voxel-sphere central region of 60425aa (and `calculate_ellipse`, `get_plate_corners`, the KMeans/BFS plate split, `tests/unit/test_tibia_central_region.py`) is gone; pinned now by `tests/unit/test_tibia_regions.py` (20% ± 1e-4, circular plate → four 20% wedges, invariance under 20° axial rotation / 7° slope / anisotropic + oblique sampling / SimpleITK, mirror swaps plates, enclosed intercondylar area not filled, denuded hole filled).
- `Tibia`/`Femur` clean their extracted cartilage mask before any processing (`_remove_mask_outliers`, `outlier_ratio=0.1` ctor arg): per-label 26-connectivity components smaller than the ratio of the total cartilage volume are dropped, removing mislabelled blobs / detached clusters. Legit cartilage (femoral horseshoe, the two tibial plateaus) is far larger than the threshold and retained; pass `outlier_ratio=0` to disable. **It now affects only the voxel point cloud** (`point_labels`, `extract_subregions`, `plot_segments`, the transfer QC): the subdivisions and the thickness read the label array directly, through `cartilage_coverage` / `articular_surface_mesh`. A stray blob is dropped from those by other means — the surfaces keep only the largest connected component of the bone (or of the bone+cartilage union), so a detached blob is not meshed at all, and a blob next to the bone can at most mark a few bone triangles covered.
- `find_thinnest_area` (re-exported; wrapper `calculate_thinnest_cartilage_area`) locates the thinnest *area* of the tibiofemoral cartilage robustly: it sums the tibial+femoral thickness maps over the in-plane cells present in *both* (the contact region), rasterises to a dense grid, NaN-aware median-smooths (kernel `k` derived from `neighbourhood_mm` / the frame's `grid_mm`, forced odd) to reject single-cell outliers, argmins for the neighbourhood centre, then refines to the actual thinnest cell within a radius disc. Positions are `(u, v)` cells of the knee's shared in-plane frame, **not voxel indices** (see the thickness bullet); both maps must be keyed in the same frame or it raises (`_overlap_spacing`), which is what stops a femur measured without a tibia from being paired up with a tibial map. **Read the warning on the function before using its output.** A cartilage map stops rather than fades, and `cartilage_coverage`'s distance test puts a band of near-zero cells at every cartilage edge, so the global argmin of a knee is almost always a rim cell. Two restrictions keep the search off it, both bearing on the same artefact: the neighbourhood-centre search is restricted to cells whose whole `k x k` window lies inside the overlap footprint (we are looking for the thinnest *area*, so the area has to be fully measured), **and the refinement disc now obeys the same restriction** — it did not, which is why the shipped step-4 code returned a rim cell on most knees: over the 93 clean `dfg_knees` the femoral thickness at the returned point had a median of 0.62 mm and was below 1 mm in 67% of results (tibial 0.71 mm, 79%) against real cartilage of 1.3-3.5 mm; with the refinement restricted those become 1.51 mm and 11%. **A residual remains at the extremes of flexion**: at 0° and 90° the contact genuinely runs off the end of the labelled condyle, so 18% and 41% of results still read a femoral thickness below 1 mm and the arc position of the point piles up against the trochlear or the posterior end — how much of that is the label edge and how much real contact geometry cannot be told from these segmentations. For that residual `edge_margin_mm` (distance from **each bone's own** map boundary) and `min_thickness_mm` exist, both **off by default**: every artefact indicator improves monotonically with the margin (0/2/3 mm → 14.0/11.1/5.7% of results below 1 mm femorally) because eroding the search region just drives the answer to the middle of the contact, so the data cannot choose a value, and for a flexion trend any value biases towards the null. Anything ≤ 1.5 mm is a no-op at the default neighbourhood and grid. If nothing survives, the whole overlap is searched and a WARNING is logged. Returns `{point, center, center_thickness, combined_thickness, tibial_thickness, femoral_thickness, kernel_size}` (`center_thickness` is the robust smoothed value, `combined_thickness` the raw value at `point`). `plot_thinnest_point` (re-exported; also `calculate_thinnest_cartilage_area(..., plot=pv.Plotter)`) renders the cartilage in 3D (`base='thickness'` heatmap or `base='segments'` voxels) and points a red arrow at the thinnest point — its 3D position is the midpoint of the two bones' articular surfaces recorded for that cell (`ThicknessMaps.positions`, `thinnest_point_position` — the former private `_thinnest_point_physical`, which now takes the two thickness maps rather than the `Tibia`/`Femur`). Given a `{label: result}` dict instead of a single result, it draws one labelled arrow per entry (`left` red, `right` blue, `both` black; `None` entries skipped).
- **Per-compartment thinnest area**: `find_thinnest_area_by_compartment` (re-exported; also `calculate_thinnest_cartilage_area(..., by_compartment=True)`) returns `{'left': result, 'right': result}` keyed by **patient** side, each with exactly the `find_thinnest_area` fields. `split_tibia_thickness_by_compartment` partitions the tibial thickness dict by key suffix (case-insensitive; `*LT` → left, `*RT` → right; unknown keys raise), and `find_thinnest_area` runs on each subset against the *full* femoral map — the overlap restriction confines each search to its plateau, so a globally thinner other side cannot win and smoothing never crosses the notch. An empty / non-overlapping side raises a `ValueError` naming it. Side mapping: `tibia_regions.find_plates` names the plate with the smaller RAS world x patient-left (checked against the affine on K1_0_belastet: after LPI `affine[0,0] < 0`, i.e. +index x → patient left), so it rests on the NIfTI header being correct. A dict re-keyed with medial/lateral names (`*MT` keys) is refused.
- **Femoral subregions follow Wirth & Eckstein 2008** (`morphometry/cartilage/femur_regions.py::subdivide_femur`, plan step 3; replaced the tibia-relative CWBZ scheme outright, no legacy switch). Like the tibia they live on the femoral tAB (`surface.py`) in world mm, so `Femur` now needs the **femoral bone label** (`bone_label=1`; `femur_bone_label=1` on `calculate_femoral_cartilage_thickness` / `calculate_knee_cartilage_thickness` / `calculate_thinnest_cartilage_area`; `--femur-bone-label` on both knee scripts); a missing bone or uncovered bone raises. **Femoral frame** (bone only, so it does not move with flexion): shaft axis = the first principal direction of the centroids of 3 mm cross-sections of the femur in a band 5 mm below to 25 mm above the top of the articular surface (`estimate_shaft_axis`), seeded by bone-centroid minus cartilage-centroid, 3 refit passes, proximal bins dropped while they hold < 0.75 of the next bin's voxels (an incomplete, FOV-cut cross-section has a biased centroid). That band is *metaphyseal on purpose* (it even starts 5 mm inside the condylar flare, not in the shaft): a knee FOV holds only 25-40 mm of femur above the cartilage and its proximal end is cut, often very obliquely, differently in different acquisitions of the same knee. **The band was chosen by flexion reproducibility on these same 96 scans, so the invariance figures below are not an unbiased estimate of it, and it is not the argmax of its own objective.** Over the 24 series (cF-area CV averaged over the two condyles, median/p90/max; A-P-extent CV median/max): (-5,25) 2.41/4.40/6.66 and 1.47/2.86; (-5,35) 2.54/3.45/4.48 and 0.14/2.22; (0,30) 2.77/3.80/4.16 and 1.63/3.75; (5,35) 2.53/3.90/5.25 and 1.61/8.57 plus one scan it cannot fit at all. (-5,35) is therefore at least as defensible as the default, which only wins the cF-CV median. What the band moves and does not: the thirds' *shares* barely (max difference between any two of those four bands, per scan: median 0.24 pp, max 1.58), the shaft axis by a median 1.9° (max 7.5°, (-5,25) vs (-5,35)) and the cF area and A-P extent measurably (median 0.3%, max 4.2%) — i.e. exactly the quantities the reproducibility figures are about. `subdivide_femur(shaft_band_mm=..., shaft_bin_mm=..., shaft_min_bin_ratio=...)` exposes it. M-L axis = the posterior condylar line (`posterior_condylar_axis`, iterated 3x from world +x, orthogonalised against the shaft axis, oriented patient-right); A-P = `cross(prox, ml)` — **no world direction is consulted for anterior**, it follows from the right-handed frame, which is why a 90°-flexed knee needs no special case. Because the M-L axis *is* the posterior condylar line, both condyles' posterior ends share one A-P coordinate. **Landmarks**: the trochlear notch is read off the *bone*, not the cartilage (`notch_depth_profile` / `find_trochlear_notch`): per 1 mm coronal slice the most distal bone coordinate in each 1 mm M-L bin, the valley depth = the drop from the lower flanking maximum to a bin between them (an empty bin = the open fossa counts as infinitely deep), then walk anteriorly from the deepest slice until the valley is shallower than `notch_depth_mm` (8; the fossa is 20-30 mm deep, the trochlear groove 3-6, and the profile falls ~1.5 mm per mm there, so ±2 mm of threshold move the border ~1 mm). How far the trochlear cartilage is labelled across the groove varies between acquisitions of one knee and the bone does not, which is why the cartilage-gap version of this was dropped. cF = notch plane (normal = A-P axis, i.e. parallel to the shaft) back to `weightbearing_fraction` (0.6; 0.75 is the other published choice) of the way to the condyle's posterior tAB end. **Thirds**: normalised arc length across the condyle (`arc_length_coordinate`): cut cF's tAB into coronal cross-sections 0.5 mm apart, parametrise each contour by arc length from its internal (notch-side) end, interpolate onto the vertices, `split_mesh_by_scalar` at 1/3 and 2/3 — the paper's per-triangle-strip construction as a continuous field, so the triangulation need not be strip-ordered. The articular surface wraps proximally round the posterior condyle, so a coronal plane in the back of the window cuts it twice; within the window the two sheets are separate patches, cF is the largest and the rest goes to the posterior zone. Re-measured with `_largest_patch` disabled and everything else fixed: without it 9 of the 93 clean scans have a third more than 1.2 pp off 33.3%, the worst being 40.5% (K7_90_unbelastet). The discarded patch is a sliver of the window in a healthy subdivision (per condyle: median 0.00%, p90 0.21%, max 14.90% on K4_90_belastet's right condyle), and more than `MAX_WRAP_FRACTION` (25%) logs a WARNING. **Extension, not in the paper**: the trochlea (`alf`/`arf`, anterior of the notch plane) and the posterior condyle (`plf`/`prf`, behind the 60% plane), each split into the two sides by the same plane that separates the condyles (through the fitted line of intercondylar-fossa centres, `ml = slope*ap + intercept`, MAD-filtered, |slope| clamped to 0.5 with a WARNING). Cartilage voxels take the region of the nearest tAB triangle centroid (`FemurSubdivision.region_codes_at`), stored in `Femur.point_labels`; QC exactly as for the tibia (`Femur.transfer_qc`, WARNING over 1% beyond 6 mm, fed into `scan_qc_flags` as `femur_transfer_far:<fraction>`). `Femur.plot_regions()` draws the labelled tAB with the frame, the notch plane and the two 60% planes (the paper's Fig. 6 view). **The femoral subregions no longer need a `Tibia`** — but the thickness *maps* do, for the shared in-plane frame: `Femur.calculate_thickness(tibia, ...)` / `calculate_femoral_cartilage_thickness(image, tibia, ...)` take it for that alone, and without one the maps are keyed in a femoral frame that `find_thinnest_area` refuses to overlap with tibial maps (see the thickness bullet). `plot_segments(tibia=None, ...)` still ignores it. On the 96 `dfg_knees` all 96 subdivide (~2-4 s per femur; K7/K8/K11_90 remain flagged, not trusted). Shares of cF tAB (n=93, mean (SD)): patient-left condyle i 33.4 (0.30) / c 33.2 (0.14) / e 33.4 (0.21), patient-right i 33.5 (0.29) / c 33.2 (0.13) / e 33.3 (0.20) (paper Table II, cMF/cLF: ic 33.5/33.4, cc 33.2/33.2, ec 33.3/33.4). cF tAB 548 ± 101 / 542 ± 82 mm², notch-to-posterior extent 30.1 ± 3.4 mm, trochlea 1209/1103 mm², posterior condyle 882/942 mm², whole femoral tAB 5225 (4266-6445). **Do not pool femoral subregion rows produced before and after this change** — the regions are different patches of cartilage. K1_0_unbelastet knn: `iclf` 2.428 -> 1.854 mm, `cclf` 2.742 -> 1.976, `ecrf` 1.399 -> 1.313, `right_anterior_zone` 2.538 -> 1.929. The old scheme's implausible values are gone: K7_90_unbelastet `iclf` 12.059 -> 1.351 mm, K9_90_belastet `icrf` 6.288 -> 1.745, K10_90_belastet `right_anterior_zone` 7.470 -> 2.253, K10_90_unbelastet `left_anterior_zone` 7.004 -> 3.901. `extract_central_weightbearing_zone`, `get_femoral_thirds`, `extract_anterior_posterior_zones`, `_restrict_to_contact_cluster`, `_si_gap` and `_femoral_condyle_dividing_line` are gone with their tests (`tests/unit/test_cwbz_flexion.py`, `tests/unit/test_femur_split.py`); pinned now by `tests/unit/test_femur_regions.py` (thirds 1/3 ± 0.01 on a cylindrical condyle, notch and 60% borders, shares invariant under 30/60/90° flexion and under axial/oblique rotation, anisotropic + oblique sampling, SimpleITK, mirror swaps condyles, shaft axis within 2.5° in any pose, the proximal wrap, laterality naming, the QC flag). **The cohort figures — flexion invariance, the sagittal-angle validation, the per-subregion thickness means and the detour rule's cost in mapped area — are in [`docs/evidence/cartilage_cohort_figures.md`](docs/evidence/cartilage_cohort_figures.md).**
- **Cartilage thickness follows Wirth & Eckstein 2008 section II-C** (`morphometry/cartilage/thickness.py`, plan step 4; replaced the voxel-column `mesh` / `knn` methods outright, no legacy switch). Per subregion it is the **area-weighted Euclidean distance between the two surfaces**, both ways: `cMe` = each cAB triangle's exact distance to the articular cartilage surface AC, `bMe` = each AC triangle's distance back to cAB, `aMe = (cMe + bMe) / 2` (the number to quote), every triangle weighted by its own area. `tab_*` are the same over the whole tAB with denuded triangles counted as 0.0 mm, so `tab_aMe = aMe * cAB/tAB` exactly. `aMax` / `aMav` (mean of the largest 1% of the area) / `aSD` (area-weighted population SD) / `aMin` / `aMiv` come from the two directions pooled into one sample in which each direction carries half the weight — so the pooled mean *is* `aMe`; `weighted_statistics` drops zero-weight entries, so the denuded placeholder cannot read a clean region's minimum as 0. **There are two families and they must not be mixed**: the bare names are ThC.**cAB** and the `tab_` ones ThC.**tAB** (the same with denuded bone at 0.0 mm), so `aMe ± aSD` is self-consistent, `tab_aMin` is exactly 0 for any region with any denuded bone while `aMin` is the thinnest cartilage actually measured, and `tab_aMax` / `tab_aMav` differ from their cAB twins only for an almost wholly denuded region. The CSV carries both families in separate blocks; the JSON `statistics` block carries all of them. **AC** is `surface.articular_surface_mesh`: the outer surface of the bone+cartilage **union** (so no bone-cartilage interface appears in it and a one-voxel-thick layer is still recognised), minus the cartilage rim and the smoothed foot of the rim — a triangle is kept only if its normal is within 60° of the bone normal beneath it *and* the voxel half a voxel *down the bone's normal* carries the cartilage label. Keeping the rim reads 1.63 mm on the flat 2 mm plate phantom and keeping only its foot 1.67, against a true 2.00 (now 1.95); the damage is proportional to a subregion's perimeter-to-area ratio, i.e. exactly what separates the peripheral subregions from the central one. `surface.surface_distances` is VTK's exact point-to-triangle distance, not nearest-centroid (which would be up to ~0.1 mm short on a 0.1 mm² triangle mesh). Only the **joint-facing sheet** enters the per-position maps (`_joint_facing_sheet`): projecting a curved surface onto the shared plane folds it onto itself — cells whose contributing femoral centroids span more than 5 mm are 10/7/13/22% of the femoral cells at 0/30/60/90° of flexion (26-47% of the tAB *area*) and 14/5/2/18% of the cells the thinnest-point search uses, with the largest spreads 30-60 mm — so without it a cell mixes the trochlea with the posterior condyle and its recorded articular position lands *inside the bone* between the two sheets, which is where the 3D marker and the femoral arc coordinate read it from. The regional statistics are per triangle and do not move. `measure_thickness` returns a `ThicknessMaps`, a `dict` subclass, so every existing consumer still sees `{subregion: {(u, v): mm}}`, with `.statistics`, `.frame`, `.positions` (the articular-surface point of each cell, for 3D markers) and `.triangle_thickness` (aligned with the subdivision mesh, for the heatmap) attached. `method=` is kept on every entry point but **rejects every value** with a message naming the replacement, so old call sites and `--method knn` command lines fail loudly.
- **One in-plane frame shared by both bones: the tibial plateau plane** (`thickness.KneeFrame`, `knee_frame_from_tibia`). The maps are keyed by the integer `grid_mm` (0.5) cell of each triangle centroid's projection into that plane, with `e1` patient-right (the line joining the two plates' centres of gravity S), `e3` the mean plate normal and `e2 = e3 x e1` anterior; the origin is the midpoint of the two S. The collapsed direction is the plate normal, which is the direction the tibiofemoral joint is compressed along at **every** flexion angle, so the femoral contact patch — distal condyle at full extension, posterior condyle at 90° — stays over the plateau and the two maps keep overlapping (pinned by a flexion phantom in `tests/unit/test_cartilage_thickness.py`: in the tibial frame the common footprint stays > 1500 mm² at 0/30/60/90°, in a co-rotating femoral frame it collapses below 300 mm² at 90°). A femur measured without a `Tibia` gets a frame from its own subdivision (`knee_frame_from_femur`, `source='femur'`), which is only the contact plane at full extension; such maps are valid on their own but `find_thinnest_area` refuses to overlap them with tibial ones. This replaced per-voxel-column keys that were array axes 0 and 1 whatever axis a subregion was measured along — the femoral posterior zones were keyed by `(x, depth)`, so their columns collided and their values were placed at the wrong in-plane position at 60-90° of flexion. `Femur.depth_axis` / `_depth_axis` / `_zone_surface_points` / `_thickness_surface` / `build_cartilage_meshes` / `get_superior_and_inferior_surface_points` and `Tibia.superior_surface` / `.inferior_surface` are gone with them.
- **Do not pool cartilage thickness rows produced before and after this change.** The old `knn` method measured one-directional nearest-neighbour distances between *voxel centres* on the two voxel surfaces and so ran about one voxel short; on K1_0_unbelastet the new `aMe` is higher in all 20 subregions (median +0.40 mm, max +1.26): tibial `clt` 1.800 -> 2.487, `crt` 1.311 -> 1.809, `plt` 1.729 -> 2.451, femoral `cclf` 1.976 -> 3.236, `iclf` 1.854 -> 2.300. On K1_90_unbelastet (median |Δ| 0.364) the two trochlea zones instead come *down* from their implausible voxel-column values — `left_anterior_zone` 4.002 -> 2.613, `right_anterior_zone` 3.827 -> 2.686 — because the old column axis crossed the whole trochlea. Per-position maps are not comparable at all (different keys, different frame). **Spread, not precision** (these are not repeats; the paper's test-retest benchmark is rms SD 19-84 µm): loaded vs unloaded at the same subject+angle, median |Δ aMe| 0.085 mm (p90 0.278, max 1.13, n = 900 subregion pairs); across the four flexion angles of one subject+load, median SD 0.117 mm (p90 0.296, max 0.769), median CV 5.7% — loosest in the tibial anterior and central regions (`art` 15.6%, `alt` 9.8%, `crt` 9.7%) against 2-5% for the femoral thirds. The tibial *label* itself is not flexion-stable on every subject — K1's tibial cartilage loses 33% of its voxels from 0° to 90° — so part of that spread is segmentation, not measurement.
- Knee cartilage thickness has 3D visualisation (`morphometry/cartilage/knee.py`): `Tibia`/`Femur` each expose `plot_segments()` (raw subregion voxels as distinctly-coloured cube glyphs — the `*_SUBREGIONS` tables), `plot_regions()` (the subdivided tAB) and `plot_thickness(thicknesses)`, which colours the **subchondral bone area itself** by the per-triangle thickness (`ThicknessMaps.triangle_thickness`) — the paper's Fig. 4(b) / 5(b) view, no surface reconstruction involved. A plain per-position dict without that array falls back to a point cloud at the cells' recorded surface positions. `plot_knee_segments`/`plot_knee_thickness` render both bones in one PyVista scene (re-exported from `measurements.cartilage`); the `calculate_*_cartilage_thickness` wrappers take `plot: bool | pv.Plotter` to draw the heatmap. All accept an optional `plotter=` and only `.show()` when they created it. `docs/illustrations/plot_knee_zones.py` is the didactic render built on all of them: run off-screen, it writes the subdivision figures (tibial tAB from superior with the central ellipse and the ±45° wedge planes, femoral tAB from inferior/posterior with the femoral frame and the notch / 60% border planes, both bones in one scene, the shared-scale thickness heatmaps with the 0 mm band highlighted, the thinnest-point arrows, and 0° vs 90° flexion side by side) plus rotatable HTML and a per-run `index.html` gallery into `out/knee_zones/`, and prints the shares the figures must match. A multi-panel figure cannot be one interactive scene (PyVista's exporter carries a single renderer), so the comparison figures also export each panel alone and a page putting them side by side. `--animate` films a turntable of every figure (`.gif` at half linear size, plus a full-size `.mp4` where `ffmpeg` is on `PATH`), each panel spinning about **its own** anatomical up vector so the panels of a comparison stay in step; note that an off-screen `screenshot` returns the *previous* render, so a frame loop must `render()` explicitly and copy the returned buffer. It reads the central share, the weight-bearing fraction, the bone labels and the region colours off the library, so it cannot drift from it; see `docs/illustrations/README.md`.
- `morphometry/cartilage/surface.py` is the shared bone-surface geometry for the Wirth & Eckstein 2008 subregions (`docs/wirth_eckstein_2008_plan.md` step 1; used by `tibia_regions.py` and `femur_regions.py`, which also take their region-transfer QC — `transfer_qc`, `TRANSFER_FAR_MM`, `TRANSFER_MAX_FAR_FRACTION` — from here; `tibia_regions` re-exports them, as callers have always imported them from there). `bone_surface_mesh` = marching cubes on the (largest-component) bone label in index space, mapped to **world mm, RAS frame for both backends** through the full index-to-world matrix (`index_to_world_affine`; SimpleITK's LPS is converted, so anatomical directions in world coordinates do not depend on the loader; winding fixed for reflecting directions, outward normals), windowed-sinc smoothed (`smooth_taubin`, 20 it., pass band 0.1: sphere area within 1%, surface moved ~0.02 mm). Where the bone touches the array border (FOV cut through the shafts) the mask is extended by replicated slices and the smoothed surface is cut **exactly** at the outer voxel boundary, so the mesh is *open* there with no artificial cap. `cartilage_coverage` (cAB) = triangle centroid within `tolerance_mm` of a voxel centre of that bone's cartilage label (KD-tree, world mm); default `0.6 ×` voxel diagonal (0.52 mm on `dfg_knees`) = the half diagonal a face-adjacent cartilage voxel can be away plus 20%. It is an empirical knee point (K1_0_belastet tibial cAB 1681/1931/2000/2024/2099 mm² at 0.40/0.44/0.48/0.52/0.75 mm: tighter riddles cAB with pinholes, looser keeps growing it along the rim). Because the distance test also reaches sideways past the cartilage edge, tAB is ~1-1.6% larger than with a normal probe (label 0.4 mm out along the outward normal: tibia 2027 vs 1995, femur 4707 vs 4660 mm²) — the documented alternative. Only the given label counts, so the other bone's cartilage never covers this bone. `total_subchondral_area` (tAB) = cAB + edge-connected uncovered components that do not touch the open (FOV) boundary and are smaller than `max_hole_fraction` (0.5) × cAB area — size is what separates holes from the non-articular rest of a bone that does not reach the FOV (e.g. the femur at 90° flexion). Denuded bone at the *rim* is not enclosed and not recovered; the rule is not plate-aware (cartilage meeting around the intercondylar area would get it filled), which is why `subdivide_tibia` runs it per plate. `articular_surface_mesh` builds the articular cartilage surface AC from the bone+cartilage union (see the thickness bullet) and `surface_distances` gives exact point-to-triangle distances; `bone_surface_mesh` therefore accepts a sequence of labels and meshes their union. `split_mesh_by_scalar` / `split_mesh_by_plane` cut triangles exactly along a linear-per-triangle level set in **numpy** (VTK's clip filters interpolate in float32, ~1e-8 area error), conserving area to double precision, keeping winding, copying cell data and interpolating point data; a level through a vertex reuses that vertex (no duplicate points / spurious open edges, which would otherwise read as FOV boundary to the hole rule). All shares are by `triangle_areas`, never voxel counts.
- **Cartilage deformation (Δ) maps** (`morphometry/cartilage/{registration,deformation,deformation_plot,robust_search}.py`, driven by `scripts/analyse_cartilage_deformation.py`). **Alignment is from bone only**: each configuration's bone surface, cropped to `REGISTRATION_CROP_MM` (40 mm) of its articular end, is rigidly ICP-registered (trimmed, one-sided residual) onto the reference's bone cropped `REFERENCE_CROP_MARGIN_MM` (10 mm) longer, so the moving surface lies inside the fixed one; the anatomical frames (which may rest on cartilage) only seed it. `REGISTRATION_MAX_RMS_MM` = 1.0 mm and `MAX_ROTATION_CHANGE_DEG` = 15° (initial-to-final rotation; a trimmed residual cannot see a ~180° flip of a near-symmetric bone) flag a comparison `registration_rms:<mm>` / `registration_rotation:<deg>`; the 1.0 mm was kept by the rule "a visible gap above the bulk of `registration.csv`, else 1.0", chosen from residuals alone: on the full runs (552f598, 0d993a3 and f53c02a, identical registrations) femur rms median 0.121 / p90 0.158 / max 0.208 mm (n = 98), tibia 0.091 / 0.125 / 0.142 mm (n = 88), one bulk, no gap, nothing flagged; rotation change median 2.28 / max 11.75° (femur), 1.02 / 5.69° (tibia); 3 of 186 hit the 60-iteration cap unconverged with rms 0.10-0.17 mm. The **tibial map** is the reference's plateau plane (`KneeFrame.in_plane`); the **femoral map** is `UnrolledFemur`: horizontal = M-L from the notch point, vertical = signed arc length (mm, absolute, never normalised) along the sagittal **bone** profile from the **trochlear-notch plane's distal crossing** (a bone landmark — the labelled cartilage's extent is not reproducible between acquisitions), negative up the trochlea, positive round to the posterior condyle; built once per knee on the reference and applied to every registered configuration (not a cohort atlas: millimetres on two knees are different anatomy). Its domain is the **whole** reference bone up to the FOV cut, not the registration's 40 mm crop: the femoral articular surface reaches up to 50.9 mm above the bone's distal end (proximal trochlea; 9 of the 15 reference femurs have covered tAB beyond 40 mm), and the 40 mm domain left all of it unmapped (K9: 4.85 of its 7.03 pp). Only the coordinate's domain changed — the registration still crops (the moving ⊂ fixed requirement); no level's used/unused status changed, and the arc of every reference triangle mapped both ways is bit-identical except at the old cut edge (39.7–42.1 mm, up to 5 mm off before, ≤ 102 mm² per femur). A sagittal level whose profile makes a detour of more than `PROFILE_MAX_DETOUR_MM` (2 mm) between its origin and its lowest point is **not used**: there the crossing lies on the condyle's side wall with a fold between it and the condyle, and anchored there the level's arc ran 10-33 mm long — on K1_30_unbelastet a strip at arc 50-85 mm beside the patient-right condyle in every panel. 2 mm is above the < 1 mm a circular distal arc adds over its chord; on the 15 reference femurs (2300 levels) the detour is median 0.05 / p90 0.6 mm and the 134 levels above 2 mm (5.8%) all lie at |ml| ≥ 29.5 mm except three in the fossa — the tail is continuous from ~1 to 35 mm, so the 2 mm is from the arc bound, not a gap. A level is also unused when its profile has no notch-plane crossing or corner at all (`no_crossing`; on the 15 reference femurs all such levels lie within 4 mm of the bone's M-L ends, the condyles' end faces). Unused levels — of either kind — can form interior runs (K11 35-41 mm, K10 35-39 mm); a vertex in **any** interior gap wider than `2 * slice_mm` takes a kept level's value only within one slice of it (the outer-edge rule) and is NaN further in — never a blend across the gap. **Hotspot** = the most negative median-smoothed Δ (`find_largest_thinning`, `--neighbourhood-mm` 3), per compartment, searched only where the whole smoothing window is measured **and more than `edge_margin_mm` (`DEFAULT_HOTSPOT_EDGE_MARGIN_MM` = 2.0, `--edge-margin-mm`) from each scan's own footprint edge** (reference and configuration separately, array border counting; the rule is `robust_search.interior_cells`, which `find_thinnest_area`'s `edge_margin_mm` also uses, bit-identically). The margin is geometric, not tuned on Δ: `cartilage_coverage` puts a band of near-zero thickness at every cartilage edge and two acquisitions' labels end several voxels apart, while the window rule removes only one 1 mm cell. On the current run, margin 0 vs 2: femoral hotspots at exactly 2.0 mm from an edge (the second ring, the closest the window rule allows) 144 of 294 → 0 (min 2.24 mm, median 2.24 → 2.83), tibial 85 of 264 → 0 (median 5.00 → 7.62); no fallback either way. **Open caveat — this is not solved, only moved**: with the margin, 116 of 196 femoral and 58 of 176 tibial left/right hotspots still sit ≤ 3 mm from an edge (femoral median 2.83 mm, tibial 7.07 mm; f53c02a), i.e. just past the margin. Only left/right are counted: the whole-bone hotspot is the deeper of the two and coincided with one of them in 186 of 186 comparisons (the earlier "176 of 294 / 86 of 264" counted it twice). The smoothed minimum still tends to sit next to the edge, where cartilage ends and two acquisitions' labels disagree; `report.md` prints these counts. A larger margin was not chosen, because it could only be justified on Δ. Per-subregion Δ is `aMe(config) − aMe(reference)` with **each scan's own** subdivision (independent of the registration), so its `status` depends only on the two scans' measurements of that bone: a registration error or a reference that cannot be mapped/unrolled fails that comparison's maps, hotspots and figure panel but leaves its subregion rows `ok` (`report.md` lists them under "Maps and hotspots failed, subregion Δ kept"). **Units differ between maps and tables**: the Δ maps, hotspots and figures carry the one-sided cAB → AC distance per position (the cMe direction, `ThicknessMaps.triangle_thickness`), the tables aMe (both directions averaged), so their magnitudes are not directly comparable; the colour bars and `report.md` say so. **Same-side assumption**: without `--laterality-table` subregions stay patient-side and are pooled as if every knee were the same side. The Δ is acquisition-to-acquisition spread plus deformation, not precision. **Open caveat — enclosed cavities in the cartilage labels**: 18 of the 113 scans in 6 knees (K10 all seven scans with 8 femoral / 4 tibial single-voxel cavities; K14, K8, K11, K2, K15 one to five) have background pockets fully enclosed by a cartilage label. Each reads as a ~3 mm spot of near-zero thickness (on K10 8 femoral and 4 tibial spots in the reference map, hence in every panel), and K10's whole-bone hotspot sits on one in 3 femoral comparisons (0° unloaded, 0° and 60° loaded) and about 3 tibial ones. This is the library's thickness, not the deformation pipeline: filling enclosed cavities (or dropping internal AC components) before measuring is follow-up work that moves existing thickness values; `report.md` lists the affected scans per run. **Do not pool** Δ maps / hotspots produced before the edge margin, the detour rule, the gap cap and the whole-bone unrolled domain with later ones (hotspots moved; femoral map coordinates at the outer condyle edges and above 40 mm changed); per-subregion Δ rows are unaffected by all of them. **Figures are self-explaining**: `deformation_plot.knee_caption` / `cohort_caption` draw a two-column caption under the panels (setup, sign of Δ, colour scales, markers, axes, abbreviations — quoting the run's `--neighbourhood-mm` / `--edge-margin-mm`, which `process_knee` passes on as `hotspot_settings`); the figure height follows the panel aspect (`_figure_height`). Panel backgrounds are `NO_DATA_GREY` so a NaN cell never reads as the white of Δ = 0, the reference panel names its subregions (femoral thirds as `ic`/`cc`/`ec`, too narrow for `icLF`), and in the cohort scatter the whole-bone hotspot is a black ring drawn last, because it usually sits on one of the compartment dots and was hidden under it. **Registration figures** (`plot_knee_residuals`, `plot_registration_sections`, `plot_registration_cohort`): `knee_delta_maps` now also fills `BoneDeltaMaps.residuals` — per configuration, on the Δ grid, the area-weighted mean *signed* distance (`signed_surface_distance`, VTK implicit distance, + outside) of its registered covered tAB centroids to the reference's **whole** bone surface (not the crop, so no crop edge enters); on K1 it is mostly within ±0.2 mm (a few spots at the footprint edge saturate the ±0.5 mm scale) while Δ reaches ±1 mm, which is the point of drawing it on the same map. The residual figure uses a fixed ±0.5 mm `PuOr_r` scale (orange/brown = scan bone outside the reference, purple = inside) so knees compare. The section figure cuts the exact surfaces the ICP uses — `reference_registration_surface` / `configuration_registration_start` were factored out of `knee_delta_maps` for that, so the figure cannot drift from the registration — with sagittal planes (normal = reference frame row 0) through the area-weighted centroid of each side's covered reference tAB (`section_origins`). The cohort figure reads `registration.csv` rows, all of them (flagged ones included); its femoral dropped area includes the reference's own ~2% unmapped baseline. `write_knee_figures` now takes the knee's scan records to draw the sections, and each figure fails on its own (a `failures.csv` row of stage `figure`, error prefixed with the figure name). **What the profile-detour rule costs in mapped area is in [`docs/evidence/cartilage_cohort_figures.md`](docs/evidence/cartilage_cohort_figures.md).**
