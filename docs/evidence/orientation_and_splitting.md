# LPI orientation and left/right splitting

*Moved verbatim from `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-11-12 when that file was cut below its size limit; `CLAUDE.md` keeps a summary and links here. Nothing below was rewritten.*

2. **Coordinate system** — masks must be placed in **LPI** orientation before measurement. Always call `seg.transform_coordinate_system()` right after loading; `remove_outliers()` is also commonly applied before measurement. The reorientation only permutes/flips voxel axes, so a strongly oblique scan can come out labelled non-LPI: `Image.achieved_axcodes` (live `nib.aff2axcodes` of the current affine) and `Image.orientation_ok` (achieved == requested `axcodes`; `None` before any request) record it, with one WARNING on the `morphometry` logger; the behaviour itself is unchanged. Known cases: 3 of the 96 `dfg_knees` scans — K7_90_belastet (raw IAR -> LSP), K8_90_belastet and K11_90_belastet (raw SPR -> LIA). The scanner-frame tilt of the slice axes is *not* the cause: it comes from knee flexion and is harmless (axis 2 vs world S-I median 4.6/11.1/24.1/38.7° at 0/30/60/90° flexion; 9 healthy scans exceed 40°, K10_90_belastet 57.5°, all LPI and fine). What is wrong is the anatomy's rotation in the index frame: the tibial plate normal is ≤17.0° from index axis 2 in all 93 LPI scans but 25.7° (K11), 75.5° (K7), 83.0° (K8) off, and the femur-minus-tibia cartilage centroid along axis 2 is -28 to -12.2 mm in the 93 vs -2.6 (K7), +1.6 (K8), +35.3 mm (K11). It surfaces as a near tie in axis labelling (K7's first axis: 0.706 S-I vs 0.703 A-P) that nibabel's greedy, voxel-axis-ordered `io_orientation` breaks differently after the permutation. The user attributes these scans to a scanner misconfiguration; they are flagged in the knee scripts' `qc_flags`, not fixed or dropped. K11_90_belastet is additionally a separate defect the orientation flag catches only by coincidence: 1 mm isotropic, 344 slices, 512 mm FOV (all others 0.31×0.31×0.75 mm, 172 slices), tibial cartilage of 9.4k voxels scattered over a 258×318×213 mm box; its tibia subdivision fails ('plates overlap'). `orientation_ok` is only meaningful on the reoriented image (and crops/splits/reflections of it): `mirror_image_left_right` copies `axcodes` but flips the affine, so a mirrored LPI image reads `False` (RPI).
3. **Left/right splitting** — pipelines split a full-body mask at `array.shape[0] // 2` into `left_*` / `right_*` halves. Note: "left" / "right" refers to the *image* side, not the patient side. Functions take a `side='left'|'right'` argument and document this convention explicitly. Whenever a half is used as an `Image`/`Segmentation` (i.e. with geometry), split with `image_io.split_image_left_right` — never wrap `array[half:]` in the parent's affine. Its guarantee (both backends): the left half keeps the parent geometry; the right half's index `i` maps to the same physical point as the parent's `i + [half, 0, 0]`. The parent affine on the right half put every right-side physical coordinate `half * spacing_x` off (195 mm on the CT fixture). Index-space landmarks from a right half still need `+ half` on x to address the whole mask (e.g. `right_fhc_adj[0] += left_mask.shape[0]`). It shares its affine-advance with `split_ct_image`'s z-crops and the public `image_io.crop_image(source, axis, start, end)` (all via `_crop_axis`); the API's changepoint hip/knee/ankle split (`api/ingest/dicom.py::_split_volume`) uses `crop_image` too. Two left-right flips exist and are not interchangeable for physical-space code: `image_io.mirror_image_left_right` relabels indices (mirrored index `i` ↦ parent `n-1-i`, every voxel keeps its physical location — a left leg stays a left leg physically), while `image_io.reflect_image_left_right` reverses the voxels but keeps the geometry, producing the physical *mirror twin* (a left femur becomes a right femur in index and physical space). `geometry.split_left_right` is the geometry-free array split.
