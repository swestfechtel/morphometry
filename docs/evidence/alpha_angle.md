# The alpha angle: reslice invariance and the level question

Why `calculate_alpha_angle` reslices the way it does, and why its level reads ~23 deg below the
published asymptomatic mean. Moved verbatim out of `CLAUDE.md`, which keeps the construction, the
precondition, the two gates, the do-not-pool burden and the standing caution against tuning.

The short version: the invariance defect is fixed and the level question is **closed negatively** --
the level is the radial position of the section plane, the `plane_rotation` mechanism ships, the
maximum-over-the-sweep reduction was built and withdrawn because it does not converge, and both
principled per-position quality criteria are ruled out by measurement. Do not tune `tol`, the
`0.6 * r` band or the `1.2 * radius` ceiling toward the literature.

**A note on cross-references.** These paragraphs were written inside `CLAUDE.md`, so an
"above", "below" or "recorded here" may point at a bullet that stayed there rather than at
something on this page. Nothing was reworded, which is the point; the file it came from is
`CLAUDE.md` and the two are meant to be read together.

## What the old reslice did, and what it cost

**The alpha angle's oblique reslice is index-frame invariant (task #5's other half, 2026-11-05).** `measurements/hip.py::calculate_alpha_angle` brings the femoral neck axis into a fixed-z slice by **rotating the whole volume** — `scipy.ndimage.rotate(mask, axes=(0,2), angle=rotation_angle, reshape=True, order=0)` — and then used that rotated array for two separate things: transporting the head and neck centres through it (paint a 3×3×3 cube, rotate it, re-centroid, round to `int16`) and reading the femoral contour off `rotated_image[:, :, head_z_rotated]`. `reshape=True` rotates about the **input array's centre** and sizes the output to fit, so both depend on where the anatomy sits in the array. Measured over the 120 NaKo sample halves under a pure pad of (3, 0, 2) voxels — every index changed, every voxel's physical position identical — the head radius and the rotation angle are **exactly invariant (120/120)**, the cube-transported landmarks move on 39 and 36 halves (max 1 and 2 voxels), and **the resampled slice moves on 118 of 120** (median 10, max 44 voxels of content; the contour's shape relative to the head centre on **0/120**, median 0.45, max 1.86 voxels). So the defect is downstream of the rotation angle, exactly as in the condylar line — but it is *not* mainly the landmark transport, and **fixing only the transport would not have fixed the measurement**. End to end: median 0.65°, p90 3.13°, max 7.45° on the isotropic path, only 9 of 600 pad probes exact.

## How far the invariance goes, and the one probe that is not exact

**One probe is not exact, and the claim is qualified accordingly.** Every *pad* probe is 120/120 and an in-plane bounding-box crop is 120/120, but a bounding-box crop of the **femur alone across all three axes** is exact on only **12 of 120** (residual median 0.523°, max 4.510°, against the shipped code's 0.903 / 4.739 on the same probe).

**Attributed, and the residual is not in the reslice.** Restricted to the halves where the head centre, neck centre, rotation angle and radius are all crop-corrected identical, the angle residual is **0.000e+00** — 12/12 for the 3-axis and z-only crops and 120/120 for an in-plane crop — so the reslice is exactly invariant whenever its landmarks are, and the honest claim is **“invariant given its landmarks, which a z-crop can itself move”**. The axis is **z exclusively**: an in-plane-only femur crop trimming a mean 53 voxels of x and 35–54 of y is 120/120 exact with *zero* movement of all four landmarks (so `split_image_left_right`'s x split is safe), and the z-only crop reproduces the 3-axis crop to the digit. A dose-response pins the mechanism beyond doubt: removing a mean 21.7 leading z slices (the whole-label bounding box) is **120/120** exact, removing 146.5 (the femur's own box) is **12/120** — same axis, same operation, differing only in how many *empty* slices go. The cause is `get_femoral_head_center`'s slab walk, which was removed in the next commit — **so that qualification is now historical and the two changes compose into unconditional invariance under that crop**: measured with the call production makes, the alpha angle is exact on **120 of 120** halves under the same 3-axis femur-bbox crop (max 0.000e+00°) against **1 of 120** before either change, with the cohort means reproducing to the decimal (31.096 → 31.145 anterior, 34.747 → 34.744 posterior). One caution for anyone re-measuring this: pass the image half **unreversed** and let `side=` do the reflection, as `scripts/process_nako.py:79,86` do. Pre-reversing a right half *and* passing `side='right'` reverses it twice; before the sentinel became a `ValueError` that silently returned a window-corner angle, and after it raises on 20 of 120 halves — all of them right halves, 8 of them with anterior exactly equal to posterior, which is the fingerprint of both searches hitting the same sentinel. That is a probe error, not a defect, and it is recorded here because it looks exactly like a regression.

## Why the sampler is grid-constant

**The sampler is `mode='grid-constant'`, and `'constant'` would have left a residue of the very defect.** `map_coordinates(order=0, mode='constant')` returns `cval` for any coordinate outside the **closed** interval `[0, n-1]`, so it discards the outer **half voxel** at both ends of every axis — a dependence on where the array ends. `'grid-constant'` treats the domain as `[-0.5, n-0.5)`, which is the same nearest-voxel rule the interior already uses. This is not theoretical: on `PA000668` image-right (12 slices, section plane at z = 11.26) a **high-end** z pad — which changes no anatomy index whatsoever — moves the pair by **14.978°** under `'constant'` ([44.857, 14.782] → [29.879, 2.270]) and by **0.000** under `'grid-constant'`. A deep-FOV cohort hides it completely: an independent implementation using `'constant'` still scored 120/120 invariant on NaKo, because every NaKo half is 264 slices deep and the plane never leaves the array.

## The precondition on the non-isotropic path

**Precondition: full 3-D isotropy, which is stronger than task #10's in-plane rule, and it is exactly right.** Derived per component rather than assumed: `sz` is required because an x–z *index* rotation is a rotation of the anatomy only if `sx == sz`; `sy` enters the **rotation angle itself** (it is `arccos(−dx/|d|)` and `|d|` contains `dy`) and sets the metric of the second axis of the `(u, y)` contour and circle fit; and `0.6 · r`, `tol = 2` and `1.2 · radius` all compare a distance in the `(u, y)` plane against a radius or count in **in-plane voxels**, so they need all three equal. It therefore cannot be weakened to in-plane-only or to `sx == sz` alone. Implemented the way task #10 did it: the entry point takes an `Image`/`Segmentation` **or** a bare array, plus an optional `spacing=` that wins over the image's; a known, anisotropic spacing raises **`ValueError` and nothing else** (the batch scripts catch only `(RuntimeError, AssertionError, ValueError)`, so anything else would abort a cohort run instead of writing a NaN row), and an unknown spacing is **not** warned about — task #10's deliberate choice, since a warning would fire on every existing call over a known-conforming cohort. `scripts/process_nako{,_large}.py` drop their `.array` so the production callers are checked; NaKo is 1 mm isotropic, so their behaviour is unchanged. Two residuals: the guard reads the **zooms, not the affine's orthogonality**, so a sheared affine passes and still shears the angle (the same residual task #10 accepted); and the guard only validates — `isotropic=` / `x_ratio=` still select the head-centre geometry independently of the real spacing, and the spacing is deliberately **not** passed down to `get_femoral_head_center`, which would switch the NaKo code path and move the golden for an unrelated reason.

## The failed contour search: the 3000-case NaKo survey

**It is unobserved on the NaKo sample but NOT dead code, and the distinction matters.** The search fails on **0 of 120** halves on the shipped path and on both new variants, across the base case and all three index-frame probes (960 searches), with the `radius + 2 < d < 1.2 · radius` band holding a minimum of 3 and a median of 6 contour points — thin but never empty, and identical on both paths, so the replacement does not make the path reachable. But the predicate demonstrably *can* find nothing: on the non-isotropic Augsburg path the shipped search fails on **35 of 100** halves and the new one on 31 — so 0/120 is a sample, not a proof. The one-sided 95% upper bound is **~4.9%** (up to ~3,000 of the 60,814 cohort halves), taking the effective n as the **60 subjects** rather than the 120 halves, since two halves of one subject share site, protocol and acquisition and are not independent draws; the naive per-half bound of 2.47% is too tight.

**On any case where it fires, this change replaces a silent window-corner angle with a NaN row**, a material improvement on the published path rather than a formality — and the new construction *resolves more* halves than the old (31 failures against 35; nine newly resolving, five flipping the other way and **merely different rather than thinner**: three of those five have *more* section voxels and contour points, and the band-count distributions are indistinguishable cohort-wide, shipped median 86 against the new 87).

**On the Augsburg path the raise is also moot**: all 31 raising halves are 1.09375 × 1.09375 × 10.0 mm and `validate_isotropy` refuses **31 of 31** once the spacing is known, so either the caller passes geometry and the guard refuses the data before the search runs, or it passes a bare array and has opted out of the precondition the construction rests on. The defect is a **silent wrong answer, never a crash** — no exception was ever possible.

**What is NOT yet evidenced**, and must not be written as though it were: that any *published* NaKo value is a corner artefact. Nothing fired on the 120-half sample and the Augsburg demonstration is on data the guard now refuses. The defensible claim is that the predicate is demonstrably capable of finding nothing, that the shipped code could not distinguish that case from a real measurement in the CSV, and that the published cohort was never checked.

**It has now been checked, and the path DOES fire on real NaKo data (2026-11-06) — so the "0 of 120" above was a sample too small, not a dead path.** Stratified sample of **3000 of the 30,407 cases** (150 from each of the 20 chunk directories, evenly spread through each one's sorted listing), 6000 halves, measured through the production call (`calculate_alpha_angle(half, side=side, isotropic=True)`, the half **unreversed** with `side=` doing the reflection):

**5980 resolved, 12 halves on 12 distinct subjects hit the failed contour search** — 0.2% of halves, 0.4% of subjects. Taking the subject as the unit of independence, as the bound below did, the one-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson bound is **0.647%**, i.e. **at most ~197 of the 30,407 cases** and a point estimate of ~120, against the standing bound of ~4.9% / ~3,000. So the exposure is real but two orders smaller than the worst case, and roughly a hundred published NaKo rows most likely carried a silent window-corner angle.

**Quote that as a proxy, not a count**: it is measured with the *current* predicate, and on Augsburg the shipped and replacement constructions failed on 35 and 31 of 100 halves respectively, so the two are close but not identical.

**Two further things the same sample turned up.** (a)

**Scope it precisely: the other 8 of the 15 are implausible without being impossible** (84.52°, 79.37°, an anterior of 0.003 that prints as 0.00) and are *still returned*, because separating them from real anatomy needs a threshold fitted to a cohort — as does the head-radius check that would catch the two ~2x-radius halves. So the 15 split **7 impossible / 8 merely extreme**, and only the first group is refused. `0` and `90` are properties of the construction, not of any cohort, which is what makes this separable from the open question of the measurement's absolute level. One consequence worth knowing: the **non-isotropic** path routinely exceeds 90° (PA000001 image-right reads 93.40°), so it is now refused there too — correctly, since this file already records that path as geometrically meaningless, and `test_head_centre_translation.py::test_alpha_angle_is_unchanged` was generalised to assert that a *refusal* is invariant under translation just as a value is.

**Those 15 split into three signatures, which means the item is really two complementary checks, and only one of them is a range check.** Probed case by case against 12 normal halves (whose fitted head radius is a tight 19.85-26.79 mm): (i) **two halves have a head radius ~2x anatomical** — `126308` left and right at **52.06** and **53.40 mm** — and one has it far too small, `124184` right at **10.78**; their angles are then meaningless, so a **head-radius** plausibility check would catch them and an angle range check would not. (ii) **six halves have a perfectly sound radius and an angle above 90°** (`113304` 20.88 mm / 90.96°, `100023` 21.98 / 96.33, `100016` 21.91 / 95.78, `117737` 24.79 / 99.44 and 24.08 / 98.93, `122390` 16.81 / 97.15), which only a range check catches. (iii) one half, `106727` right, returns **0.00 and 0.01** with a 30 mm radius — both searches collapsing onto effectively the same point. So `0 < alpha < 90` is a genuine *range* check needing no threshold fitted to any cohort, and it should be paired with a radius check that does need one.

**The mechanism of (i) is NOT diagnosed, and one obvious explanation is refuted**: it is tempting to blame `_femoral_head_slab_bound`'s escape hatch, which returns `mask.shape[2] - 1` (no effective bound) when the head's equator is never observed and whose own docstring warns that without a bound the sphere is "fitted to the whole proximal femur, silently returning a radius two to three times anatomical". Both `126308` halves do hit that hatch — **but so does a normal half** (`100337` left, hatch fired, radius 19.97), and `126308`'s slab comes out a perfectly ordinary **46 and 44 slices**, the same as the normal cases'. So the radius blows up with a *normal-length* slab, the hatch is neither necessary nor sufficient, and what is actually ill-conditioned is the fit on that point cloud — most likely a shallow cap left by the two in-plane cutoffs, which is a hypothesis and not a measurement. Note this is a **different** defect from the ~1.2 mm slab-depth bias recorded above; do not merge the two.

**One correction to the reasoning recorded above**, which does not change the `ValueError` choice: the justification "the batch scripts catch only `(RuntimeError, AssertionError, ValueError)`, so anything else would abort a cohort run" is **inapplicable to this function** — `calculate_alpha_angle`'s only production callers are `scripts/process_nako{,_large}.py`, and both wrap every measurement in a bare `except Exception`, so even a `TypeError` becomes a NaN row there. One did occur in the sample (1 of 6000, from `circle_fit`'s `leastsq` on too few points) and would not have aborted anything. Raising `ValueError` remains right for consistency with the rest of the package; the stated reason just belongs to `compute_torsion.py` and the Augsburg/NMDID scripts, not here. Left alone at the time, deliberately, and **repaired in `d980792` (2026-11-06)**: `femur = np.where(rotated_image == 1, 1, 0)` hardcoded label **1** instead of `segmentation_label`, so the contour ignored the caller's label while the head centre honoured it. It was held back out of `8d138af` only to keep that commit's attribution clean. No caller exercises it (all pass 1), so the repair is zero-movement — and the branch is no longer untested: `test_alpha_angle_reslice.py::test_the_contour_honours_the_callers_segmentation_label` paints the identical resolving phantom at labels 2, 5 and 7 and requires the pair to be bit-identical to the label-1 reading. It **discriminates**, which is the reason to trust it: against the pre-fix line all three parametrisations fail, and they fail as a `ValueError` rather than a wrong number, because a mask holding nothing at label 1 gives that contour **no points at all**. So the old behaviour on a non-default label was not a subtly shifted angle but a raise — which the batch scripts would have recorded as a NaN row.

## Rounding the neck cell: measured and rejected

**Rounding it instead was measured and rejected, and for two reasons neither of which is principle**: with everything else new, rounding gives anterior mean |L−R| 2.9414° / median 2.5774 / Pearson +0.6185 against the continuous cell's 2.8578 / 2.2286 / +0.6209 — worse on **7 of the 8** L/R statistics across both angles — and its movement is smaller only at the *median* (anterior 0.579 vs 0.932°) while its p90 and max are **larger** (3.024 / 4.853 against 2.805 / 4.484), so “smaller movement” is not an argument for it either. The deeper reason is that **rounding could not have made this a pure invariance fix anyway**: the shipped head cell is `int16`-*truncated* and then its cube centroid re-rounded in the rotated frame, and truncation does not commute with translation, so no faithful reproduction of it can be invariant. Anchoring the grid at the head centre makes the head cell exactly `(Ru, Rv)` by construction. One semantic change is therefore **forced** either way, and rounding the neck cell would only add a quantisation on top of it. So the agreement regression below belongs to the **plane position** — the shipped plane runs through the *rounded* transported head centre, up to half a voxel off, the new one through the centre exactly — and not to the neck-cell semantics. The remainder is the plane position plus the head cell, which the shipped code rounded **twice** (`int16` before the cube, then `round` of the transported centroid), so de-rounding it also moves the anterior/posterior split boundary and the `0.6 · r` circle-fit band. `alpha_angle_left` (NaKo) was re-captured, [24.466, 29.211] → **[26.736, 32.351]**; no other golden moved — the `tests/golden/` diff holds that one file alone, which matters because `MORPH_UPDATE_GOLDEN=1` rewrites every golden it evaluates, not only the failing ones.

## Two independent implementations agree

**Two independent implementations agree.** The change was written by one agent and the measurements reproduced by a second working from its own reimplementation; the two agree to **four decimals on every** L/R and movement statistic, and both reproduce the shipped `alpha_angle_left` golden bit-exactly before the change. The reslice basis is pinned against `scipy.ndimage.rotate` bitwise at nine angles, and the failed-search path is unreachable on **0 of 120** halves on the shipped path and on both new variants across the base case and all three index-frame probes (960 searches, no failures).

**One plot-only fix rode along**, stated here because nothing tests it: the circle-fit band was the single overlay the shipped code forgot to mirror on a right half, so it was drawn flipped against the image. No measurement reads it.

## The level question: the radial-position sweep, built and withdrawn

**Literature checked 2026-11-06, and it changes the item rather than answering it: there is no plane-matched reference range to compare against, so the 54.1° figure cannot adjudicate this measurement at all.** Three things from the search. (a) The alpha angle is strongly **plane-dependent**: comparing radial MRI slices against standard oblique-axial images gives a significant discrepancy in **54% of subjects**, normal on oblique axial and ≥55° on radial, because the antero-superior head-neck bump commonly sits *above* the oblique-axial plane. (b) "Normal" values are quoted anywhere from **52° to 62°**, and the original 50-55° pathological cut-offs are described as having been **invalidated** by later healthy-population studies — so 55° is not a fixed line either. (c)

**No source was found that gives asymptomatic normals separately per plane**, which is exactly the number needed here.

**What this implies for our function, as a hypothesis the literature supports rather than a result:** it reslices so the plane *contains* the femoral neck axis, which is the plane the alpha angle was originally defined in — but a plane containing that axis can be rotated about it (the radial series), alpha is highest antero-superiorly, and this function picks **one** such position rather than sweeping and taking the maximum. A single non-antero-superior radial position reading ~23° below a figure that likely reflects the worst position is then expected rather than anomalous.

**That question is now ANSWERED by measurement (2026-11-06): the level gap is the radial position, and sweeping closes most of it.** The construction reslices the plane spanned by `u = (cos θ, 0, −sin θ)` and `v = (0, 1, 0)`; a radial series rotates that plane *about the neck axis*, i.e. rotates `v` about `u`. Substituting `v(φ) = cos φ · v + sin φ · (u × v)` in the reslice and leaving **everything downstream untouched** — so each φ is the real measurement in a different radial position, and φ = 0 reproduces the shipped value exactly — over 20 NaKo cases / 40 halves at 15° steps from −60° to +60°: the anterior mean runs **30.26° at +15° up to 45.03° at +60°** while the posterior runs the other way (45.11° at −60° down to 28.05° at +45°), i.e. the two angles trade as the plane turns, exactly as a radial series should. The maximum over that sweep gives an anterior mean of 48.45° against the shipped single position's 30.34° — but **that +18.10° is largely artefact and must not be quoted as closing the gap, which an earlier version of this paragraph did.** A convergence test settles it: taking the maximum over an increasingly fine sweep of `[0, 90)°` on 30 NaKo halves gives a mean of **33.70° at 1 position, 46.97 at 3, 57.90 at 5, 69.10 at 9, 79.73 at 13 and 80.22 at 19**, with the median reaching **86.66°** and **18 of 30 halves above 85°**. It does not converge on an anatomical value — it climbs monotonically toward the 90° plausibility ceiling, i.e. it finds the worst *artefact* each refinement admits rather than the antero-superior bump. So the +18.10° measured at a 9-position sweep is a point on that divergence curve, not an estimate of the effect. So the measurement is not broken and the landmarks are not at fault: it reports one radial position where the clinical value is the maximum over the series.

**Four caveats, because this is a diagnosis and not a shipped feature.** 20 cases not the full sample; a 15° step over ±60° is not a full radial series, so the true maximum is higher still; a maximum over 9 samples is **biased upward** by noise alone, so +18.10° is an over-estimate of the effect of a principled sweep; and at the extremes the sweep picks up artefacts — at φ = −60° only 37 of 40 halves resolve and the anterior maximum is **89.78°**, i.e. right at the `(0, 90)` plausibility boundary, so a naive max would harvest exactly the impossible values that gate now refuses.

**The MECHANISM is shipped and tested; the REDUCTION was implemented, measured, and REVERTED — which answers the question negatively.** (`49fb168`-ff). `calculate_alpha_angle` and `_reslice_femoral_neck_section` take `plane_rotation: float = 0.0`, which turns the section plane about the neck axis (about `u`, so it selects the radial position) — **at the default it is bitwise the old construction**, verified on 6 real halves and by the whole characterization set, so no value moved and no golden was re-captured. A swept position is the real measurement: it goes through the isotropy guard and the `(0, 90)` plausibility gate rather than around them. What is **not** implemented is which position to report: the anterior and posterior angles peak at opposite ends of the sweep, so a per-angle maximum returns two numbers measured in *different planes*, and that changes what the returned pair means.

**And the published reduction was then built and withdrawn, which is the answer rather than a deferral.** A `calculate_alpha_angle_radial` taking the maximum over `[0, 90)` was implemented — the range is *not* a free parameter, since `anterior(φ + 180°) == posterior(φ)` to 1e-6 (verified on real data at four positions), so sweeping 90° with both angles covers every distinct half-plane exactly once — and the convergence test above killed it: the maximum drifts to ~87° as the sampling is refined.

**It was reverted rather than shipped**, because a function whose docstring promises the clinical convention while returning a number that climbs with sampling density is a footgun, and `method='tomczak'` is this file's record of what untrustworthy optional paths cost.

**What this establishes:** a radial maximum is only meaningful if each position is trustworthy, and the `(0, 90)` plausibility bound is far too loose to exclude a degenerate plane. A usable sweep would need a per-position *quality* criterion, and **the obvious principled candidate is ruled out by measurement rather than by assertion**: a circle fit has three degrees of freedom, so a floor on the band's contour-point count would be a mathematical requirement and not a fitted threshold — but the absurd positions are **not** sparse. Over 156 resolved (position, half) results from 12 halves × 13 positions, the band holds a median **97** points where the result is plausible (<55°, n=124, range 79-111), **101** where it is high (55-70°, n=13) and **97** where it is absurd (≥70°, n=19, range 81-109) — three completely overlapping distributions. So the divergence is **intrinsic to the construction at off-axis positions, not a sparsity artefact**, a degrees-of-freedom floor cannot gate it, and any criterion that could would have to be **fitted to a cohort** — the one thing this file consistently refuses.

**Implementing it found two latent bugs, which is the argument for having done so rather than reasoned about it**: the window sizing used `offsets[:, 1]` and the neck cell `neck[1] - head[1]`, both shortcuts valid only while `v` was exactly `(0, 1, 0)`. Each is identical at the default — so no shipped value was ever affected — and each would have made a swept position silently wrong. The first was caught by the function's **own** margin assertion (the one guarding against `get_contour`'s erosion inventing a border contour) and the second by the new tests; `tests/unit/test_alpha_angle_radial_position.py` pins the default's bitwise identity, that the rotation is about the neck axis rather than an image axis, a full turn, and the guards. Sources: a systematic review of 2,114 asymptomatic hips (PMC5620002), `radsource.us/femoroacetabular-impingement/` for the plane discrepancy and the 52-62° range, and `academic.oup.com/jhps/article/3/3/223/2378951` on the invalidated cut-offs.
