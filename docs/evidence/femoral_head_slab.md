# Femoral-head slab and sphere fit: the evidence

The measurement evidence behind the femoral-head sphere fit: the ~1.2 mm medial drag on the
isotropic MRI path, the inflated CT head radius, the six arbiter routes closed by measurement,
the constructions that were built and rejected, the blast-radius tables, and the removal of the
`zscore` slab-start guard. Every paragraph here was moved verbatim out of `CLAUDE.md`, which
keeps the decisions, the residuals and the do-not-pool warnings and links here for the workings.

Read it when revisiting `C.FEMORAL_HEAD_SLAB_RADII`, the in-plane cutoffs of
`get_femoral_head_center_ct`, or any claim that a new cue could adjudicate the slab depth.

**A note on cross-references.** These paragraphs were written inside `CLAUDE.md`, so an
"above", "below" or "recorded here" may point at a bullet that stayed there rather than at
something on this page. Nothing was reworded, which is the point; the file it came from is
`CLAUDE.md` and the two are meant to be read together.

## The slab walk coupled the head centre to empty space

**The attribution is confirmed from two directions and by two agents**: the landmark magnitudes above were produced twice from independent probes and agree exactly, and the 12 of 120 halves on which `layer_high` is unchanged are *precisely* the 12 whose four landmarks are unchanged and whose downstream alpha angle is bit-identical — which is what identifies this walk as the mechanism rather than merely a correlate. Padding is nearly harmless by contrast (5.2e-11 voxels even for a 120-slice pad) because it *adds* zeros to a vector already dominated by them, and a dose-response confirms that is the whole story: trimming a mean 21.7 empty slices leaves 120/120 exact, trimming 146.5 leaves 12/120.

## The guard did nothing it was designed to do

**The guard does nothing it was designed to do.** In the **as-given** frame the walk never fires at all on this cohort — 0 of 120 halves skip a single slice. And of the 294 skips it makes in the *cropped* frames, **every one is a negative z-score** (an unusually small slice); the upper branch that `np.abs` enables never fires, so the inline comment ("too few mask points") describes the only branch ever taken and the `abs` is cosmetic here. So on the production path the guard rejects nothing, and its only observable effect anywhere in the repo is to create the position dependence above.

**And it was not inert by construction — it could fire, which is what makes its removal a fix rather than a tidy-up.** With scipy's `ddof=0` the largest |z| any element of an `N`-vector can reach is `sqrt(N-1)`, so the threshold of 2 is attainable for any array with `N >= 6` slices. For a mask occupying a fraction `f` of the z extent whose **non-empty** slice areas have coefficient of variation `CV` (write `c² = 1 + CV²`), a near-empty apex slice has `|z| = sqrt(f) / sqrt(c² - f)`, so it fires iff **`f > 0.8 · c²`**; at full occupancy that is simply `CV < 0.5`. That form is exact under its premise — validated to a max relative error of **4.4e-15** over 500 random area vectors with a genuinely empty apex, with `f > 0.8c²` equivalent to `|z| > 2` identically — so the caveat is about the premise, not the algebra: **use it on thin-sliced data and compute the statistic directly on thick-sliced data.** Against the true `np.abs(zscore(...))` — **in the z-trimmed frame, with the apex slice included in `c²`**, the only frame where the comparison discriminates — it is accurate on NaKo (+4.2% median error, fire/no-fire verdict correct on 112 of 120) and fails on Augsburg (+108% median, worst case +24000%, verdicts 78 of 100); in the as-given frame it gives +10.7% and +75.0% with the verdicts trivially right because nothing fires either way, and dropping the apex from `c²` moves Augsburg's verdict count to 66, because at 1 mm the apex slice is a thin cap at **4.1%** of the mean slice area while at 10 mm it already cuts **52%** and is nowhere near empty. The measured numbers carry the claim: in the as-given frames the apex |z| maxes at **0.847** (NaKo) and **1.325** (Augsburg) against the threshold of 2; trim away only the empty slices and it becomes a median **2.466** on NaKo, firing on **108 of 120** halves, against a median 0.904 on Augsburg, firing on 2 of 100. Those same **2 halves fire under either calling convention** — the guard sits above the `isotropic` branch and reads only the mask, so its trigger cannot depend on an argument it never sees; what the convention changes is whether the firing *moves the answer*, which it does on 1 of the 2, a different one each time.

**The anti-correlation with its own purpose is a measurement that rests on no cohort at all.** Hold `f = 1` over 56 slices with the apex at 6% of the mean of the rest — a genuine top-of-head cap, exactly what the guard was written to reject — and sweep the CV of the remaining areas: the apex |z| falls monotonically 7.416 / 4.238 / 3.147 / 2.487 / 2.061 / 1.558 / 1.183 at CV 0.00 / 0.20 / 0.30 / 0.40 / 0.50 / 0.70 / 1.00, firing down to CV 0.5, and an unrelated linear-ramp family collapses monotonically too, though down to 0.4 rather than 0.5 — the analytic boundary at `f = 1` is exactly `CV < 0.5` for *any* family, so that 20% spread is the near-empty premise failing (a 6% apex is not empty), the same sensitivity the validity note above records. Both generators were reproduced independently. So a real cap is flagged when the stack is featureless and goes unflagged once the stack has any genuine spread: the guard's sensitivity is **strictly decreasing in the very quantity that makes an outlier possible**. The two cohorts show the same thing on anatomy — NaKo has the *lower* slice-area variability (CV 0.393, `c²` 1.155, 116 of 120 below CV 0.5) and fires on 90% of halves once trimmed, while Augsburg has the *higher* variability (CV 0.570, `c²` 1.324) and fires on 2%. Together with the fact that firing depends on the count of **empty** slices — a property of the field of view, not of the anatomy, which needs no statistics at all — that is why the guard went rather than being corrected.

## Blast radius of the slab walk

**Blast radius: the isotropic path only, and narrower than it first appears.** The walk sits on the *shared* path (before `if isotropic:`), but the two paths amplify it completely differently — the same "one mechanism, two amplitudes" pattern recorded for the mirror-equivariance work: on the **consensus (non-isotropic)** path `layer_high` only sets the *seed* and the refit re-converges, so over 100 Augsburg halves (trims of 4-15 slices, none a no-op) the head centre moves on **1 of 100 by 0.021 mm** and the radius on 1 by 0.0005 — essentially immune, **but that is the explicit-`spacing=` convention, which is the one production uses** (`femur.py:745` passes `spacing=hip_image.spacing`, so the whole MRI torsion path does). With `spacing=None`, where the slab geometry comes from `x_ratio` alone, it is a *different* half and a far worse number: `PA000110` image-right moves **0.2261 voxel along the slice axis, i.e. 2.26 mm on these 10 mm slices**, with a 0.124 mm radius change. Pooled over the two conventions 2 of 100 halves carry it, one per convention — so always say which convention, because "essentially immune" is true of production and false of the fallback; on the **isotropic** path `layer_high`/`layer_low` delimit the point cloud handed to `sphere_fit` **directly, with nothing downstream to re-converge**. So the exposure is the NaKo isotropic consumers over `process_nako_large.py`'s 30,407 cases — CCD, CEA, alpha angle, acetabular anteversion and depth, projected femoral offset, anteversion, subchondral.

## Removing the guard: how it was verified

**No golden moved**: `git diff tests/golden/` is empty, which is the strongest single check available, since this landmark feeds the CCD, the CEA, the acetabular version and depth, the projected femoral offset, the anteversion and the subchondral family. With the guard gone the empty-slice coupling is **gone, not reduced**: a trim of only the empty z slices reproduces the centre to **1.09e-10** (NaKo) and 2.96e-12 (Augsburg with the production `spacing=`; 6.73e-11 with `x_ratio` alone) where the pre-change code was off by up to 1.96 mm — so the guard was the only source of it **that reaches the head centre**. It is *not* the only array-extent dependence left in the function: `_femoral_head_slab_bound` still reads `mask.shape[2]` in three places (its loop bound, its `shape[2] - 1` fallback for when the head's equator is never observed, and its clip), and on a constructed post-change case — a head sphere cropped through its upper half, so the equivalent radius is still rising when the bone ends — the returned `layer_low` differs by one slice between a tight array and a padded one. That is unobservable rather than absent: the slices the padded bound admits are empty, so the fitted centre and radius are identical to six decimals, and `femur.py:745` is the only production caller passing `return_layers=True` and discards both layers. Do not restate this as "the rest is trim-invariant". That residual is **not** bit-exact and must not be quoted as such: a crop shifts every z coordinate in the point cloud and `sphere_fit`'s `lstsq` SVD is not bit-exact under that shift — the same 1e-11 floor this file already records for the isotropic path's mirror equivariance. The tests therefore assert `atol = 1e-8`, not 1e-9: the Augsburg `x_ratio`-only convention is the noisier one (27 of 100 halves below 1e-12 against 94, centre max 6.73e-11, radius max 1.67e-10), leaving 1e-9 only ~6x of margin, while 1e-8 still sits **six** orders below the smallest pre-change movement (0.0195 voxel / 1e-8 = 1.95e6). (Bit-identity *is* exact for the same array pre- versus post-change; it is the trim property that carries the 1e-10.)

**The earlier note here — that any fix would move the head centre and carry "a far larger do-not-pool burden than the alpha angle's" — is refuted for removal**, though it remains correct for the other candidate fix (next bullet). Its own stated precondition — that "the guard is inert and only causes harm" be checked on a cohort where it *does* fire — was met **in substance but not literally, and the difference is worth stating**: no real cohort fires in its as-given frame, so there was no such cohort to check. What exists instead is a constructed firing configuration where removal is demonstrably **correct** rather than merely different — the phantom in `tests/unit/test_femoral_head_slab_start.py` (`f = 1`, `c² = 1.108`, |z| 2.811), where the old guard starts the slab 3 slices below the first labelled slice and the new code starts it at the first, which is the right answer for a mask whose true centre is known — plus the trimmed real halves (108 of 120 NaKo, 2 of 100 Augsburg), where removal is only shown to be *different*, not better. So the evidence for removal is: bit-identical wherever the guard is silent, correct where it is made to speak, and never validated as more accurate on real data where it speaks, because no real data makes it speak.

## The thin-top-slice question, closed negatively

**Both are closer to the cue, consistently and significantly, and the effect is negligible**: `drop_top` better on 103 of 120 (Wilcoxon p=1.4e-15) by a median **0.0011 mm**, `new_top` on 85 of 120 (p=0.0035) by a median **0.0044 mm**. Against that, `new_top` *moves* the centre by a median **0.2071 mm** (p90 0.4728, max **1.5822**) — it travels **47 times further than it improves**. The p-values are tiny because the improvement is consistent, not because it is large; this is the significant-but-not-meaningful trap.

**The dominant term is one no candidate touches**: all three sit ~1.19 mm from the cue (median 1.189 / 1.188 / 1.105), so there is a ~1.2 mm systematic offset between the sphere-fit centre and the pure-head-section centre — the head is not a perfect sphere, or the two are not the same quantity — beside which the candidates' 0.03-0.21 mm differences are noise. Adopting either would move every isotropic NaKo value (CCD, CEA, alpha angle, acetabular version and depth, projected femoral offset, anteversion, subchondral) and carry the full do-not-pool burden, to buy an improvement two orders of magnitude below the movement. The probe reproduced the shipped isotropic fit **bit-exactly** (max |Δ| 0.000e+00 on all 120), so this compares the real code and not a lookalike. What that item left behind is the ~1.2 mm offset itself — a different and larger question than the one it posed, and **a decision rather than a task, now taken (hold at 2.0 pending CT; the end of this bullet has it)**: it is `C.FEMORAL_HEAD_SLAB_RADII` (shipped 2.0, zero movement), whose comment carries the whole evidence base and the warning against choosing its value by cue score. The original caution still stands for anyone revisiting it — the `CEA_UPPER_MARGIN_FACTOR` and `x_ratio` precedents both record what happens when a parameter is chosen against the data it will then be quoted against.

## The isotropic slab bias, the six arbiter routes and the two-sided box

**The disagreement is systematic, not scatter**: in the image-left frame the sphere centre sits a mean **-0.968 mm** along index axis 0 (medial-lateral; sd 0.485, |mean|/sd 2.00, **the same sign on 118 of 120 halves**) and +0.405 mm along axis 1 (antero-posterior; same sign on 100 of 120), for a median in-plane distance of **1.189 mm**.

**The mechanism is identified and confirmed by dose-response, not merely correlated**: the slab handed to `sphere_fit` (`layer_low - layer_high`) is a median **46 mm** against a median head radius of 23.2 mm, i.e. **1.98 head radii — a full radius past the equator, well into the neck** — the offset distance correlates with slab depth (r = +0.415), and truncating the slab moves the fit **toward** the cue monotonically in depth: cut at the equator (`layer_high + r`) the median distance falls **1.189 -> 0.402 mm** (closer on **110 of 120**), at 1.3 r to 0.428 (108/120), at 1.6 r to 0.481 (109/120). So two thirds of the disagreement is the slab reaching into the neck.

**The comparison that makes this matter is with the path that already fixed it**: `_fit_femoral_head_consensus` exists on the *non-isotropic* path precisely because the old lateral cutoff "could not tell lateral head surface at the equator from neck surface below it", and against essentially this cue it scores a median **0.3 mm** in-plane with a signed medial offset of ~0.0 (see the consensus bullet above). The isotropic path never got that treatment — it still uses the raw slab plus two index-space cutoffs — and sits at 1.189 mm, **4x worse, with the bias in the same medial direction the consensus fit was built to remove**.

**A second quantity moves the same way, and it is weaker evidence than the centre — do not quote it as a confirmation.** The fitted *radius* can be checked against the same sections independently of the centre: for a sphere whose apex is at depth 0, a section at depth `d` has radius `rho = sqrt(2Rd - d^2)`, so `R = (rho^2 + d^2) / (2d)`. Over the same 120 halves that cue gives a median **22.424 mm** (its own within-half sd is a median 0.504 mm, so the cue is good only to about half a millimetre), against the shipped fit's 23.204 mm — a signed error of **+0.580 mm** — and the equator cut's 22.505 mm, a signed error of **-0.195 mm**, three times smaller in magnitude and closer on **71 of 120** halves. That 71/120 is a far cry from the centre's 110/120, exactly as the noisier cue predicts. Two cautions: the equal-area section radius under-reads a non-circular section, and the **direction** of the shipped fit's over-estimate is *not* the naive consequence of including neck points (the neck is narrower than the head, which would pull the sphere smaller), so no mechanism is claimed from the radius — only that a second, independent quantity moves toward the cue under the same cut rather than away from it.

**Blast radius, measured on one downstream consumer**: under the equator cut the NaKo alpha angle moves on **120 of 120** halves by a median **1.51°** (anterior; max 7.20) and **1.50°** (posterior; max 8.52), with no half gaining or losing a value — so "moves every isotropic NaKo value" is a measured statement for at least this one, not an inference.

**And it settles, negatively, the attractive hypothesis that this defect also explains the alpha angle's low level** (31.1 / 34.7° against a published asymptomatic 54.1 ± 5.1, its own item below). It does not: the cohort *means* barely move — anterior **-0.45°**, i.e. *away* from the literature, closing -2.0% of the ~23° gap, and posterior **+0.67°**, +3.5% — and **0 of 120** halves clear the 55° cam cutoff either way. The per-half movement is 1.5° and the mean movement is 0.5°, so the slab bias is largely *scatter* in the alpha angle rather than level, and the two items are independent. The level question therefore needed its own explanation, and it has one: the **radial position** of the section plane, measured 2026-11-06 and closed 2026-11-09 (the alpha-angle bullet above carries it in full — the two angles trade as the plane turns about the neck axis, this construction samples one position where the clinical value is the maximum over the series, and the reduction was built and withdrawn because the maximum does not converge). So the level is a property of the plane definition rather than of the slab defect or of the landmarks, exactly as the hedge here anticipated, and it is not an open item. **The decision is now a one-line edit, which is as far as it can be taken without taking it.** The literal `2.0` is named `C.FEMORAL_HEAD_SLAB_RADII` (shipped at **2.0**, i.e. exactly the current behaviour — no value moves, no golden moved), and that constant's comment carries the whole evidence base: the 118/120 sign consistency, the 1.189 -> 0.402 mm dose-response, the 1.811 mm centre movement, the alpha-angle blast radius, the two cautions, and the warning that choosing between 1.0 / 1.3 / 1.6 by which scores best against the cue would be the `CEA_UPPER_MARGIN_FACTOR` mistake. Verified as a working lever rather than a cosmetic rename: on a NaKo half, 2.0 gives r 24.780 / slab 47 slices and 1.0 gives r 24.418 / slab 33, with the centre moving in the direction the cue predicts.

**So this was a decision and not work** — flipping it moves every isotropic NaKo value over 30,407 cases, which is the user's call and not a maintenance task — **and the decision has since been taken: hold at 2.0 pending CT. See the end of this bullet.** **An INDEPENDENT cue now exists, and it points one way (2026-11-09) — which is the evidence this decision was missing, though it still does not make the flip mine to perform.** The objection recorded above is that the pure-head-section cue cannot choose among 1.0 / 1.3 / 1.6 because the equator cut was already judged against it on these same 60 cases. So a second cue was built that shares **no voxels and no code** with the head fit and was never used to choose any slab depth: **acetabular concentricity**. In a non-dysplastic hip the acetabular lunate surface is concentric with the head, so a sphere fitted to the **acetabular** label's articular patch estimates the same centre from the *pelvis*. The patch is selected by proximity (<= 3 mm) to femur **voxels** — data, never a fitted centre — so no head fit enters the selection, and the patch is **identical for every candidate**, so the selection cannot favour one. It passes its conditioning check *a priori* rather than by tuning: the fitted acetabular radius is a median **25.7 mm** against the head's 23.1, i.e. slightly larger, exactly as a concentric joint with a cartilage space implies (median 578 patch points; 117 of 120 halves fit, 117 conditioned).

**It reproduces this bullet's own setup, which is what makes it comparable:** the shipped slab comes out a median **46 voxels = 1.99 head radii** against the 1.98 recorded above.

**And it confirms the bias from a new direction**: the shipped centre sits a mean **-1.334 mm** from the acetabular centre along index axis 0, **the same sign on 107 of 108** halves, against the head-section cue's -0.968 mm on 118 of 120 — two independent cues, same axis, same sign, comparable magnitude.

**Sweeping the shipped lever against it** (patching `C.FEMORAL_HEAD_SLAB_RADII` and calling the real `get_femoral_head_center`, so this is the number a flip would actually produce), n=117: at **2.0** median 3-D distance to the cue **1.684 mm** / ML mean -1.334; at **1.6**, 1.331 / -1.108; at **1.3**, 0.942 / -0.847; at **1.0**, 0.846 / -0.763.

**Read the per-half counts with the correction, because the naive one badly understates the effect and nearly got written up backwards**: a strictly-closer count misses that shortening the bound changes *nothing* on a half whose column walk already exited the bone above it, so an unchanged half is neither closer nor further. Closer / unchanged / further is **35 / 82 / 0** at 1.6, **76 / 40 / 1** at 1.3 and **74 / 39 / 4** at 1.0 (Wilcoxon over the movers p = 5.8e-11, 3.2e-14, 6.2e-14). So shortening is **monotone in both statistics and essentially never worse** — at 1.6 it is *never* worse on any of the 117 — and there is a safety gradient: 1.3 buys almost all of the improvement while degrading 1 half, where 1.0 buys a further 0.1 mm and degrades 4.

**What this does NOT do is license the flip.** It is still a one-line edit that moves every isotropic NaKo value over 30,407 cases, which is the user's call and not a maintenance task. Four caveats, none of them dismissals: concentricity fails in dysplasia and arthrosis, and a general-population cohort contains both; the cue is itself a sphere fit to a partial patch and so carries its own error, making this an agreement between two imperfect estimates rather than a measurement against truth; the 3 mm proximity threshold is a parameter, albeit one that cannot favour a candidate because the patch is shared; and these are the **same 60 cases** as the NaKo reader-agreement tables, so a slab choice made here has seen them — a weaker form of the `CEA_UPPER_MARGIN_FACTOR` concern than calibrating against the readers themselves, but not none.

**CT was then measured (2026-11-09) and turned out NOT to be the genuinely held-out test it promised to be**; the end of this bullet has it — the exposure is confirmed and larger than MRI's, but the acetabular cue fails its own a priori check on whole-leg CT, so the test has no arbiter.

**DECIDED 2026-11-09: hold `C.FEMORAL_HEAD_SLAB_RADII` at 2.0 and revisit once CT can be measured.** The user was asked directly, with the sweep above and a recommendation of 1.0 (the equator being the only candidate with a geometric derivation rather than the best score), and chose to change nothing until the held-out test exists. So this is no longer an open decision: the defect is characterised, confirmed by two independent cues, and the lever is exposed and verified as a working one, but **the shipped value stays 2.0 and no isotropic NaKo value moves**. What it waited on was CT — which shares the mechanism by construction but whose magnitude was then unmeasured — and that has since been measured (2026-11-09; the cap binds on 89 of 92 halves, the centre moves a median 2.262 mm, and the radius stays implausible on 28 of 92 even at 1.0).

**It confirmed the exposure without adjudicating the value**, so what the decision waits on now is a lunate-surface cue or a cadaveric ground truth — a landmark that is neither the head fit nor the whole hip bone, and which does not exist — and not a mount. That is a definition problem and new work, not a blocked measurement, which is why this stays a documented property with the shipped value held at 2.0 rather than an open task.

**Re-measure on CT first, then revisit; do not flip it on the NaKo evidence alone**, which is the decision taken here and the reason the two cues above are recorded in full rather than acted on.

**The CT measurement now exists (2026-11-09, the mount having come back) and it does NOT adjudicate the value — it confirms the exposure and removes the arbiter.** Over all 46 NMDID verification cases = **92 image halves**, legs prepared exactly as production rows are (`process_nmdid.load_case` / `split_legs`): **cue-free findings, which are the trustworthy ones.** The diameter cap **binds on 89 of 92 halves** against 78 of 117 on NaKo, so the mechanism is *more* pervasive on CT — expected, since a whole-body scan gives the column far more femur to walk down. Shortening to 1.0 moves the centre by a median **2.262 mm** (p90 4.306, max **7.495**) against NaKo's 1.811, so the **blast radius is larger on CT, not smaller**. But the radius-plausibility signal is **weak and does not favour shortening much**: the fitted head radius exceeds the 18-26 mm adult ceiling on **32 of 92** halves (35%) at the shipped 2.0 and still on **28** at 1.0, and exceeds 30 mm on 5 against 4 — so 1.0 does not rescue the implausible radii, which is evidence *against* reading the equator cut as a fix rather than for it.

**And the independent acetabular cue CANNOT be transferred to CT as constructed, so this test has no arbiter.** It fails the same a priori check that licensed it on NaKo: there the cue radius exceeded the head's essentially always (25.7 vs 23.1 mm, as a concentric joint with a cartilage space implies), whereas on CT it is **larger on only 45 of 92** — a coin flip — the median difference is **-0.204 mm** (the wrong sign), the patch holds **2762** points against NaKo's 578, and the cue-to-head distance is **8.91 mm**, **5.3x** NaKo's 1.68. The cause is already documented in this file: whole-leg CT label maps carry **one label for the entire hip bone** (which is why the CEA needs `acetabulum_radius_factor` and `get_acetabular_surface`), so a proximity band round the femur picks up ilium, pubis and ischium rather than the lunate surface.

**So do not quote that run's 8.91 -> 5.92 mm "improvement", closer on 88 of 92: it is the distance to a reference that is itself wrong.** Isolating the lunate surface would mean `get_acetabular_surface`, which rays out of **the head centre** and is therefore circular for this purpose — so a genuine CT arbiter needs a landmark that is neither the head fit nor the whole hip bone, and does not currently exist.

**The inflated CT head radius is now DIAGNOSED, the recorded hypothesis about it is REFUTED, and that is what the licence failure below was actually waiting on (2026-11-10).** This file records the radius exceeding the 18-26 mm adult ceiling on 32 of 92 CT halves, says shortening the slab does not rescue it, and offers one explanation — for the two NaKo runaway halves — that "what is actually ill-conditioned is the fit on that point cloud, most likely a **shallow cap** left by the two in-plane cutoffs", flagged there as a hypothesis and not a measurement. It has now been measured, by a probe that replicates `get_femoral_head_center_ct`'s body and **certifies itself by reproducing the shipped radius bit-exactly on 92 of 92 halves** before any diagnostic is read off it; it independently reproduces the **32 of 92** implausible count recorded above, which is the second check that it measures the shipped path.

**The shallow-cap hypothesis is wrong, and wrong in the opposite direction.** The angular half-width the final cloud subtends about its own fitted centre is a median **174.1 deg** (range 124.2-180.0) and **0 of 92 halves are below 90 deg**, so no cloud is a cap at all; and the implausible halves are *wider*, not narrower — median **179.0 against 166.7 deg**, Mann-Whitney **p = 7.2e-08**. A cloud wrapping a full sphere's worth of directions is what a cloud containing head **plus neck and shaft** looks like, which is the opposite diagnosis.

**The mechanism is an asymmetry between the two in-plane cutoffs, visible in the code and confirmed in the numbers.** Both are boxes about the top-slice centroid `com_high` with a half-width meant to be "one medial radius", but the **lateral** one takes its half-width from `superior_half` — the top half of the slab, head-dominated — while the **coronal** one takes `min_c` from `contour_points` *after* the x cut, i.e. from the slab cloud that already holds neck and shaft. So the coronal half-width is estimated from the very contamination it exists to trim, and the feedback is positive: a contaminated cloud widens the box, which keeps more contamination. Measured: `half_y` is a median **54.54 mm** on the implausible halves against **38.98** on the plausible ones (**p = 9.9e-12**, Spearman against the radius **+0.773**), it exceeds 40 mm — an 80 mm A-P box, wider than any femoral head — on **59 of 92 halves (64.1%)** where `half_x` exceeds 40 mm on **1 of 92**, and the `half_y / half_x` ratio is a median **1.87** (max 2.72). The slab depth is a genuine but *weaker* term (Spearman +0.621, p = 8.8e-04 against half_y's 9.9e-12), **which is precisely why shortening `FEMORAL_HEAD_SLAB_RADII` to 1.0 leaves 28 of 92 radii implausible**: the dominant term is the coronal box, not the slab. The slab cap fired on 2 of 92 and the escape hatch on 3, so neither is the mechanism — the same verdict the NaKo halves gave.

**One conditioning signal exists and is deliberately NOT turned into a gate.** The fit residual needs no external reference (it is a property of the fit) and correlates well — Spearman **+0.768**, median **6.67 mm** on implausible halves against **3.56** on plausible, p = 4.4e-12 — but the two **ranges overlap** (1.50-6.52 against 3.82-8.75), so there is no gap and every threshold trades one error for the other: >6 mm flags 26 halves, 22 implausible and 4 plausible; >5 mm flags 45, catching 30 of the 32 but also 15 sound ones. That is the tibial-continuum situation this file already describes, so a residual gate would rest on a **fitted** threshold and is not added here.

**What this changes, and what it does not.** It does not move any value: nothing was altered, and correcting the coronal cutoff would move every CT hip measurement (CCD, centre-edge angle, acetabular version, femoral offset, subchondral, and through the mechanical axis the HKA and MAD) with the full do-not-pool burden, which makes it the same class of decision as the slab lever and equally the user's. What it does is replace a guess with a mechanism and explain the obstacle below: **the concentricity licence is unavailable on CT because the head radius is inflated by a cutoff estimated on contaminated data**, so the fix for the arbiter problem is not a better acetabular cue but a coronal cutoff derived — as the lateral one already is — from head-dominated points.

**A third route was then built and measured rather than argued about. It fails too — but it relocates the obstacle, which is the useful part (2026-11-10).** The objection just above is specific to one construction, so it does not rule out a cue that never touches the head fit: a **consensus sphere fit on the pelvis patch alone**, seeded from the patch and iterated to a stable inlier set within `C.FEMORAL_HEAD_CONSENSUS_TOLERANCE_MM` (2.5 mm) — the same RANSAC-style construction `_fit_femoral_head_consensus` uses for the head — on the expectation that non-articular ilium, pubis and ischium sit far from the cup's sphere and fall out of the inlier set. Over all 92 verification halves: **the consensus does essentially nothing, dropping a median 0.1% of the patch** in 2-3 iterations, so it reproduces the naive cue almost exactly — median cue-to-head **8.916 mm** at the shipped 2.0 against the **8.91 mm** recorded above, agreeing to three digits (patch 1628 points, against the naive 2762 and NaKo's 578). The proximity band is therefore already sphere-consistent at that tolerance and a consensus step has nothing to exclude; **tightening the tolerance until it did exclude something would be choosing a parameter by which slab candidate it then favours**, which is the `CEA_UPPER_MARGIN_FACTOR` mistake, so it was not done.

**What the run does establish is that the a priori LICENCE is unavailable on CT for a second and more fundamental reason, independent of how the patch is chosen.** The check that licensed the NaKo cue is "the acetabular radius must exceed the head's", and on CT it fails on **54 of 92** halves — but the failure is **the head radius, not the cue**: `head_r` is a median **26.70 mm** on the failing halves against **23.48** on the passing ones (Mann-Whitney **p = 1.0e-08**), while `cue_r` is statistically **indistinguishable** between the two groups (24.37 against 24.10, **p = 0.44**), and **31 of the 54** failures carry a head radius above the 18-26 mm adult ceiling. The cue's own radius distribution is meanwhile entirely sensible — median **24.37 mm**, sd 2.19, **85.9%** inside a plausible 22-32 mm — which is what makes that attribution clean rather than a guess. So on CT the concentricity check compares the cue against a reference that **is the quantity under test**: wherever the slab bias inflates the head radius the check fails whatever the cue does. That is a *different* circularity from the `get_acetabular_surface` one, and it cannot be engineered around, because any concentricity-based licence needs a head radius one can already trust.

**The sweep still prefers shortening, and still must not be quoted.** On the 79 halves whose cue radius is plausible the median cue distance runs 8.916 (at 2.0) -> 8.910 (1.6) -> 8.108 (1.3) -> **6.229 mm** (1.0), closer on **76 of 79** at 1.0 against 1 further and 2 unchanged (Wilcoxon over the movers p = 1.9e-13) — monotone, and in the same direction as NaKo's sweep. It is still the distance to a reference sitting ~9 mm from the head centre, which no concentric joint does, so it is the same uninterpretable "improvement" the paragraph above refuses, now reproduced on a second construction.

**Net on the arbiter question: three constructible routes are closed by measurement rather than one by argument** — the ray-cast surface (circular on the head centre), the raw proximity patch (non-articular bone) and its consensus refinement (a no-op) — and the licence all three would need is itself unavailable while the head radius is the thing in question. So what is missing is **not a cleverer patch but a reference external to this image family**: cadaveric ground truth, or a cohort whose acetabulum is labelled separately the way NaKo's is. That is the precise form of "a lunate-surface cue or a cadaveric ground truth", and it is why this is a definition problem and not a measurement waiting to be run.

**Net: the CT exposure is real, binding on 97% of halves and larger in millimetres than MRI's; the value remains undecidable on the evidence, and the shipped 2.0 stands.** What would decide it is a lunate-surface cue or a cadaveric ground truth, not more of this.

**A FOURTH route was then built and measured, and it is the most informative failure of the four, because it refutes the mechanism THIS FILE recorded a day earlier (2026-11-10).** The diagnosis above attributes the inflated CT head radius to the **coronal** in-plane cutoff being estimated from a cloud that already holds neck and shaft, where the **lateral** one takes its half-width from ``superior_half`` (head-dominated) — and notes that if correcting it restored plausible radii, the concentricity licence would become available and the acetabular cue could finally adjudicate the slab value. So the symmetric variant was implemented — coronal half-width from ``superior_half``, exactly as the lateral one already does — over all 92 verification halves, with the probe **certifying itself** by reproducing the shipped ``get_femoral_head_center_ct`` radius bit-exactly on **92 of 92** halves at both slab candidates before any diagnostic was read off it.

**It is a clean negative.** The coronal half-width really does shrink — on **82 of 92** halves, by a median **4.48 mm** and up to **35.72** (median 44.82 -> 26.84 mm; the count above 40 mm falls from 59 to 38) — and the fitted radius moves on **2 of 92**, by at most **0.053 mm** (at 1.0: 7 of 92, max 0.138). The implausible count is **unchanged at 32 of 92**, the fit residual is identical to three decimals (median 4.971 either way at 2.0, 3.526 at 1.0), and the licence is unmoved: ``cue_r > head_r`` holds on **38 of 92** at 2.0 and 48 of 92 at 1.0, the same halves before and after.

**So ``half_y`` is a CORRELATE and not a cause, and the mechanism paragraph above is corrected rather than merely qualified** — its 9.9e-12 separation and its Spearman +0.773 against the radius are real and still worth knowing, and they do not survive the intervention test, which is the only test that distinguishes the two.

**The reason is in the code, not in the data, and it is worth stating because it generalises. Both in-plane cutoffs are ONE-SIDED**: each trims the FAR side using a half-width measured from the NEAR extremum (`max_s = com + (com - min(superior_x))`, then `half_y = com - min(after_x_y)` with `final = after_x[y <= com + half_y]`). A cloud reaching far **anteriorly** therefore produces a large ``half_y`` and is granted an equally large **posterior** allowance that it never uses — the box is vacuous, which is exactly why shrinking it moves nothing on 90 of 92 halves — while the anterior excursion that produced the half-width is **never trimmed at all**. Reading a large ``half_y`` as 'the box keeps contamination' inverts it: the box keeps contamination the cloud does not have, and ignores the contamination it does.

**Net: the arbiter question now has FOUR routes closed by measurement** — the ray-cast surface (circular on the head centre), the raw proximity patch (non-articular bone), its consensus refinement (a no-op) and the symmetric coronal cutoff (a vacuous bound) — and the shipped 2.0 still stands. What the negative leaves behind is one construction that has not been tried and that the code argument actually implicates: a **two-sided** box about ``com_high``, both half-widths from ``superior_half``, so the far anterior and lateral excursions are trimmed rather than licensed. That is a measurement and not a decision, and it is the one worth running next; correcting any cutoff would move every CT hip measurement and remains the user's call either way.

**It was run, and it is the first construction that moves the CT head radius at all — a partial positive that still does NOT restore the licence (2026-11-10).** Same 92 verification halves, same self-certification discipline: the probe reproduces the shipped ``get_femoral_head_center_ct`` radius bit-exactly on **92 of 92** halves at both candidates, and it independently reproduces the consensus run's licence figures to the digit — 54 of 92 failing, head radius 26.70 against 23.48 mm on the failures (p = 1.0e-08), cue radius indistinguishable (p = 0.44) — which is the cross-check that it measures the same quantity and not a lookalike.

**The radius comes down, monotonically, and never makes a sound half worse.** At the shipped slab 2.0 it moves on **54 of 92** halves, **down on 53 of those 54**, by a median 0.533 mm and up to 6.034; the count outside the 18-26 mm adult range falls **32 -> 26** (6 fixed, **0 newly** **broken**) and the count above 30 mm falls 5 -> 3. At 1.0 it moves on **75 of 92** (down on 73), the implausible count falls **28 -> 17** (11 fixed, 0 newly broken) and above 30 mm 4 -> 1. Shipped-2.0 against box-1.0 is **32 -> 17 implausible**, a median radius of 25.353 -> 24.070 mm and a per-half median move of -1.033 mm (max -5.710).

**Conditioning does not degrade, which is the risk this construction runs and the reason the cap check was built into it**: the angular half-width the cloud subtends about its own centre goes from a median 174.1 to 171.6 deg at 2.0 and 143.2 to 139.9 at 1.0, with a **minimum of 124.2 and 112.7** — no half becomes the shallow cap the refuted hypothesis imagined — and the kept fraction barely moves at the median (0.728 -> 0.726 and 0.724 -> 0.706, min 0.343), so the gain is not bought by discarding most of the cloud.

**The fit residual is the weakest number here and must not be quoted as the strongest**: it improves from a median 4.971 -> 4.223 mm at 2.0 and 3.526 -> **2.087** at 1.0, but a smaller point set fits a sphere better whatever it contains, so a residual computed on a different selection is not evidence on its own. The cue-free plausibility count is what carries the result.

**And the licence is NOT restored, which is the finding that matters for the slab decision.** ``cue_r > head_r`` rises from 38 of 92 to 43 at 2.0 and from 48 to **64** at 1.0 — so at best it **still fails on 28 of 92**, against a NaKo licence that holds essentially always. The attribution on those 28 is unchanged: the head radius is still the discriminator (25.62 against 23.26 mm, p = 4.8e-07) while the cue is statistically indistinguishable (24.36 against 24.38, p = 0.89), and **13 of the 28 still exceed the 26 mm ceiling**. So the obstacle is **halved, not** **removed**, 17 of 92 radii remain implausible — something else is also wrong, most plausibly the z-axis contamination the slab lever itself addresses — and the acetabular cue still cannot adjudicate the slab value.

**Do not read the cue distance as validation either**: it falls 8.814 -> 7.110 mm at 2.0 and 5.720 -> **2.935** at 1.0, which looks like the strongest signal in the run and is the least usable one, being the distance to a reference this file already establishes as wrong on whole-leg CT; choosing a variant by it would be the `CEA_UPPER_MARGIN_FACTOR` mistake a fourth time. It is recorded because its direction agrees with every other signal, not because it scores.

**What the construction has going for it is that it adds no fitted parameter** — both half-widths are the shipped *lateral* rule (one head-dominated half-width about ``com_high``) applied on both in-plane axes in both directions — so it is an a priori completion of the existing construction in the same sense the equator cut is, and over 184 half-candidate pairs it never moved a plausible radius out of range. What it lacks is any reference saying the smaller radius is the **right** one.

**Decision unchanged, and not mine to take**: correcting these cutoffs would move every CT hip measurement — CCD, centre-edge angle, acetabular version, femoral offset, subchondral, and through the mechanical axis the HKA and MAD — with the full do-not-pool burden, exactly like the slab lever beside it. It is recorded here as a measured candidate and implemented nowhere; the shipped one-sided cutoffs and the shipped 2.0 both stand.

**A SIXTH criterion then turned up that is NOT circular, needs no new run, and is the first one to bear on the slab value itself (2026-11-10): bilateral symmetry.** Every route above failed for one of two reasons — the reference was the quantity under test, or it was the wrong bone. The two femoral heads of **one cadaver** are neither: head radius is a bilaterally conserved quantity, so ``|r_left - r_right|`` within a case is a reference-free consistency check, and it was already sitting in the probe's own output.

**What makes it usable here is task #11**: ``get_femoral_head_center_ct`` measures a right half as the *reflection* of a left one, so both sides run the identical code and an L/R gap **cannot** be a side-dependent code artefact — it is anatomy, segmentation, or the cloud. Over the 46 cases (all 46 with both halves): shipped at 2.0, mean **1.584 mm**, median 1.238, p90 3.537, max 5.721, within 2 mm on **32 of 46**, Pearson **+0.779**, relative asymmetry 5.16%; shipped at 1.0, mean **1.807**, median 1.191, p90 3.754, max **7.107**, within 2 mm on **30**, Pearson **+0.725**, 5.01%; two-sided box at 2.0, mean 1.342, median 0.962, p90 2.871, max 4.552, 33 of 46, +0.805, 4.25%; two-sided box at 1.0, mean **1.278**, median **0.821**, p90 **2.588**, max 4.592, **35 of 46**, +0.791, **3.78%**.

**It supports the in-plane box and does NOT support shortening the slab, which is the opposite of what the acetabular sweep said** — and the acetabular sweep is the one resting on a reference this file establishes as wrong on CT. The box improves symmetry on **31 of 46** halves against 5 worse at 2.0 (Wilcoxon **p = 5.5e-04**) and 28 against 17 at 1.0 (p = 1.5e-03). Shortening the slab *alone*, by contrast, leaves the median flat (1.238 -> 1.191) while the **mean (1.584 ->** **1.807), the p90 (3.537 -> 3.754), the max (5.721 -> 7.107), the within-2 mm count (32 -> 30) and the correlation (+0.779 -> +0.725) all degrade** — i.e. it makes a handful of legs markedly more asymmetric without improving the body of the cohort.

**The obvious objection was checked and does not hold: this is not variance compression.** Shrinking every radius toward a common bound would reduce ``|L-R|`` for free, and the pooled sd does fall (2.964 -> 2.558) — but Pearson is invariant under a global affine transform, so a *rise* from +0.779 to +0.791 (and +0.725 -> +0.791) cannot be produced by compression, and the asymmetry normalised by each pair's own mean improves on the same ordering (5.16% -> 3.78%).

**Three limits, stated because this is the strongest cue available and therefore the easiest to over-read.** It is **consistency, not** **accuracy** — a construction that is symmetrically wrong passes it perfectly, exactly as this file says of every left/right criterion it quotes; the two halves of one cadaver share acquisition and segmentation quality, so part of the error is common and cancels, which makes the test blind to any *bilateral* bias (and the ~1.2 mm medial drag is precisely such a bias); and n = 46 pairs. So it discriminates between constructions without licensing either as correct, and it is quoted here as the one reader-free signal that distinguishes the two levers rather than as a verdict on the radius.

**The CT blast radius is now MEASURED, which closes the last gap in this decision (2026-11-10).** This file has said throughout that flipping the slab 'moves every CT hip measurement', naming the consumers from the call graph — an inference, never a measurement, where the MRI side has had its alpha-angle figure (120 of 120, median 1.51 deg) since 2026-11-06.
